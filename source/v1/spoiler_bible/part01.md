THIS FILE SPOILS EVERY CHARACTER

Canonical murder facts
• Sterling Voss dies from a fast-acting botanical cardiac toxin in his private cordial.

• The poison is introduced between 6:40 and 6:49 in the Donor Salon.

• The active murderer is not predetermined; one of the 15 core players becomes the murderer through the
animal draw.

• Every core branch supplies a unique motive, access story, physical/timeline clue, and final confession.

• All fixed clues are compatible with all 15 murderer identities. Branch-dependent evidence is supplied from
the player's own packet, so the facilitator never has to select the correct clue.

• Optional roles have full murderer branches for material symmetry, but standard play does not place them in
the murderer draw.

Ominous but non-supernatural layer The 'curse' atmosphere is deliberately human-made or mundane. Imogen wrote the anonymous warning note as provocation. Riley's unauthorized lighting change causes the eerie flicker. Provenance gaps, the black-star archival seal, and the exhibition's morally charged objects create unease. Nothing supernatural causes Sterling's death.

Character branch matrix
1. Adrian Vellum - Executive Director of the Meridian Museum
Victim relationship: Sterling had privately informed you that tonight's gift would come with a new governance agreement: he wanted veto power over acquisitions, his name on the museum, and your resignation within sixty days. You smiled through the meeting and told no one.

Hidden complication: You have already accepted a confidential offer to lead a rival museum next year, which makes any argument with Sterling look like succession planning.

Innocent route: You entered the Donor Salon at 6:32 to replace Sterling's speech cards. At 6:40 you slipped into the museum office and took a confidential wired-landline call from the rival museum that has offered you a job; the call record runs continuously until 6:50. Revealing that record clears your timing but exposes your planned departure.

Murderer route: At 6:47 you returned to the Donor Salon under the pretense of correcting Sterling's speech cards. Furious at the takeover terms, you tipped the stolen toxin into his private cordial and wiped the stem with your pocket square. You left calmly and resumed hosting. Innocent evidence: SPEECH CARDS - Adrian's handwritten speech cards were found beside Sterling's service tray. Adrian admits entering the Donor Salon earlier but insists he left before the critical window.

Murderer evidence: WITNESS TIMELINE - A catering assistant saw Adrian leaving the Donor Salon at 6:48. Adrian had claimed his only visit ended before 6:35.

2. Celeste Frame - Chief Curator of World Cultures
Victim relationship: You discovered that Sterling knew the Star Bowl's provenance file contained a major gap and ordered the exhibition to proceed anyway. He threatened to remove you from the show if you raised the issue publicly.

Hidden complication: Against board instructions, you commissioned an independent provenance review and hid the preliminary report in the Registrar's Office.

Innocent route: You were in the Donor Salon at 6:25 correcting an object label. From 6:40 to 6:49, the Registrar's scanner log shows your curator code repeatedly scanning the independent provenance report you were not authorized to commission. Revealing the log clears your timing but exposes the report.

Murderer route: At 6:44 you used a label correction as an excuse to enter the Donor Salon. Believing Sterling was about to lock dubious objects permanently into the museum, you poisoned his cordial and returned to the gallery before anyone noticed. Innocent evidence: CURATOR'S PENCIL - Celeste's distinctive pencil was found beneath the edge of Sterling's service table. She says it was left during an earlier label correction.

Murderer evidence: CURATOR'S PENCIL - Celeste's pencil was found beneath Sterling's drink tray beside a handwritten time note: 'S.V. alone - 6:40.' She denied returning to the salon after 6:25.

3. Grant Easel - International Art Dealer & Collector Liaison
Victim relationship: Sterling had learned that one certificate in a major private sale you brokered was forged before it ever reached you. He planned to imply publicly that you had created it unless you refunded him millions.

Hidden complication: You were duped on that sale, but you quietly altered your own file afterward so clients would not see how badly your due diligence failed.

Innocent route: You entered the Donor Salon at 6:34 to leave an appraisal folder and briefly handled a decorative decanter that looked like Sterling's service piece. From 6:40 to 6:49, the Media Room document terminal shows you revising and reprinting the disputed sale file you secretly altered. The audit trail clears your timing but exposes your cover-up.

Murderer route: At 6:43 you returned with a revised appraisal folder. You used the lookalike decanter as cover while slipping the toxin directly into Sterling's silver coupe, then wiped the stem before leaving. Innocent evidence: DEALER'S CARD - Grant's business card was found beneath Sterling's appraisal folder in the Donor Salon. Grant admits delivering the folder shortly before the poisoning window.

Murderer evidence: SILVER COUPE - A partial print matching Grant survives beneath the wiped base of Sterling's silver coupe. Grant claimed he never touched Sterling's drink service.

4. Margot Ledger - Chief Financial Officer of the Meridian Museum
Victim relationship: Sterling demanded that you disguise several gala and naming-wing expenses inside restricted museum funds until his promised donation cleared. When you resisted, he threatened to blame the bookkeeping on you.

Hidden complication: You temporarily moved money between internal accounts to cover a cash-flow shortfall, intending to reverse it after the gala. It was technically unauthorized and Sterling discovered it.

Innocent route: You met Sterling at 6:33 for a signature. From 6:40 to 6:50, the finance terminal audit shows you reversing and re-entering the unauthorized internal transfer you were trying to hide. That audit trail clears your timing but exposes the accounting violation.

Murderer route: At 6:46 you returned to obtain the signature Sterling had withheld. When he mocked the accounting mess he had helped create, you poisoned his cordial. You then copied a budget sheet to create the impression your office visit happened earlier. Innocent evidence: UNSIGNED BUDGET - Margot's corrected budget was found on Sterling's desk with a blank signature line. She admits she was seeking his signature shortly before he died.

Murderer evidence: COPIER LOG - Metadata shows Margot's '6:34' budget copy was actually printed at 6:49 after a manual clock reset. Her claimed timeline does not hold.

5. Rafael Gilt - Chief Conservator
Victim relationship: Sterling pressured you to soften a conservation report showing that one supposedly untouched object had been recently altered. He threatened to replace you with a private conservator if you refused.

Hidden complication: You changed the date on a minor treatment record last month to spare the museum an embarrassing insurance dispute. No object was harmed, but the falsification could end your career.

During the entire poison window, the Conservation Lab data logger records a sequence of manual humidity-calibration entries under your personal code at the case across the house. The log clears your timing but also exposes the treatment record whose date you altered.

Murderer route: You took the toxin sample from the conservation cabinet earlier. At 6:42, while carrying a condition-report folder to Sterling, you added it to his cordial and disposed of the stopper in your glove pouch.

Innocent evidence: LAB GLOVE - Rafael's glove carries several conservation chemicals, and his access key fits the cabinet from which the toxin sample disappeared. He says both facts are ordinary parts of his job.

Murderer evidence: LAB GLOVE - The stopper from the missing toxin vial was found inside Rafael's glove pouch. Rafael claimed he had not handled that vial in months.

6. Imogen Muse - Performance Artist & Cultural Celebrity
Victim relationship: Sterling appropriated the concept for one of your unreleased performances and planned to unveil a sanitized version of it as the gala's closing spectacle, crediting himself as creative patron.

Hidden complication: You wrote and planted the ominous anonymous warning note about 'returning what was taken.' It was meant as an art-world provocation, not a literal threat.

Murderer route: At 6:45 you carried a silver prop into the Donor Salon as cover. You used it to stir the toxin into Sterling's cordial, wiped it, and returned to Avery only moments later. Innocent evidence: SILVER PROP - A silver pick from Imogen's installation was found near the Donor Salon entrance. Imogen admits bringing the prop through that area earlier in the evening.
