THE LAST ACQUISITION
A host-blind murder mystery at The Meridian Museum of Art & World Cultures

OPEN THIS FILE FREELY - SPOILER SAFE

Premise Tulsa's The Meridian Museum of Art & World Cultures is unveiling Treasures of the World, a lavish exhibition drawn largely from the private collection of controversial billionaire donor Sterling Voss. The centerpiece is the fictional thirteenth-century Isfahan Star Bowl, a silver-inlaid brass basin whose incomplete provenance has already attracted quiet controversy. Sterling remains offstage during Act I. Just as guests finish exploring the exhibition and begin dinner, he is found dead in the Donor Salon. He has been poisoned. One of the guests did it - but that guest only learns their role when the murderer animal is announced.

Design targets
• 15 core roles, 10 secondary roles, 5 tertiary roles; tier labels are never shown to players.

• 2.5 hours of active game inside a roughly 6:00-9:30 PM evening with buffet dinner.

• Act I is movement-heavy; Acts II-III prioritize roleplay and social deduction.

• No phones or QR codes. All mechanics are physical.

• Almost no mandatory read-aloud until the final confessions.

• Medium deduction difficulty: the killer should be discoverable but not obvious.

• The facilitator may play a full character and does not know who the murderer is.

Murderer selection - improved animal method Prepare two identical sets of 15 easy animal names. Core players draw from Player Bowl A as they arrive and memorize their animal. After all core attendees have drawn, look at the unused slips left in Bowl A and remove those same animals from Murderer Bowl B. Shuffle Bowl B and draw one card. You now know the murderer animal but still do not know who drew it. After Sterling's death is announced, say: “If your animal is ___, follow the MURDERER column in your packet. Everyone else follows INNOCENT.” Optional-role guests can draw from a separate set of non-murderer animals if you want everyone to participate in the ritual.

Player truth rules
• Innocent players may evade, omit, deflect, and protect private information, but may not invent false factual events.

• The murderer may lie as needed.

• If a card explicitly instructs a player to reveal or submit evidence, they must do so.

• Players may share discoveries from Act I freely. Encourage conversation rather than hoarding.

Act I scavenger hunt Sixteen numbered clue cards are in the separate PRINT-DO-NOT-READ clue pack. When a guest finds one, they earn one scavenger point and bring it to the Evidence Table in Founders Hall. The clue becomes public immediately. There is no checklist and no required order. The hunt ends when time is called; no essential murderer-identifying clue depends on every card being found.

Murder announcement script “Before Sterling Voss could enter Founders Hall for his remarks, he was found collapsed in the Donor Salon. The museum's physician has confirmed that this was not a natural death. Sterling Voss was poisoned. From this point on, the gala is an investigation. If your animal is [MURDERER ANIMAL], follow the MURDERER instructions in your packet. Everyone else follows INNOCENT. Do not announce which column you are reading.”

Fixed evidence released at start of Act III The separate clue/forensics print pack contains five cards labeled F1-F5. Keep them face down until Act III, then place all five on the Evidence Table. You do not need to know their contents in advance.

Final ballot and reveal
• Each ballot names one murderer, one piece of evidence, and a motive; plus Best Actor and Best Costume.

• Tally murderer votes first. Announce the three most-accused characters in ascending order.

• Those three read their FINAL STATEMENT from the appropriate Innocent/Murderer branch.

• If the actual murderer is not among the top three, award the murderer an 'Escaped Justice' prize, then ask the murderer animal to identify themselves and read the confession.

• Award scavenger recognition separately so solving the murder is not the only way to win.

Preparation checklist This week
• Assign the 15 core roles first; add secondary and tertiary roles as RSVPs grow.

• Send each guest only their spoiler-safe pre-party character sheet plus the invitation.

• Tell guests costumes can be assembled from normal formalwear plus one character-specific element.

Day before
• Print this guide, props, private player packets, and randomized A/B evidence cards.

• Without reading the secret material, place each character's private page plus both A/B evidence cards in a labeled envelope.

• Cut two identical sets of core animal cards for Player Bowl A and Murderer Bowl B.

• Cut ballots and scavenger clues; prepare an Evidence Table and scavenger score sheet.

• Stage inexpensive exhibit objects and placards in the room map.

1-2 hours before guests arrive
• Hide the sixteen Act I clues. Do not make them extremely difficult; movement and discovery matter more than searching skill.

• Place the fixed forensic cards face down for Act III.

• Put each attending player's private packet at their assigned dining seat or hand it to them at check-in.

• Start with all 15 core animals in both bowls. After the attending core players have drawn from Bowl A, note the unused animal slips still left there, remove those same names from Murderer Bowl B, then draw the murderer animal from Bowl B.

• Set out awards, pens, ballots, and a container for submitted Evidence Update cards.
