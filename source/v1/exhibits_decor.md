TREASURES OF THE WORLD
Printable exhibit placards and low-cost staging guide

THE ISFAHAN STAR BOWL
Central Iran (Isfahan) • c. 1250

Hammered brass with silver inlay

A broad ceremonial basin whose interior maps constellations around a dark eight-pointed star. The object first appears in a European collection in 1912. Its documented chain of ownership becomes incomplete between 1940 and 1978 before reappearing in a Swiss private collection. Sterling Voss acquired it in 1992. The Meridian has announced it as the centerpiece of Treasures of the World.

MOON JAR OF HAEJU
Korea • late 18th century

White-glazed porcelain

An unusually full-bodied white jar assembled from two thrown hemispheres. Slight asymmetry gives the vessel its quiet character. Loaned from the Voss Collection for the first time.

CEDAR & CLOUD SCREEN
Japan • Edo period, c. 1740

Ink, mineral pigment, and gold leaf on paper

A six-panel folding screen depicting cedar branches disappearing into bands of cloud. A collector's seal on the reverse links the screen to a Kyoto merchant family in the nineteenth century.

NIGHT GARDEN ALBUM LEAF
Northern India • Mughal period, c. 1660

Opaque watercolor and gold on paper

A moonlit garden scene populated by birds, flowering vines, and a single reading figure. The page was separated from a larger album before entering the European market.

THE STORYTELLER GOLDWEIGHTS
Ghana • 19th century

Cast brass

A set of small figurative weights made for measuring gold dust. Their forms encode proverbs about speech, patience, obligation, and reputation. The set is presented here as a study in art, commerce, and memory.

CUSCO SILVER WRITING BOX
Peru • c. 1780

Silver, cedar, and velvet

A portable writing box made in Cusco with repoussé floral ornament. Its compartments hold ink, paper, seals, and correspondence - the infrastructure of long-distance colonial administration.

LAUREL KRATER
Southern Italy • 4th century BCE

Red-figure ceramic

A large mixing vessel decorated with laurel branches and two musicians. The museum label carefully notes that the vessel's modern ownership history begins only in 1968.

INDIGO COURT TEXTILE
Southwestern Nigeria • early 20th century

Indigo-dyed cotton

A large resist-dyed cloth whose repeating geometry shifts across the field rather than remaining perfectly regular. Displayed flat to emphasize both pattern and handwork.

STUDY FOR RED TRANSIT
Mariana Costa (Brazilian, b. 1978) • 2004

Oil, wax, and graphite on linen

A contemporary painting of layered transit maps, erased routes, and blocks of red pigment. The work entered the Voss Collection shortly after the artist's first major international exhibition.

LOW-COST STAGING GUIDE
• Star Bowl: thrift-store metallic bowl, decorative charger, or inexpensive brass-colored bowl on a black cloth or box pedestal.

• Moon Jar: any plain white vase or ceramic jar.

• Screen: folded foam board or trifold display board with a printed or hand-drawn branch motif.

• Album Leaf / Red Transit: print any public-domain-style decorative pattern or make abstract art on cardstock; the placard does most of the world-building.

• Goldweights: arrange inexpensive brass-colored charms, keys, or small metal objects in a shallow case.

• Writing Box: jewelry box, cigar-style craft box, or small wooden container.

• Krater: dark vase or urn with a printed decorative band taped around it.

• Indigo Textile: patterned blue fabric, scarf, table runner, or thrifted textile.

• Do not spend money trying to make every object literal. One convincing centerpiece plus museum lighting, labels, and room signs will sell the fiction.
