## Physical causal argument

The original, initially intact and locked away, disappears in the 6:12 forced opening. Its recovered neck has a fresh gap, and a cyanide-bearing blue fragment in folded favor paper joins that gap. Cyanide also appears in a miniature fragrance bottle recovered from reception waste and inside Grant's museum-star glass. Shared punch, food, sauce, PROP glass, brush-water and FIXER are clear; unopened favor stock is normal fragrance.

The natural reconstruction is that someone broke the original, transferred cyanide into a portable miniature, and introduced a small quantity or residue into Grant's empty glass during its 6:32-6:44 sideboard interval. Casey adds clean punch at 6:46 and retains it until the 6:49 toast. Empty-looking photographs do not rule out residue, and the record explicitly avoids claiming continuous coverage.

Justin's admission fits the two ends of that chain: the actual source ends on his chosen shelf, and he touches the target glass. His employment grievance supplies motive. However, no released fact assigns him the forced opening, broken neck, fragment wrapper, poisoned miniature or actual pour. That missing middle limits confidence.

**Brie** remains the strongest rival. She deliberately creates the unattended interval, handles the glass, and has an unpaid-service grievance. Clear kitchen substances do not clear a dose put directly into the glass. Towel fibers have a straightforward innocent explanation.

**Penny** admits reaching the glass beside her open case, giving a plausible concealed-carrier opportunity. Her inspectable paper case and surviving ownership letter weaken the motive and concealment theory without excluding poison delivery.

**Al** handles the glass, leaves a favor, and accounts for plaster. His reportedly sealed, inspectable favor materially weakens the miniature theory. It does not prove he could not use another common favor. Plaster outside the glass establishes contact, not contamination inside it.

**Chip** unloads a blue object and tools near reception, but produces the actual empty open-bottom copy in his kit. His selfish withholding of the receiving warning explains suspicious concealment independently of murder. Swapping bottles earlier remains conceivable but unsupported.

Justin could innocently mistake similar blue bodies without checking underneath, move an already-used original for photography, and lift a previously poisoned glass off the lease. That is a coherent innocent account, not a physical impossibility. His admitted movement therefore supports suspicion rather than proving the transfer.

## Naturalism and confusing details

The institutional fights, invoice dispute, legal qualification and insecure academic credit feel recognizably human. The comic dialogue works, though repeated polished confessions make the ensemble sound rehearsed. So many guests handling or shifting objects around one donor glass strains realism; it works as a crowded puzzle interface more than a naturally witnessed scene.

Original versus replica, star glass versus rental stem versus PROP, and canceled lighting versus independent security power are usefully distinguished. The bottle-to-miniature-to-glass theory is intelligible. What remains confusing is why the matched fragment's folded favor paper has no individualized provenance, and why no timing or independent observation separates Justin's photography from an innocent later movement of the already-used bottle. The dark-blue original and replica share a body appearance, so failure to inspect the bottom is not itself evidence of lying. The final releases reduce rival suspicion chiefly through checkable offers and explanations; they do not actually report all of those checks as performed.
