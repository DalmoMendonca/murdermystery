## Physical causal argument

The historical bottle was secured intact at 5:45, stolen through forced cabinet and case entry at 6:12, and later recovered with a newly broken neck. A contaminated blue fragment joins that neck gap. This supports opening or breaking the original to obtain poison, rather than poison supplied only through food or an innocuous prop.

A LARCENY No.1 miniature in reception waste contained cyanide; unopened stock held harmless fragrance. The miniature is a plausible portable poison carrier. The matching contaminated fragment in folded favor paper supports an opening/transfer episode, but neither paper nor miniature is assigned to a named person.

The individual museum-star glass contained cyanide; shared punch, food, sauce and the examined alternate bottles/glass were clear. The glass could have received residue while empty between 6:32 and 6:44, later dissolved by punch poured at 6:46. Photographic emptiness does not exclude invisible residue. Casey's tray custody supports this pre-fill route, although snapshots cannot establish continuous protection.

Ella admits putting a blue bottle onto the staff-returns shelf and identifies it with the recovery image. The recovered item is the original, not the open-bottom copy now in Chip's kit. She also admits holding the target glass while extracting her card. Her undisclosed sale of a promised donation and false revised delivery date give an immediate reason to prevent Grant's speech.

Together, identified-source handling, target handling, and immediate motive distinguish her. The missing links are an observed transfer, her possession of the contaminated miniature, her connection to the forced cabinet, and proof that the recovered bottle remained where she placed it. I infer those actions as the simplest guilty explanation; I do not treat them as witnessed facts.

## Innocent interpretations

Ella may truly have confused similar dark-blue bottles without inspecting the base. Another guest could have placed the original where she expected a copy, used it before or after her move, or substituted it afterward. Her base-only glass contact is compatible with adjusting a card. These explanations require extra unobserved events, but the releases do not close them.

Barb's record manipulation can be negligence and reputational protection rather than participation. Dada's threat and apparent-collapse scheme are independently explained by the canceled art piece and clear prop. Scholarship explains Art and Reed's requests for the bottle documentation. Tess's late admission meaningfully constrains direct participation in the theft, unlike most final motive-softening claims.

## Authorial and behavioral tells

At stage 8 almost everyone receives a tidily supported innocent explanation, while Ella gives a short, unsettled response and returns attention to her card. That contrast points toward her by authorial structure. It should not substitute for physical evidence. Her continued concern about delivery embarrassment could equally reflect social panic.

The symmetrical sequence of thirty admissions of misconduct is conspicuous. Repeated claims that a dead donor does not pay bills or remove liabilities are weaker exoneration than they sound: revenge, panic, personal guarantees, temporary announcement suppression and irrational action remain possible. I treated these as motive adjustments, not logical exclusions.

## Naturalism

The gala politics and service-payment dispute create a credible reason for an unattended glass. Separate safety override, service order, camera power and cabinet alarm make the physical setting coherent. Individual dialogue generally maintains useful character texture.

The abundance of hidden records, tool access, glass touches and simultaneous confessions feels purpose-built. The historical poison requires accepting a modern museum retaining dangerous original contents at a gala; withheld display approval and locked secondary containment help, but Grant's empty-bottle assurance carrying security past an independent collections warning still suggests severe negligence.

## Confusing or incomplete details

- No timeline fixes Ella's move relative to the theft, other glass handlers or bottle recovery.
- The contaminated miniature's owner, color, possession and disposal are not established; common labels intentionally do not identify a guest.
- Folded favor paper containing the matching fragment is not tied to an attendee.
- Both blue objects look similar, so identification by recovery image is suggestive rather than a guaranteed confession of knowingly handling poison.
- Empty-glass photographs may initially mislead players into assuming no dose was present. The snapshot caveat makes residue/pre-fill contamination available without naming a chemical form.
- Plaster and cloth occur outside the glass; they establish innocent contact pathways but do not clear the people who supplied them.
- The duplicate camera/show/hold distinctions are accurate and useful, though repeated enough to feel like correction of anticipated misreadings.
- Stage 1's Claire name and several punctuation marks rendered as replacement characters in the console. The scoring file used Claire O’Scuro and validated successfully. My attempted repeat of stage 1 with UTF-8 failed the once-only release guard; no extra release was read.

I would accuse Ella at the table. I would not claim the available evidence proves every step of her theft, transfer and dosing beyond a reasonable alternative.
