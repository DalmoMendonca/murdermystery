# Trial C blind review, version 21

Final accusation: Elon Mosaic. Confidence: 8/10 on the supplied scale, strong circumstantial case with reasonable alternatives. I have read exactly the eight sequential player releases, not a reveal or private identity.

## Earliest fair identification and provisional leaders

Stages 2–4: Artie was only a motive-based provisional leader, chiefly because the gift required his resignation. Stage 5: Brie led provisionally because the uncovered service station connected her to the glass opportunity. Stage 6: Elon became the strongest physical candidate, admitting blue-bottle shelf placement and donor-glass contact; identification was still pending, so accusation was premature. Stage 7 is my earliest defensible accusation: the recovered bottle is the historical original, making Elon's account place him with the actual cyanide source and the target glass. That is fair as a reasoned strongest-case accusation, not as unique deductive proof. Stage 8 improves comparison but does not make the case conclusive.

## Physical causal argument

The original held retained contents, was secured with an intact neck at 5:45, and disappeared after forced cabinet and secondary-case opening at 6:12. The recovered original has a fresh neck gap; the blue fragment in folded favor paper fits it and contains cyanide. The original, fragment, discarded miniature and inside of Grant's museum-star glass all test positive. Shared punch, meal, sauce and alternative studio/PROP containers test clear. Unopened fragrance favors test clear, so the contaminated miniature was individually repurposed rather than a poisoned favor batch.

The economical reconstruction is that someone stole and opened the original, transferred cyanide into a miniature favor, then introduced a small amount into the donor glass while it stood unattended between 6:32 and 6:44. Clear punch was added at 6:46 and Grant drank at the toast. Photographs of an apparently empty glass do not exclude a small residue; they are snapshots. A separate transfer container also explains moving the conspicuous original to an ordinary returns shelf.

Elon admits putting the bottle on that precise shelf and moving Grant's glass off his envelope. Inventory proves the recovered bottle is the original, whereas the display copy has an open bottom and is back in Chip's kit. Elon therefore has the strongest direct source-plus-target connection. Desire for Grant's trustee place supplies a plausible benefit.

The evidence does not directly attribute the 6:12 theft, neck break, fragment paper, contaminated miniature or dosing act to Elon. His bottle handling could follow another person's dosing. Nor does stage 8 prove that the bottle he placed and the one recovered must be the same without intervening movement, though his own “that's your recovered bottle” claim supports it.

## Strongest actual rivals

Brie deliberately removed coverage, handled the glass and left it on a towel. Clean food and punch do not exclude her poisoning the empty glass with a miniature. Her unpaid-work dispute need not be financially rational to motivate violence.

Penny had an open case beside the glass and returned for gloves. That offers a plausible transfer/concealment route. Inspection offered is not inspection completed. The surviving fourth letter weakens a document-elimination motive but does not erase resentment.

Frank had a pry bar and passed through reception with a plaster model. Legitimate rigging explains the tool and exterior dust, yet does not fully account for movements or rule out forced entry.

Al handled the glass and a favor. His still-sealed favor and outside plaster make his described handling more credibly innocent. Another miniature was available in principle. Chip's open-bottom copy substantially weakens his apparent bottle connection, while his tools and reception visit leave residual opportunity. Hugh retains a serious personal-guarantee grievance even though the firm's refund liability survives death; he lacks the physical link the leading candidates have.

## Innocent interpretations

Elon could honestly mistake a similarly shaped dark-blue original for the display copy, move it for housekeeping, and independently lift the glass to recover his envelope. Not inspecting the underside is ordinary behavior. The fresh neck gap raises suspicion, but no statement says he noticed it. A previous guest could have dosed the glass and left the original nearby.

Exterior plaster and fibers are well explained by Al's hands, Frank's model, Brie's towel and Elle's textile/card. They should not be treated as interior poison traces. The theatrical threat and collapse plan, Paige's publication threat, Justin's recorded demolition phrase, Tess's unrelated broken glass and the camera shutdown each have specific innocent or lesser-misconduct explanations. Dishonesty is common across the cast and cannot itself identify a murderer.

## Authorial and behavioral tells

I did not use terse wording or a character's unusually weak defense as evidence. Elon's final statement is much shorter and less elaborately exculpatory than most others; that risks becoming an authorial tell independent of the material case. Nearly all other final statements offer documents, surviving obligations, moral clarification or alternate benefits. A reader might infer selection by narrative allocation rather than solve the transfer.

Likewise, repeated “a dead donor/client does not pay” defenses should not be read as exclusions. A person may kill despite obligations, out of revenge, or for a distinct personal benefit. The mystery is strongest when it rests on the identified original and actual glass contact, not the rhetorical neatness of everyone's explanations.

## Naturalism

The museum disputes, incomplete provenance, receiving records, service authorization and separate lighting/security systems feel coherent. Having a public-facing display copy and locked dangerous original makes practical sense. The returns shelf and multipurpose sideboard provide plausible ordinary traffic.

Twenty-two guests each volunteering a carefully balanced scandal and then a polished clarification is distinctly a game structure. Multiple people recalling their precise incidental handling of the same donor glass feels engineered, although it is understandable once investigators focus on it. The dialogue's comic names and dry phrasing fit the setting. The dense paperwork may slow a party player, but the laboratory and service timeline are usable without actual image layout.

## Confusing or unresolved details

The fragment is in folded favor paper without an identified owner, place/time of recovery or a demonstrated relation to Elon's envelope. The miniature likewise has no recipient label. These are intentionally weak attribution points; they cannot independently select him.

The original and copy circulate through kit, reception and shelf in ways that need attention. The final copy-in-kit finding and original identification resolve much of this, but exact timing of Elon's move versus other contacts is absent. The target glass window is clear, individual movements within it are not.

Cyanide's historical survival, composition and dose are asserted through laboratory results rather than explained. That is adequate as a supplied game fact, but precise quantity or “empty” appearance cannot be inferred. Grant's false emptied assurance remains unexplained, leaving his own knowledge of the exhibit's danger unclear.

Overall: a fair strongest-case accusation begins at stage 7; the supplied evidence supports Elon better than the rivals but leaves a credible innocent handling account. Stage 8 sharpens explanations without delivering unique physical proof.
