## Physical causal argument

The original contained cyanide, was stolen from locked storage at 6:12, and acquired fresh neck damage. The matching fragment and a discarded miniature fragrance bottle both carry cyanide. Those facts support opening/damaging the original and transferring poison into an ordinary guest favor for unobtrusive delivery. Grant's new star glass stood exposed from 6:32 to 6:44. Small residue could have remained in an otherwise empty-looking glass and dissolved when Casey added clean shared punch at 6:46. The clean punch and other tested food/props localize the attack to the individual glass. Justin handled that glass and placed the original at the shelf where it was recovered. His job/blog grievance supplies motive.

The missing link is **who had the fragment-bearing favor paper and contaminated miniature**. Its recipient is explicitly unnamed, and no supplied witness attributes that paper to Justin. There is also no witness identifying the cabinet thief or observing transfer/dosing. Justin may have moved the already-used original by mistake; being the last known person to move a poison bottle is not necessarily being its thief or dispenser. The final defense repeats that innocent possibility rather than contradicting an authenticated observation.

I avoided treating confession length, tone or specificity as proof. ## Naturalism and confusing details

Museum bureaucracy, contested provenance, wages and funding disputes feel coherent. The unattended glass has a credible operational cause. A donated historical poison retained for more than a century is a conspicuous mystery convenience, but confirmed laboratory cyanide makes its survival acceptable within the story. The open-bottom copy is a clear, useful discriminator, though an unusually convenient replica design.

Twenty-two similarly constructed misconduct admissions are demanding to track and can feel synchronized. The final everyone-defends-their-own-evidence speeches are less natural than ordinary conversation, though playable as structured rounds. The phrase empty glass is compatible with invisible poison residue, but it should not be read as laboratory confirmation of no liquid at every intervening moment. Fragment in folded favor paper is materially under-attributed: a reader may wrongly assume it came from the same favor a character mentioned, despite many common favors and no chain of custody. Blue replica in Chip's kit versus the loose original Justin calls a copy is resolvable after inventory findings, but can initially sound like inconsistent prop continuity. Broken rental stem and original bottle-neck fragment are helpfully distinguished at the end.
