# Three prototype cases — inference audit, not a solved-case certificate

Eligible selected worlds remain Ella/30, Justin/22, Elon/22. All other roles read the revised innocent scenes. These are controlled comparisons to the previously drawn selections. This document is private and must never be shown to a blind reader.

## Shared physical argument

The original was intact in receiving, then missing after forced storage opening. It is recovered with a fresh neck gap. A cyanide-bearing fragment joins that gap; a contaminated fragrance miniature can transport material. The target glass contains cyanide, while the punch batch does not. Empty-looking glass photographs permit contamination before the later punch pour. They do not establish a continuously empty glass or identify the person doing the transfer.

The selected guest has a specific motive and admits both moving the loose blue bottle to the recovered location and handling the victim's glass before Method comparison. Act III identifies that recovered object as the original, changing the significance of their claimed prop handling. This source/target conjunction makes a shorter explanation of the selected guest's behavior than an unobserved additional poisoner followed by coincidental relocation. **Shorter is not proof:** the innocent mistaken-handler explanation remains possible, and an independent thief need not be an accomplice.

The fragment and miniature are not individually attributed. A claim that the selected guest is uniquely proven by chemistry would be false. If readers select primarily because one speaker has been allocated the important objects, the architecture still fails naturalism even if the ranking targets pass. Do not solve that by giving every rival the same two contacts.

## Ella

Motive: concealed sale of a promised donation; impossible delivery dates and portrait exclusions. Conduct: removes the presumed copy from the donor photograph, places it on the staff-returns shelf, lifts the glass to retrieve her card. Earlier disclosures, not a final confession, establish those actions. Innocent defense: the bottle really looked like a copy, and moving it did not require examining the base; another person could have poisoned the glass. Her final admission must not be counted as pre-vote evidence.

## Justin

Motive: wrong property story, threatened bookshop employment and concealment of a source. Conduct: stages a replacement introduction with the presumed copy, moves it onto the shelf for a plain background, lifts the glass off his lease, stops recording. Innocent defense: staging is vanity rather than dosing; source protection explains an interrupted recording; genuine lease does not corroborate object handling. **Naturalism risk remains:** shelf relocation may serve the designer rather than the blogger. A blind reviewer is entitled to reject it.

## Elon

Motive: thwarted board ambition and unauthorized corporate withdrawal. Conduct: handles the presumed copy while redirecting a trustee toward acquisitions, places it on the returns shelf, lifts the glass off an envelope. Innocent defense: collector confidence isn't a base inspection; the advisers' refusal explains his embarrassment, not poison transfer. The acquisition conversation is self-reported, not an independent witness of innocent handling.
