# The gift nobody wanted to accept

This is a small implementation after the process reset, not round24 or an accepted game. Three paired dossiers and actual questions are in `paired-scenes.yaml`. Shared player evidence is in `evidence.yaml`. No miniature carrier, fragment-matching exercise, or open-bottom display copy is needed for its accusation.

## Crime and cover

Grant's proposed gift makes him the museum's effective owner. He demands a celebratory photograph and toast before the board has accepted the terms. The selected murderer steals the historical poison from preparation storage after forcing the cabinet, contaminates Grant's individual glass during interrupted service, and leaves the original among staff returns. The shared drink is added afterward. Grant dies at the gala. Acquisition records explain why the source exists; the fiction contains no quantities or practical poison instructions.

The scene is not thirty people passing a bottle. The selected culprit commits the theft privately. Other guests are pursuing payment, control of the announcement, conservation approval, or the education program. The source's retained contents are not confirmed to responding security until after the death: Grant's false assurance that it had been emptied is initially treated as an exhibit-theft issue. That mistaken response is consequential, not an assertion that a known active poison can safely be ignored.

The three G accounts do not volunteer another uniquely guilty bottle accident. They attempt to explain the death through Grant's own behavior or earlier service. Their concrete claimed observations can be challenged later. The I accounts admit the same kind of interference, then explain the actual conduct and its consequences. A real scandal is corroborated in both branches; corroboration of that scandal does not exonerate a murderer.

## Suspicion is a constructed case, not an exclusive theorem

The goal is a best-supported accusation with credible rivals. The previous foundation audit remains valid about missing attribution, but it must not silently raise the requirement to eliminating every imaginable alternative. The user explicitly accepts residual uncertainty and does not want a new rule guaranteeing innocent testimony.

For each cover, ask whether the person has motive and opportunity, whether their concrete explanation conflicts with common evidence, and whether that conflict has an ordinary innocent explanation. Count a discrepancy only once. A witness who says "I assumed" is different from one who insists on having observed the event. Deliberately manufacturing a story about the victim's access to poison is significant, but not an automatic fingerprint match.

**Unresolved acceptance risks:** three roles do not establish thirty-role feasibility; self-reported observations can be mistaken; a G-only alternative theory can become an authorial tell; the quiet education role still needs a strong enough actual case. No blind test should be run until the all-thirty plan and five competing cases address these risks. No claims about scores are made.

## Release structure

1. Introductions and the sixteen-envelope hunt: gift conditions, canceled education rooms, catering authorization, source history, loan safety restrictions and planned service. The hunt retains its separate game and prize.
2. Motive: reveal the unsigned gift and initial theft/death report, then ask the named questions. No confirmed chemical or supplied suspect ranking.
3. Opportunity: reveal the working service card and brief witness accounts, then ask about the interruptions. The card has three consequential orders, not a registry of thirty corroborations.
4. Method: release the source's sealed storage record, glass stock photograph and laboratory findings. Ask the named questions. The packet provides the answers; guests assemble the case.
5. Ballot and Coming Clean: use the established top-three procedure. The confession must not introduce the first essential identifying clue.

Every essential hunt fact is repeated within the scheduled evidence, so missing an envelope cannot make the main case unsolvable. The same exhibits apply whichever character is selected. Staff are witnesses, not additional culprit choices.
