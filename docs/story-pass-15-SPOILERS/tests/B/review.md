# Blind review of releases 01–08

I read only the eight checkpoint files, in numeric order. Each result file was saved and its existence checked before opening the following release. Scores were not revised. I did not inspect a culprit selection.

## Final inference

Justin Tyme is my final lead (9/10), followed by Al Baster and Brie DeVivre (6/10 each). Justin says he carried a small bottle in his map sleeve and removed it beside Grant's glass. The lab found a small unmarked bottle in reception waste with residue compatible with the stolen original and the contaminated glass. That is a real evidentiary conjunction: concealment-capable carrier, small container, location, and a motive involving threatened source disclosure.

It does not yet identify Justin's bottle as the recovered bottle or establish that he put anything into the glass. There is no time for his action, account of disposal, fingerprint, ownership mark, distinctive bottle feature, or link to the 6:12 forced cabinet opening. A high ranking is justified; a uniquely demonstrated solution is not.

The final lead is also conspicuously signposted by composition. Twenty-one speakers receive explanations of their threatening language, objects, money, or performance; the last speaker receives a newly incriminating bottle and no explanation of its contents. The decisive distinction is partly a writing pattern. I would probably select Justin in a game even if the physical inference felt incomplete, because the final paragraph tells me which account was intentionally left open.

## Story plausibility and speech

The museum setting, disputed provenance, donor control, interrupted exhibition, and continuing reception give the crime a coherent institutional backdrop. Keeping reception service separate from the canceled stage sequence usefully avoids requiring a blackout to explain a busy sideboard. The locked hazardous bottle and an independently operating storage alarm also give the source theft a clear mechanism.

The century-old poison requires a little more explanation: the text asserts residual cyanide consistency but does not say what the preserved contents were or why they remained usable. Brief laboratory wording about preserved cyanide solution or solids would make the premise easier to accept without turning the story into chemistry instruction.

The introductions are lively and suitable for a pun-heavy party. Later speeches are fluent but too uniformly articulate. Almost every speaker confesses misconduct, specifies Grant's threat, and supplies a wry moral verdict. Lines such as being fooled and lying afterward, influence that could not be delivered, and deciding when history became public sound composed for the same essayist. Real speech needs more variable length, evasions, interruptions, personal diction, and incomplete recollection.

Grant's assistant functions as an exceptionally ubiquitous courier of incriminating documents. Across the round she catches virtually everyone, produces their most damaging message, and knows their private mistake. Give these confrontations a few different agents or established sources, or explicitly establish a dossier and a planned pressure campaign.

## Early giveaways and fair planting

No early release uniquely pointed to Justin. I initially favored Artie on removal motive, Chip on source knowledge, then Hugh/Monet on threatened ruin, and Al/Brie on glass contact. That progression is reasonably fair in its broad opportunity structure.

The bottle opportunity is late rather than fairly planted. Early Justin facts concern records, a recorder, and a map; none suggests a map sleeve holding a container. The small unmarked bottle first appears in the penultimate release and its association with Justin appears only at the end. A player can solve by matching those adjacent statements, but cannot build that particular inference across the evening.

Some other late explanations similarly answer clues I had not actually been given. Chip's handling of a glass replica, Vincent's brush-water bottle, Elle's FIXER possibility, Reed's waste sticker, and Art's potentially cropped research photograph do not all have developed prior suspicious sightings. This creates the feel of explanations for an unseen evidence round.

## Explanations versus exclusions

The clear punch, meal, sauce, PROP glass, brush-water bottle, and FIXER bottle exclude specific contaminated items. They do not exclude the owners of those items from contaminating Grant's glass separately.

Al's claim that the glass was empty when he held it is not an innocence condition. Poison placed into an empty glass would still be delivered when the punch was poured. Brie's claim that she only wiped the stem and base explains exterior cloth fibers, but does not independently establish that she never touched the bowl. Neither claim is contradicted by the clean shared punch.

A canceled performance rules out a performed collapse effect as the mechanism; it does not rule out Dada or Elle using cyanide. A signed conservation objection does not rule out Chip stealing the original. An estate inheriting a claim does not rule out Hugh's anger or an attempt to remove a threatening person. Blocked financial and governance transactions weaken instrumental motives without eliminating revenge, exposure avoidance, or mistaken beliefs.

Hugh and Tess entering after the theft report is the strongest person-specific exclusion, and even that excludes personally performing that earlier theft only if the receipts establish first arrival with no prior access. It does not exclude later handling of stolen poison. No supplied rule makes a single thief and poisoner mandatory.

## Continuity and unresolved ambiguities

Anne begins with education cuts and a forged petition endorsement, then shifts to a family archive and copying an omitted history. Penny begins with selectively disclosed family letters and then shifts to a pledge and refund. These can coexist, but the missing transitions make them feel like exchanged character threads. Explain the family link to Anne's educational role and plant Penny's pledge early, or keep each final explanation attached to the established conflict.

The Salon camera becomes an office camera in later explanations. State clearly at the first work order that the donor salon is the museum office if these are the same room. Otherwise this is a location inconsistency.

The final Dada account refers to a laboratory test of the jacket, whereas the released lab findings mention the PROP glass and various bottles but no jacket. Put that result on the actual lab card or remove the assertion.

The theft report is initially presented as a signed conservation report, later explicitly as Jordan's report. This is reconcilable if Chip filed Jordan's account, but that relationship should be stated. The original's recovery behind the sideboard leaves its path, the intermediary bottle's purpose, and the theft perpetrator unresolved.

## Concrete repairs

1. Plant Justin's map sleeve and a fleeting small-bottle sighting before the final reveal. Give the bottle a nonexclusive innocent interpretation at first.
2. Add a documented observation linking a distinctive feature of Justin's bottle to the recovered waste bottle, or a timed contradiction in his account. Keep at least one plausible competing account until the final comparison.
3. Establish who could enter preparation storage around 6:12 and how that evidence connects to the later contamination. Do not silently require that source thief and killer are the same person.
4. Give meaningful checks on Al's and Brie's glass contact rather than treating an empty glass or clean communal food as exoneration. Exact timing or an observation of their hands can narrow the relevant actions.
5. Distribute evidence explanations unevenly across rounds. Let an innocent character retain an unresolved embarrassing detail, and give Justin some mundane clarification, so paragraph asymmetry stops serving as the answer key.
6. Repair Anne/Penny continuity, define the salon/office relationship, and align the jacket and theft-report claims with the released documents.
7. Shorten and differentiate the speeches. Preserve a few excellent wry lines instead of giving every attendee the same confession-and-punchline cadence.

The case is playable and the final suspect is reasonable, but the supplied sequence rewards noticing the one unclosed final confession more reliably than proving an exclusive route from theft to glass to killer.
