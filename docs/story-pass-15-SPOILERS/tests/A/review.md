# Blind review of releases 01–08

I read only the eight checkpoint files in numeric order, wrote each result before opening the next, and have not inspected a hidden culprit. The final leading suspect is Ella Gance (9/10). Al Baster, Brie DeVivre, Hugh Bidder and Monet Bags remain materially possible, at lower scores. These are suspicion assessments, not a claim that the released facts prove one perpetrator.

## Plausibility and inference

The underlying mechanism is coherent: a locked historical bottle disappears at 6:12; the donor's recognizable glass is unattended from 6:32 to 6:44; punch is added later; Grant dies after the toast; the original is recovered behind the sideboard; the glass and a small waste bottle have compatible residue. An empty glass can be poisoned before service. The mount hold appropriately cancels the exhibition without canceling reception service, and independent security and lighting circuits prevent a false technical inference.

Ella's final admission gives her two important opportunities: presence before the theft and a small bottle handled beside the intended glass. Her withdrawn pledge and threatened embarrassment provide motive. Her earlier admission that she altered the acknowledgment table makes the final placement intelligible. The inference is stronger than a mere occupational guess.

It is still incomplete. We do not see Ella force the cabinet, transfer contents, put anything inside the glass, or dispose of the tested bottle. Her small bottle is not explicitly the unmarked waste bottle. Other people could steal or dose, jointly or separately. Her admission of early arrival establishes possibility rather than exclusivity.

## Natural speech

The introductory jokes fit a broadly comic dinner-party cast. Later speech is lucid but highly uniform: nearly everyone confesses a lesser wrongdoing, explains Grant's pressure, references an evidentiary document, and closes with an elegant ironic line. Thirty people do this twice, with virtually no evasion, confusion, interruption or inconsistent recollection. It reads as a suite of authored monologues rather than interviews after an alarming death.

Grant's assistant seemingly uncovers almost every character's secret, produces their worst message or document, and confronts them at the same gala. That concentration strains realism. The assistant and several corroborating staff are functionally central but absent from the 30-person attending roster; their testimony is filtered through others.

## Early giveaways and fair planting

The marked donor glass is heavily foregrounded from release 02. That gives a fair, understandable method clue but makes the toast route obvious well before the lab confirmation. The mount alarm and camera disconnection are plausible distractions, although the repeated instructions about independent circuits make their dismissal feel pre-managed.

Ella is not an early giveaway. Her first major conflict concerns a sold painting and replacement donation. The withdrawn pledge becomes important later. The table-card tampering in release 06 is a fair precursor to sideboard activity, but the decisive small bottle and pre-theft presence arrive only in her final speech. They have not been observed, disputed or connected to her in earlier releases.

The recovered small waste bottle appears in release 07 and Ella's matching class of object appears in release 08. This is a valid linkage to investigate, but thin planting for a player expected to solve before the last release.

## Writing pattern versus deduction

The final release makes Ella conspicuous through asymmetry. Almost every other character gets an explanation that neutralizes the precise scary object or phrase introduced previously. Ella alone supplies an unrebutted small bottle beside the poisoned glass and explicitly corrects an apparently late ticket into early attendance.

I would select Ella both because those facts fit the crime and because her paragraph is visibly the exception to a clearance parade. The second route is too strong. A reader can find her by comparing narrative treatment without reconstructing the theft and service timeline.

## Contradictions and continuity

No decisive timeline contradiction prevents the proposed poisoning. However, the camera location drifts between Donor Salon, office privacy, and an office-covered camera. Clarify whether these names refer to the same space.

Anne's family letter and Robin's shipping company become substantial personal entanglements relatively late, without an earlier bridge from their education and activism roles. They are not strict contradictions, but they feel appended to furnish balanced secrets.

Chip's final defense refers to 'the glass you saw me handling,' yet the released speeches do not previously give a clear scene of Chip handling that object. Describe the observed replica handling before explaining it. Calling a bottle replica 'the glass' also risks confusion with the drinking glass.

Dada says the lab tested the jacket, while the laboratory release explicitly lists the PROP glass and several liquids, not a jacket result. Include the actual jacket result if it matters. Several final speakers cite records that are described but never independently released; their existence and interpretation should not automatically be treated as guaranteed truth.

## Explanation is not exclusion

Al handling an empty donor glass does not clear him: preloading the empty glass is the leading mechanism. His plaster being ordinary sculpture residue addresses the outside, not the interior. Brie wiping the stem and base before filling likewise does not exclude introducing poison elsewhere. Clean punch and sauce rule out those sources, not their custodian using the stolen poison.

Clean fixer, brush water and rehearsal props discredit specific competing-container theories. Their owners could still use the unmarked bottle. A blocked financial benefit or continuing estate liability does not erase revenge, concealment or a mistaken belief about benefit. Late ticket or admission records do not prove a person never entered earlier, and late arrival does not exclude collaboration. Keep these distinctions explicit in adjudication.

## Concrete repairs

1. Plant Ella's small bottle before the final speech through a brief neutral witness observation or photograph. Give it an ordinary claimed purpose, so identifying it later requires evidence.
2. Independently corroborate her pre-theft presence with a timed image or interaction; avoid introducing the decisive correction as her own volunteered confession.
3. Give at least one other character an unresolved opportunity and one independently established exclusion from the relevant interval. Avoid clearing the entire cast in identical paragraph forms.
4. Link the waste bottle to Ella through a modest, interpretable physical detail established earlier. Do not rely only on 'small bottle' plus generic compatible residue.
5. Place the acknowledgment table relative to the reception sideboard and record when Ella changed the card, Al lifted the glass, and Brie wiped it. Current ordering cannot separate these contacts.
6. Vary the interviews: some short replies, a disputed memory, a witness correction, and fewer polished concluding jokes. Distribute discoveries among characters instead of making the assistant omniscient.
7. Clarify Salon/office naming, introduce Chip's observed replica handling, and align Dada's claimed testing with the actual laboratory release.
8. Establish why the theft was known before the toast yet service near an unattended target continued. A specific search boundary or delayed communication would help.

The story supplies a workable poisoning puzzle and a rational final suspect, but currently gives that suspect away through the final speech structure more strongly than it proves attribution.
