## What was improved

The source/replica/carrier/target chain is more intelligible than earlier cloth-transfer devices. Uninterrupted-alibi assumptions and several unsupported interpretations were exposed. Dialogue and agendas are more differentiated in places. Sequential gates, frozen inputs, result hashes and archives make failures auditable. Sent public assets remain hash-locked.

Those are useful improvements to diagnosis and development discipline. They do not establish that guests will enjoy or solve the game appropriately. No candidate has consistently met all of the requested gates. Only a few new guilty branches have been experimentally eligible; the other branches cannot be called supported because six text fields exist for them.

## What went wrong in the process

1. **The shared premise remained too weak while its wording changed.** Original relocation, copy confusion and fragment/favor accidents repeatedly allocated the important physical story to G alone. That made the selected speaker special without requiring enough deduction.
2. **Scandals were mistaken for murder cases.** Anger, forgery and access often reasonably scored4. More colorful confessions did not supply source, administration or concealment grounds.
3. **The score curve became the design target.** Attempts to hold suspicion up added synchronized activities and repeated handling. A good score does not excuse a contrived scene.
4. **Tests were too large and too similar.** Repeated four-reader/eight-stage runs consumed effort after midpoint failure was already apparent. The same three selected worlds did not demonstrate all-thirty feasibility.
5. **Production distance was underreported.** New prototypes still need actual questions, private setup, endings, schema integration, visual evidence and full PDF inspection. Old checks pass an older edition, not the current story.

## Revised plan, with explicit gates

### 1. Establish the architecture before writing another edition

Write a compact two-story bible: what actually happened, and what guests initially believe happened. Retain the museum gala and hunt. Specify source knowledge, theft/access, target preparation, administration, disposal and aftermath. Separate observed facts from inferences. Record assumptions rather than silently treating mixed waste or a photograph as exclusive custody.

Sketch one-paragraph **I/G cover premises for all30** against the same fixed reports. This is feasibility planning, not180 polished speeches. Each role needs an agenda-driven scene, a real suspicious action, an innocent mitigation and a guilty account challenged by already planted facts. Include quiet and reserve roles. No host-selected clues, absent-role dependencies, universal cloth/favor accident, perfect synchronized alibis or new names/public history.

**Gate:** every role can participate without changing shared evidence or an innocent witness. At least five core red herrings are genuine competing crime explanations with different mini-payoffs. Enough additional cases make eight/22 and ten/30 a plausible hypothesis. If this cannot be achieved naturally, reject the premise before prose and testing.

### 2. Build a small representative implementation

Choose three contrasting cover families from that architecture, including a quiet role and a role central to service/collections. Keep the five core rival scenes. Draft both branches together with comparable scene participation and dramatic substance. Build the actual question context; do not evaluate disconnected paragraphs as though they were the complete party.

For every proposed suspicious act ask: **would this person plausibly do the nonmurder version to pursue their own goal?** If the only answer is “we need a clue,” cut it. Do not fix source attribution by making every visitor handle an exhibit or fix target attribution by putting everyone’s paper under a glass.

**Gate:** root produces a written accusation argument and a credible innocent counterargument for each selected case. A single skeptical editorial walkthrough checks those arguments, cross-world compatibility and absence handling. It can reject cheaply. An editorial permission to test is not a pass.

### 3. Use bounded tests that can stop early

Run one fresh sequential reader first. Keep the eight release order, but use compact per-role reasons and explanations for the main changing cases. When ActII fails breadth or the reviewer identifies an early narrative tell, stop that test and archive the partial result. Finish Method only when it can answer an unresolved design question; label any diagnostic continuation explicitly.

A promising complete result earns one fresh independent repeat on identical material. Reader disagreement triggers diagnosis, not another batch of near-identical reviews. At most two tested revisions may use the same causal architecture; after two failures, replace the mechanism rather than its adjectives. Report failure, changes and evidence after each round. No averaging away a failed reader.

**Gate:** the requested breadth, late identification,3–4 final rivals and naturalism survive independent judgment. These scores remain diagnostics, not human difficulty percentages. Acceptance requires case explanations grounded in evidence, not just the correct name.

### 4. Expand and deliver only after the mechanism survives

Complete the30 paired dossiers and speeches, checking every selected world against fixed staff facts. Test contrasting roles and both attendance sizes, using different selections rather than only repeating the familiar three. Resolve packet questions, briefings, hunt, ballot, Coming Clean and host sequence. Then integrate the schema and assets, render/inspect every PDF page, regenerate ZIPs, commit and verify published hashes. Public sheets/invite remain locked.

**Gate:** actual thirty-route coherence, physical packet usability and reviewed outputs. A transcript test cannot satisfy human pacing or physical printing; state those practical limits honestly.
