## Why the filled-glass claim is usable, and its limit

The lie is purposeful only if the guest is trying to minimize the significance of their interruption or contact: “service was already finished” is an excuse ordinary people use. “I saw punch” as an isolated visual detail is an authorial clue delivery device. Rewrite from the individual's concern first, then check that the service-state commitment survives. Avoid identical clauses or a guilty-only sudden precision about minutes, liquids, and photographs.

The later photo contradicts an observation, **not an identity**. An innocent could confuse the donor glass with a different glass, conflate a later sighting, assume preparation was complete, or conceal a different visit. A guilty guest could make the same error without that error proving murder. Do not claim that no innocent can lie. The final case combines the false account of the specific target, its actual exposure, earlier motive and consequential behavior, and the source's independently established presence at that location. Several rivals still have concrete target/source-response connections without this particular challenged account; that is why plausible 5–6 alternatives can survive.

Check every selected world for a natural reason to make the claim. Brie knows service procedures and Barb knows the response chronology: their claims should be self-protective minimization, not implausible ignorance. If a variant cannot sustain a credible motive for the lie, flag that variant instead of patching it with an unrelated accident. A common mechanism across thirty guilty alternatives will always risk a branch tell; shared syntax and forensic vocabulary would make it worse.
