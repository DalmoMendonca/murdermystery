# Trial A, version 22: blind text-reader review

Final accusation: Ella Gance. Confidence: moderately high, approximately 75%; score 8/10. She has the strongest case, with reasonable alternatives remaining.

## Identification timing

Stages 1–4 did not support identifying the murderer. Artie was a provisional leader because forced resignation gave him an obvious motive; stage 4 deliberately gave almost everybody comparably serious motives. Stage 5 made Brie a provisional priority because the unattended glass was her service responsibility. Stage 6 shifted my provisional lead to Ella: she admitted placing the recovered blue bottle on the returns shelf and handling Grant's glass. The bottle's identity was still unresolved.

Stage 7 was my earliest defensible accusation. The recovered bottle was the original, not the copy Ella claimed, and it contained cyanide. That combines an objectively checkable discrepancy with contact with the particular victim glass. Stage 8 maintains that lead but does not make it conclusive.

## Physical causal case

The historical original was secured intact at 5:45, stolen after forced storage opening at 6:12, and recovered with fresh neck damage. Cyanide connects original, matching blue neck fragment, used miniature fragrance bottle, and victim glass. The similar blue display copy has an open bottom and cannot carry liquid. The recovered miniature is intact, so the fragment is not an ordinary broken favor bottle.

The donor glass was empty at photographed placement and collection, then filled with clear shared punch. A plausible method is transferring original contents into the miniature and contaminating the empty donor glass during the 6:32–6:44 sideboard interval. Photographing an empty glass would not exclude residue. The miniature would let poison travel under cover of a standard gift; its contamination, alongside clear unopened stock, supports deliberate use. The original could then be discarded on the returns shelf.

Ella admits both placement of that actual original and target-glass handling. She had sold the promised painting, lied about delivery, and wanted Grant's announcement stopped before investment loss and broken promises became public. She explains the bottle as a misidentified copy, but no corroborating observation establishes that explanation.

The case still lacks a person-specific link to the forced opening, the used miniature, or the folded favor paper containing the fragment. The statement that Ella placed the recovery-picture bottle is very consequential; it is her own statement, not a continuous independently documented chain. She may have genuinely moved an already-discarded original after someone else's transfer.

## Rivals and innocent explanations

Brie deliberately withdrew reception coverage and moved the glass. Clear food and punch eliminate a shared-kitchen route, but do not eliminate direct treatment of the empty glass. Her towel explains external cloth, not internal poison. She remains my strongest rival.

Chip had tools, a blue bottle, and concealed receiving notes. Original/copy substitution would fit his access, though the open-bottom copy still in his kit and his damaged-mount work support an innocent account. Cary and Frank possessed tools that could force storage, but their handling and rigging records supply ordinary explanations; neither is directly connected to liquid transfer.

Al handled the glass and used a favor as an invoice weight. Exterior plaster is consistent with that ordinary handling. His still-sealed favor weakens the theory that it supplied the contaminated miniature or fragment wrapping. Minnie and Penny both had target-glass access and concealed damaging materials; no poison-object handling is tied to either.

Ella could have moved the original unknowingly, and a different glass handler could have poisoned the glass earlier. The discontinuous photos explicitly preserve that possibility. Her claim is weaker than the combined incriminating acts, but cannot simply be declared false because she is the leading suspect.

Tess is the strongest exclusion under the single-killer theft-led chain: her admission receipt puts her outside at the forced opening, and her broken rental glass predates the donor glass. Other final defenses usually weaken motive rather than provide full alibis. An estate retaining claims, an audit surviving, or a client being unable to pay after death does not itself exclude a murderer.

## Naturalism and authorial tells

The sideboard's mixed use and invoice dispute create understandable access without a contrived total blackout. Security accepting a wealthy donor's unsafe assurance is credible institutional negligence. Distinguishing canceling show control from reception service and camera power avoids a common causal muddle.

Thirty similarly polished admissions of wrongdoing feel written to distribute suspicion. Final statements likewise tend to supply neat corrective documents and point directly at what players should examine. Ella's notably short final defense, lacking the concrete exonerating document most others introduce, feels like an authorial signal. My accusation does not depend on that signal; it follows original disposal and glass handling.

The retained hazardous contents of an 1891 bottle are accepted by the packet's lab, but their condition and potency after such storage are not explained. Nothing establishes the particular offender's prior knowledge that the original contents were usable, apart from general museum information. These are plausibility uncertainties, not reasons to invent another poison source.

## Confusion and limits

The fragment's folded favor paper and used miniature have no recipient-specific custody in the released text. This prevents an exclusive transfer chain. The bottle's recovery-picture identity needs careful reading: Ella's stage 6 claim becomes incriminating only when stage 7 identifies that same recovered item as the original.

Statements about surviving copies, sealed favors, and invoices can be read as offers of examination rather than independent laboratory findings. I kept that distinction in assessing alternatives. Empty-at-two-moments photographs do not prove no liquid ever entered the glass.

This review assesses text player releases only. I did not inspect images, visual layout, source, manifests, private selection, or other trials.
