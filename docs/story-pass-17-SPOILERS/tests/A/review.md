# Blind story review: version 17, trial A

Ella Gance is the strongest final suspect, scored 10/10 within the stated one-playing-character-murderer premise. This is confidence in the best explanation, not a claim of eyewitness certainty. Her path was 2, 2, 2, 5, 5, 7, 9, 10. Stage 7 was the decisive release; stage 8 strengthened it by placing her before the theft and with a miniature beside the donor glass.

The physical inference works: the stolen original had an intact neck when secured, its fresh blue break fits the wrapped fragment across the inventory stamp, both carry cyanide, and Ella admits folding a blue bit in gift paper and throwing it away. An intact miniature from reception waste also carries cyanide, whereas the shared punch and unopened favors are clean. She admits removing her miniature from her pledge envelope beside Grant's glass. Her explanation that she cut herself on blue glass inside a gift packet becomes a poor account of these combined facts. The common label does not identify the contaminated miniature as hers by itself. No witness reports seeing a pour, and the evidence does not directly identify who forced the cabinet. A competing theory could put another guest's poison fragment in her packet, but it requires several coincidences or deliberate framing without positive support.

The plausible story is that Grant's planned public acknowledgment threatened to expose her sale of the pledged painting. She arrived early, stole the poison, opened or damaged its neck, used a miniature to carry it unobtrusively, adulterated the empty museum-star glass during its unattended interval, and concealed the original and fragment near the reception sideboard. That sequence connects motive, means, opportunity, and misleading account. Her cut could have occurred during the break or concealment; its timing is not established. Her available sale proceeds also make the motive more about reputation and unwillingness to surrender money than unavoidable poverty.

The story nevertheless feels engineered in places. All thirty guests disclose a wrongdoing, coercion, escalation, then a clarification in matching waves. Many finale statements answer precisely the obvious objection to their earlier material. The surviving unanswered physical connection consequently signals Ella before a player completes the causal chain. Her early admission is almost uniquely tailored to a later forensic match. The plentiful favors, desk business, false threats, canceled cues, and colored bottles are sensible camouflage, but the total symmetry is conspicuous. Grant blackmailing or humiliating nearly everyone makes him a useful puzzle victim and a less fully human person.

Fair planting is strong. Assorted colored favor bottles, the sideboard's mixed use, the intact original receiving condition, the separate toast order, the forced storage alarm, and the canceled show sequence precede their payoff. The shard comparison is a discriminating physical fact rather than a subjective motive ranking. Al and Brie have honest reasons to touch the actual glass; their traces make those contacts tangible. Ella's shard account arrives before the fit and cyanide findings, so the player can update without hindsight. No hidden later rule is needed to interpret the evidence.

No hard inconsistency prevents the solution. The distinction between an early arrival and a later ticket collection is possible but convenient; a clear earlier attendance record would make the reversal feel less like a final correction. The laboratory says an intact miniature was recovered, while Ella's account describes a loose shard inside her packet; those are not logically incompatible, but the incriminating inference depends on treating the coincidence as implausible. A century-old sealed poison retaining cyanide sufficient for murder invites a material plausibility question that the contents-retained and laboratory findings address narratively. I did not consult outside sources to assess that chemistry.

Clarified misconduct is frequently mistaken for exoneration if read too generously. A debt surviving Grant's death does not erase rage or delay benefits. A blocked gift or frozen option does not prevent revenge. Clear brush-water, FIXER, sauce, or a prop only excludes that tested route, not access to the stolen cyanide. Brie wiping the stem and base is her account, not an independent elimination. The strongest real exclusions are the documented post-theft arrivals for Hugh, Tess, and Anya under a lone murderer theory, Anne's corroborated bookings during the likely glass window, Al's separately recovered clean miniature, and the independent cancellation records for the staged effects. Even those should be stated with their limits rather than treated as proof every guest statement is truthful.

Voices are distinctive enough to support play: Sue is exact about legal categories, Chip procedural, Dada possessive and theatrical, Penny family-conscious, Reed anxious about corrections, Anne focused on colleagues' jobs, and Al blunt about labor and payment. Ella's wounded social composure suits her motive. Some voices blur because almost everybody delivers polished, self-aware admissions and neatly balanced closing sentences. More uneven emotional responses, interruptions, or mundane recollection could reduce that shared authorial cadence without adding clues.

Overall: a fair and solvable physical chain with substantial camouflage, but a highly patterned confession structure. The final identification is stronger than the individualized dramatic motivation; Ella's public humiliation supports the murder but the transition from fraudulent pledge to planned poisoning remains inferred.
