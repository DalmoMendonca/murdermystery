# Blind review: version 18, trial A

Final accusation: **Ella Gance**, with moderately high confidence (roughly 80%). No identity file was consulted. All eight assessments were saved and separately validated in sequence.

Ella had sold her promised donation and feared Grant exposing her before the social circle on which she depended. Stage 8 adds the most consequential physical account: she held the donor glass, an open miniature was beside it, and she cleared what she calls broken gift packing. Stage 7 had already established cyanide in the donor glass, original poison bottle, fitted blue neck fragment and discarded fragrance miniature; the fragment was in folded favor paper. Ella's account therefore overlaps both likely transfer and disposal rather than merely the common sideboard opportunity. Her earlier statement about changing an acknowledgment card and excluding trustees from her photograph also fits the same visit.

This is a strongest-case accusation, not a conclusive demonstration. Nobody identifies the miniature as Ella's favor, she does not explicitly admit pouring it, and no released evidence places her at the 6:12 forced cabinet opening. Her account can be interpreted as innocent handling and tidying after another guest used the miniature. The final statement's wording makes that interpretation possible without inventing an accomplice. A single culprit could contaminate the glass, leave the waste, and have Ella clear it unknowingly.

Credible alternatives are Brie DeVivre, who directly handled the glass and deliberately left reception without cover; Monet Bags, who prolonged the service manager's absence; Barb Dwyer, who altered the incident account and moved the waste basket; and Al Baster, who handled the glass beside his favor. Al was my strongest suspect at stage 7, but external plaster is admitted contact, not administration, Frank independently supplies another plaster source, and Al invites inspection of his supposedly unopened favor. Minnie admits moving the glass in the final release, but her canceled blackout is not connected to cyanide. None has Ella's combination of open miniature and fragment-bearing packing disposal.

Earliest fair identification point: **stage 8**. At stage 7 the source and transfer route are fair to infer, but the common unassigned favor is insufficient to identify Ella. Stage 6 gives several direct handlers, and earlier stages mostly provide motives. A player can reasonably prioritize Al or Brie before the final statements without missing an obvious Ella-specific clue.

Fairness: the exact-one-player/no-accomplices premise is clear. Independence of camera power, cabinet alarm and lighting console is repeatedly specified, avoiding a misleading technical shortcut. Replica, PROP glass and original are distinguished by material and construction. Retained copies and dated documents appropriately explain lesser misconduct without pretending they provide total alibis. The strongest final clue is late and is an uncorroborated admission; a trace tying Ella to the contaminated miniature or the original theft would strengthen uniqueness. The glass interval is precise and usable.

Plausibility: donor blackmail, payment conflicts and compromised paperwork plausibly unite this setting, but Grant having leverage over essentially every attendee feels engineered. Almost everyone converging on the same sideboard is functional yet crowded. A historical poison retaining lethal potency, forced theft from two containment layers and transfer into fragrance packaging are asserted rather than explained; acceptable for a theatrical mystery, less persuasive as strict forensic realism. The unstaffed service explanation is concrete. Ella's final account never explains why a miniature was open or whose it was, leaving the most important causal step underdetermined.

Voice: introductions have memorable art-world puns and individual comic hooks. Longer rounds offer some distinct manners, particularly Chip's handling advice, Art's academic qualifications and Dada's theatrical pride. Their overall structure is highly repetitive: admission of wrongdoing, blackmail grievance, qualifying denial, and an explicit disclaimer that a document is not an alibi. Final statements repeatedly anticipate forensic arguments with unusual precision for spontaneous guests. Shortening some statements and varying willingness to confess would improve character texture.

Accessibility: thirty suspects and several document rounds create substantial working-memory demands. Exact named introductions and numbered documents help. A visible cumulative timeline and distinction chart for original/replica/PROP/favor would help readers without revealing the solution. In this text-only release, photo references appear as asset names, including REQUIRED and pass17/pass18 suffixes, rather than rendered evidence. Written descriptions supply the essential clues, but those internal filenames disrupt immersion and make it uncertain whether visual details are missing. Typography and times should be normalized, particularly the early `at6:29` spacing.
