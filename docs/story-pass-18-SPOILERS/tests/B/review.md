# Trial B blind review, version 18

Final accusation: **Justin Tyme**, moderate confidence, suspicion 8/10. I would choose him under the single-murderer premise, but would not call the evidence effectively conclusive. No secret identity was read.

The causal case is a cyanide-bearing historical original stolen at 6:12, subsequently broken at the neck; the joining fragment and contaminated miniature establish a transfer route rather than merely matching blue colors. Grant's individual glass, not shared punch or food, carried cyanide. Justin finally admits opening a favor beside his map and moving Grant's glass off the lease. That places one guest at both the plausible transfer container and the target glass. His livelihood and reputation were threatened by Grant. Al's favor is offered for inspection as still sealed, weakening the earlier plaster-based case.

The earliest fair point for a provisional identification is stage 8. Stage 7 establishes the method but leaves Al and Brie ahead of Justin because his open favor and glass handling have not yet been disclosed. Stages 1–6 cannot fairly single him out. Even stage 8 does not uniquely establish guilt: the recovered miniature has no name, no released witness identifies it as Justin's, and nobody ties him directly to the forced cabinet opening or the matching fragment. His statement about the original not being in his map sleeve is conspicuously defensive but is not independently contradicted by released evidence.

Brie is the strongest credible alternative (6/10): she intentionally left reception uncovered and directly moved the glass. Her explanation for cloth fibers is plausible but does not exclude contaminating the inside. Monet intentionally prolonged Casey's absence and knew where the glass was (5/10). Al directly handled it, although the seal offer and Frank's plaster model weaken the specificity of exterior plaster (5/10). Chip's kit, Penny's open case and Barb's handling of the waste basket also leave unresolved opportunities (5/10). None has an exclusion-grade alibi, and clear food or prop tests exclude routes rather than people.

Fairness strengths: chronology is precise; replica, favor and original are distinguished physically; laboratory findings are shared; common labels do not magically identify ownership; the camera, lighting and storage alarm are explicitly independent. Statements can lie under the announced rules, and the text avoids pretending that a timed message or a dated document continuously accounts for a person's movements.

Fairness limits: the decisive individual fact arrives only in the last speech as Justin's own admission, rather than a corroborated handling observation. The physical bottle/fracture evidence solves the route but does not identify its carrier. A later assertion of an intact favor seal should ideally have an investigator result, particularly when other guest claims may be false. The released material mentions photo identifiers but provides no images; I could assess only their accompanying prose. Any image-dependent clue would need a text equivalent.

Plausibility: the locked secondary case and forced-entry alarm make the theft intelligible. Continuing an unattended donor toast after a historical-poison theft is a severe institutional failure, but Grant's false reassurance and withheld condition warning give a reason. Cyanide retained from 1891 remains a specialist premise accepted from the laboratory findings rather than something a player should infer chemically. Almost every guest visits the same sideboard to remove, conceal or alter papers; the concentration of twenty-two distinct scandals and opportunities feels arranged for the game more than organically likely. It nevertheless prevents job titles from becoming automatic access proof.

Voice: role-specific details work well, especially the payment disputes, damaged research credit and institutional compromises. The long runs of confession speeches repeatedly use the same pattern: admit lesser misconduct, explain motive, qualify what it proves. By the final round many characters sound equally forensic and carefully self-limiting. Justin's recorder claim in stage 6 overlaps Paige's retrieval claim; if there are two recorders, label whose device each is so this does not read as an accidental continuity issue.

Accessibility: twenty-two names and eight dense releases impose a high working-memory load. A public evidence index and simple timeline would help without supplying the answer. Keep cyanide inside the glass distinguished from nonspecific plaster/cloth outside; preserve text descriptions of the fragment fit and replica's open bottom. The stage numbers were useful for freezing deductions and prevented hindsight from distorting the earlier uncertainty.
