# Architecture review — spoilers

This is a read-only review of rounds 16 and 17 and the immutable public character copy. I have not inspected private selections or the unfinished round 18 dialogue files. The Game Narrative and Genre Craft mystery references informed the review: a solution should be the best explanation of planted facts, and suspects should pursue their own purposes rather than deliver a prosecution followed by a defense.

## Recommendation

Retain the shared favors and the fixed glass timeline. Retain the fracture as evidence of the stolen original's presence at reception, but reject round 17's exact Act II disposal admission as the identifying device. Replacing its words with thirty more conversational versions will not repair its architecture. It remains the one guest volunteering the police's unusually specific recovery description.

Build round 18 around **a concealed crime amid several real attempts to control Grant's announcement**. The other guests are not all planning murder. Some want the toast delayed, the signature prevented, a debt acknowledged, an incriminating record recovered, or an humiliating image suppressed. Several of those actions genuinely create the conditions the killer exploits. They remain consequential after the poison route becomes clear.

This improves the investigation story: people are arguing over who made the night possible, who is using the death to escape another problem, and which records they will surrender. It also supplies alternatives without inventing fourteen innocent poisonings.

## The unavoidable attribution limit

If every physical exhibit is identical for all thirty culprit choices, those exhibits cannot by themselves distinguish one of those choices. They can establish source, route, timing, and physical contact. Identity must come from the selected world's statements, observations, or contradictions. That is a legitimate variable-culprit format, but it should be stated accurately in the design notes.

The existing final case has two extremes. Round 16 lets a reader prefer Al's actual contact to Justin's absence of a reassuring ending. Round 17 obtains identification by giving the guilty guest a distinctive source-fragment story. Neither is a balanced independent attribution mechanism. The new design needs a **specific contradicted account**, not an innocent-truth guarantee, an anonymous contaminated miniature treated as personal property, or a missing closure treated as proof.

A contradicted account can make the culprit the strongest inference while allowing that they handled incriminating material without committing murder. A perfect personal match to the poison source followed by an admission of handling a bottle beside the target glass will understandably generate near certainty. Do not demand three or four equally serious alternatives after simultaneously presenting that chain as conclusive proof.

## Proposed release structure

| Release | What becomes knowable | What must remain open |
|---|---|---|
| Hunt | Favors are ordinary; source is secured; Grant has simultaneous public and private disputes; toast has its own service order | Who stole the source; who interfered with the toast glass |
| Act I evidence | Death, secured-source theft, unsigned donor conditions | Which disputes actually benefit from death; announcement versus murder |
| Act I statements | Distinct wants and damaging decisions | No universal confession followed by an ethical explanation |
| Act II evidence | Cabinet forced at 6:12; target glass exposed 6:32–6:44; reception had several ordinary functions; records show service and security failures | Fragment recovery details and any personal source connection |
| Act II statements | Real attempts to delay, redirect, conceal, or obtain something; limited accounts of movements | No unique blue-glass/cut/favor-paper account; no categorical individual alibis manufactured by admission receipts |
| Act III first evidence | Original recovered behind sideboard; common miniature and target glass contaminated; shared refreshments clear; source fragment found in favor paper | Ownership of contaminated miniature; who placed poison in the glass |
| Act III statements | Accounts of the disputed material and service decisions, including a culprit's false explanation | Statements are claims that players must compare, not police-certified innocence |
| Act III final exhibit | Fracture comparison, already described as pending; actual recovered favor's intact condition | Fragment handler versus thief versus poisoner; several concrete alternative cases |
| Accusation | Players argue source + route + contradicted account + motive against alternatives | Coming Clean supplies no new essential proof |

The last physical exhibit must arrive **before ballots**, with discussion time. This is a proposed small change to release ordering, not a license to hide a fact until Coming Clean. If the implementation requires all Method evidence before Method statements, the account being tested must be planted before that evidence, without revealing its matching recovery details. Otherwise its explanatory value collapses: anybody can repeat what the report just told them.

## Specific changes to round 17 exhibits

1. **F1 / gift conditions:** preserve the unsigned agreement. Stop making this one document the entire motive landscape. Its significance differs: Artie loses a job, Monet faces an audit exposed by the transaction, Anne loses a teaching room, collectors lose control of an acquisition. The differences must produce different actions and consequences, not thirty versions of “Grant had leverage.”

2. **F2 / death and source:** preserve the historical acquisition, secured original, empty display replica, and 6:12 forced opening. Describe the missing source plainly. Do not reveal its color, fresh fracture, or the favor-paper recovery here. The closed receiving-case photograph is appropriate. A stolen source is suspicion of a theft; it does not establish who subsequently used it.

3. **F3 / service:** preserve all required times: glass unwrapped at 6:32, collected at 6:44, clean punch added at 6:46, toast at 6:49. Remove the blue fragment and folded favor-paper sentence from Act II. Keep the service-manager departure, but make its underlying dispute a planted, independently recorded event: a pending extra-course authorization prompted the trip; the finance refusal and service alteration identify the people whose decisions caused it. Do not claim all thirty guests were seen handling the glass. Reception proximity and a legitimate errand suffice for general opportunity.

4. **F4 / fracture:** keep the physical join across the inventory stamp. It is useful, legible evidence and need not become a more exotic laboratory trick. Its conclusion is “this material came from the original,” not “the person who disposed of it poisoned Grant.” The common favor paper establishes no owner. Its location establishes no exclusive custody. The intact contaminated miniature makes a claimed breakage of *that bottle* false, but does not prove that no other favor ever broke. Describe that distinction accurately.

5. **F5 / laboratory:** keep the clear shared punch and foods, and the contaminated original, fragment, miniature, and target glass. Keep cloth/plaster on the outside of the glass. Separate tested objects from people: clean brush water does not clear Vincent; clean fixer does not clear Faye; a clean favor does not prove Al never used another miniature. These negative results disprove particular routes. The statements should stop using them as whole-person acquittals.

6. **Al's separately recovered favor:** preserve it only if there is an actual documented reason and custody history for its collection. Its clean result teaches that ordinary fragrance favors are not poisoned stock. It should not function as a privilege that only Al gets. Do not add comparable recovery certificates for every other guest.

7. **Discovery 10 / camera:** retain the separation between Donor Salon camera and cabinet alarm. Show Barb's order and the junior officer's surviving notebook as two records with different purposes. Avoid a corrective annotation that tells players exactly how to reason. Disconnection at 6:29 cannot hide the 6:12 theft. It can still worsen the investigation and expose Barb to a serious accusation.

8. **Discoveries 14–16 / crisis and service:** preserve the alarm, collections' objection, Grant's false empty-bottle assurance, mounting hold, and independent toast. Make the timeline acknowledge an active incident, rather than pretending everyone forgot the hazard. Security requests assistance at 6:22; the existing text still needs a credible reason why service continues through 6:49. The least disruptive explanation is a limited storage closure while staff follow the mistaken search assumptions, with Grant pressing to continue reception. No heroic security response should be invented retrospectively in innocent Barb's speech.

## Three or four serious competing cases

Use the existing core cast for these cases so the 22-person version retains them. When one is the selected culprit, the others remain viable. Chip, Penny, or Sue can provide an additional alternative from their established actions. These are proposals for stronger records and testimony, not claims that the existing round already supplies every detail.

| Person / case | Concrete basis for suspicion | Contextual payoff that does not erase the case |
|---|---|---|
| Barb: suppression of the theft response | Orders a search before a theft entry; seeks the junior officer's notebook; permits a camera gap; knows the source location and institutional procedures | The alarm survives and camera timing cannot conceal the theft. Those facts limit the accusation; they do not explain why she wanted the notebook or prove she never approached reception |
| Monet: engineered service interruption | Conceals a financial message; blocks an authorization that takes the service manager away; has reason to prevent tonight's announcement | The unauthorized payroll transaction and kitchen refusal are real. No extra course was made. Neither fact excludes targeting the exposed glass |
| Brie: direct service interference | Alters Grant's service order, has a recorded threat over payment, wipes the target glass, and manages the staff's competing instructions | Clear punch and sauce defeat communal poisoning. Cloth on the exterior explains legitimate contact but does not account for all contact with the glass or explain the timing of the argument |
| Al: physical contact during a debt dispute | Takes or shifts Grant's distinctive glass while demanding payment; plaster corresponds to his actual contact; a favor is also present | The sampled favor is clean and an ordinary artistic gesture can explain the contact. His threat and access remain real; do not conclude that the clean bottle clears him |
| Chip, where needed: authority over the source and disputed condition records | Knows original versus replica, has an inaccurate condition report under pressure, and takes a protective action that also changes access to a room | His storage objection is real and the replica is harmless. Professional objection can coexist with a private theft; no invented “therefore could not have done it” |

None needs to confess to a second murder scheme. These actions can each be a route into one actual murder, but in the innocent world each character pursued something else. Each alternative should have at least one surviving cost or unresolved lie after Method. Resolved misunderstandings are satisfying; complete moral clearance is unnecessary.

These cases alone will not produce ten people above five after Act II in a thirty-person test. The remaining suspicion must come from consequential disputes clustered around the same deadline: threatened ownership claims, stolen speech cards, an interrupted press release, an altered authorization, a canceled effect, a held invoice. Give players reasons to wonder whether the visible action was the whole action. Do not distribute a dozen identical bottle touches merely to broaden scores.

## A workable culprit account, and its limits

The selected guest should have an ordinary role-specific reception errand in Act II, shared in kind with several innocent errands. The guilty version should not add a whole extra forensic paragraph. It can contain a small false explanation of an action already being discussed: why an envelope was retrieved, why papers were discarded, why the person returned after finishing their stated business. Innocents must also be capable of evasions, incomplete recollections, and consequential withheld facts.

If retaining the fracture as an attribution aid, postpone the **broken-gift explanation** to Method questioning, before the fracture result. A practical example: when confronted with the recovered material, the guest insists they discarded the contents of their own damaged favor, and describes a damage feature inconsistent with the intact contaminated miniature. Their Act II errand independently places them at reception during the exposed-glass interval. The final comparison identifies the shard as the stolen source. The inference is that the guest supplied a false innocent origin for material they handled at the murder scene.

That is stronger than round 16 and later than round 17. It also remains contestable: the guest might have swept up someone else's fragment or had a different damaged gift; the poisoned miniature is not individually labeled; a second person could have had the glass. The culprit's explanation needs a reason to arise in the conversation—defending their own discarded parcel, for example—not an unsolicited souvenir anecdote. Do not have police announce that the paper belonged to that guest unless the actual exhibit supports it.

This is still vulnerable to a guilty-only writing tell. If testing shows the late account produces automatic certainty, **stop using the fragment to identify a person**. Use it only to establish route, and distinguish the culprit through a role-specific contradiction about their return to reception and disposal, corroborated by another character's mundane observation. That observation may vary with the selected world's actual events; the physical exhibit must stay fixed. The corroborating speaker must be active in the chosen cast, and their printed claim must remain contestable. This alternative requires additional authored testimony; a fixed anonymous report cannot supply a personalized eyewitness without hidden selection-dependent evidence.

Do not pretend that this fallback already proves every culprit. Some role-specific accounts will be less persuasive than others. Every-culprit review is necessary before integration.

## Feasibility and remaining risks

- The requested balance is a useful playtest target, not a narratological guarantee. Ratings should record readers' judgments without prompts directing them to maintain ten suspects or reserve four alternatives. Suspicion ratings are not additive probabilities; if readers interpret “above five” as a greater-than-even probability of sole guilt, the numeric breadth requirement is incoherent.
- No ascertainable culprit before III is achievable by withholding the decisive interpretation and avoiding a unique early confession. It does not mean players may never guess correctly. An early correct guess based on personality is different from an earned, uniquely supported inference.
- A correct final leader plus credible alternatives is achievable as **best explanation with uncertainty**. A uniquely proven final culprit plus multiple equally supported alternatives is not. The reveal must acknowledge the actual evidential strength of the case.
- Fracture contact is not necessarily theft or poisoning. Anonymous common containers and common wrapping do not acquire ownership through confident prose.
- Thirty possible criminals with identical public evidence impose a real ceiling on how personalized objective proof can be. Role-specific lies can vary; physical cause cannot secretly change to rescue a weak selection.
- Grant's continuing reception after the source alarm remains a significant plausibility weakness. Give his pressure visible consequences and show the institutional mistake; avoid multiplying convenient outages.
- The outside fibers and plaster support contact, not the route into the glass. Do not invent a laboratory finding that proves “only innocent exterior handling” unless such a finding is actually justified by the fiction.
- Dialogue needs different conversational behavior, not merely different vocabulary. Brie delegates and demands orders; Sue limits the question; Cary answers briefly and corrects a physical mistake; Paige seeks a source; Penny bargains over a letter; Anne wants an immediate decision about staff. Only some should volunteer admissions. A third-act answer can worsen a relationship rather than supply a polished life lesson.

Round 18 should be accepted only if its readers independently explain why the leader's account is less credible than the surviving alternatives, and why those alternatives remain dangerous possibilities. Meeting the score bands through breadth instructions, retrospective interpretation, or cosmetic handling anecdotes would conceal the structural problem.
