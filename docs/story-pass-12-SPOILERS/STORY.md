# Pass 12: interrupted unveiling — SPOILERS

This is an isolated story prototype, not the published game. The three YAML files here are its editable source. Public invitations, character sheets, portraits, and production downloads are unchanged. Test inputs are frozen before the reviewers begin; later corrections require another version.

## The causal story

Grant wants the gala to ratify his control of the museum and announce his acquisition. He uses the prospect of funding to extract signatures, publicity, and concessions. His acquisition also exposes an older ownership dispute. Meanwhile the people making the exhibition and evening happen have concealed professional shortcuts, unpaid work, and lost credit.

A genuine mounting defect interrupts the unveiling. Guests wait in the Donor Salon while the crew checks the display. Grant tries to use the delay to finish the disputed agreements. Arguments about the collection, money, and the show overlap in one public confrontation. A catering payment dispute takes the service manager back to the kitchen, leaving Grant's presentation glass on the table.

The murderer has already taken poison during the earlier acquisition walkthrough. During the interrupted reception, the murderer contaminates Grant's empty glass. The glass is subsequently collected on a tray, filled from the shared punch, and served for Grant's toast. The punch and food are clear. His glass is not.

The bottle remains partly full, with a disturbed stopper and a split on the hidden side of its new custody band. This replaces the implausible unnoticed empty bottle. The collection belongs to Grant; the conservator has requested an empty replica and withheld exhibition approval. That conflict explains the dangerous original's presence, but does not excuse the safety failure.

## Three plots, not thirty unrelated visits

| Conflict | What Grant wants | What the guests conceal | What the confrontation produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| Museum control | Naming rights, signatures, favorable coverage, trustee support | Unauthorized money transfers, false endorsements, leaked negotiations, family pressure | Documents withdrawn, signatures refused, demands made in front of witnesses |
| Acquisition and ownership | A marketable provenance and permanent gift | Missing permits, softened opinions, incomplete accession records, covert purchases, substitutions | Competing documents and ownership claims that may explain apparent destruction or concealment |
| Exhibition and gala production | A flawless announcement credited to him | Unsafe approvals, unpaid labor, erased credits, an unapproved blackout and canceled performance | A real closure, a halted show, a service interruption, and several legitimate objects near the glass |

Characters have individual actions within these conflicts. Their actions need not all happen consecutively; the confrontation is crowded and overlapping. Whether that overlap still feels artificially convenient is an explicit test question.

## Evidence release and what it can establish

The hunt comes before the formal investigation. All sixteen discoveries are shared with everyone; the host supplies any not found. They provide scandal context and introduce the acquisition, canceled show, camera order, mount problem, unpaid service, and donor glass. They are not sixteen independent instructions for solving the murder.

| Release | Observations | New question | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before Motive: F1 + F2 | Grant dies after the toast; cyanide from the acquisition bottle is implicated; its contents remain but stopper/band have been disturbed | Who has a reason to stop his announcement, and how did the poison become available? | Motive and hazardous access do not identify a killer |
| Motive statements | Individual threats, bargains, and concealed scandals | Which grievances could drive murder, and which may account for other wrongdoing? | A serious scandal is not evidence of poisoning |
| Before Opportunity: F3 | The glass is unattended between its placement and collection; mounting delay and kitchen dispute explain the room's activity | Who could encounter the glass during the interrupted reception? | Presence is shared; the camera gap is not a unique culprit trait |
| Opportunity statements | Each character admits a distinct act during the confrontation | What was each person actually trying to achieve? | Several acts remain suspicious until Method; no required innocent continuous alibi |
| Before Method: F4 + F5 | Condition record and walkthrough establish source opportunity; donor glass has a star-marked base; rehearsal glass is marked PROP and tests clear; food/punch clear; foil construction is common stock | Which accounts connect source, actual glass, and physical findings? | Common material is not a unique tear match. Records explaining misconduct do not automatically prove innocence |
| Method statements | Innocent explanations disclose the purpose and context of earlier misconduct; selected murderer account connects source access and actual glass contact | Which combined account best supports the murder? | The inference must be supported by what players hear, not clearance metadata or a confession withheld until after voting |

The replica rehearsal glass and donor glass are separate objects. This distinction must remain visually obvious if adopted. Production will need a prop-glass photograph/diagram and a donor-base detail; text-only trial results cannot certify those visuals.

## Test protocol and release gate

Three independent reviewers start without game history, solutions, source YAML, or knowledge of the selected culprit. Trial A uses all thirty characters; B and C use the confirmed twenty-two. Culprits are randomly selected without replacement within declared role groups, not sampled uniformly from all thirty. Each reviewer saves scores before receiving the next of eight releases. Every trial is retained.

Desired results: no uniquely established murderer before Method; at least ten of thirty or eight of twenty-two above 5 after Opportunity; selected culprit leads after Method with several credible alternatives; concrete causal plausibility and distinct voices. These are review targets, not instructions to reviewers to produce particular scores. Expert transcript scores do not estimate a human party's solve rate.

This prototype rewrites all thirty Opportunity accounts and fifteen innocent Method accounts, with selected guilty Method corrections. It retains much of Motive and Coming Clean. Before publication, every ending and question must be reconciled with the revised events, all thirty culprit paths audited, the actual visual exhibits tested, and PDFs rendered and inspected. No claim of finished party readiness is made by passing an authored metadata check.
