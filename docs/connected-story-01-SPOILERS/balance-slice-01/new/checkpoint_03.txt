Question for Monet Bags, Sue Venir, Brie DeVivre: What would accepting Grant's gift change for you?

Monet Bags
The donation is large enough to cover payroll. That's the attraction. It also gives Grant a say in what we buy, what we build and who runs the place. Artie calls it a rescue. I keep asking whether the rescuer gets a vote on every paycheck. We need the money. That doesn't make these terms affordable.

Sue Venir
A draft is a useful place to argue. An announcement from a microphone is a useful place to pretend the argument is over. Grant's agreement reserved control of future purchases to him. I had advised the museum on the acquisition behind this exhibition. If the ownership file fails, my advice will be read with it. I wanted the board to decide what it was accepting before anyone called this a rescue.

Brie DeVivre
Grant asked for another cheese course after we'd agreed the menu. The museum hadn't approved the charge, and he hadn't paid it. We had already bought the food. Monet wanted the bill stopped; I wanted someone to tell me where the money was coming from. The private toast was on the original order. I couldn't settle the museum's gift agreement, but I could insist that feeding the donor was still a bill.

Question for Hugh Bidder, Paige Turner: What business with Grant had become a problem for you?

Hugh Bidder
Grant wanted his money back on the Star Bowl. The certificate was false before it came to me, but my firm's letterhead carried it to the buyer. I changed our file instead of admitting we'd been fooled. My settlement offer wasn't enough. Other clients wanted to know which other purchases he ought to worry about; The auction house was delighted to circulate that question. Grant wanted the refund and my admission. That combination would finish my business.

Paige Turner
The paper was going to call the gift a rescue. The retained property records and the ownership file gave me a different story. Grant wanted the source behind it. I had published one sentence I couldn't support, and his lawyer had every right to demand a correction. That didn't make the rest of the report false. I wanted the correction in print and the underlying records on the editor's desk before the gala announcement swallowed both.

Question for Artie Ficial, Chip Patina, Dada DiCapo, Tess Tament, Barb Dwyer: What did Grant want from you, and what would agreeing cost you?

Artie Ficial
Grant wanted his name above the door and his opinion inside every department. I built this museum by asking rich people for money. I know what a thank-you costs. This one cost the board its authority. Monet needed the check; Sue needed an agreement we could actually sign. I needed a museum left to direct.

Chip Patina
Grant wanted a recent repair called an ancient feature. There is a difference. One requires a conservator; the other requires a very cooperative liar. I refused the wording, after having made myself rather vulnerable by changing a treatment date on the insurance copy. I was trying to get the object covered. That does not improve what I did to the paperwork.

Dada DiCapo
Grant commissioned a performance, then decided the artist was an unfortunate extra. He wanted my name off the program and his name on everything else. My piece was called Donor Collapse. An empty dinner jacket falls out of a gilded chair. Apparently a donor can own satire, provided it never resembles him.

Tess Tament
Grant calls it the family gift when he wants applause and his gift when anyone asks a question. I wanted a vote on the thing bearing my family name. He thought I could appear in the photograph and inherit the explanation. I had already asked Sue to read the succession clause. Glamour is much cheaper when you know what you are agreeing to finance.

Barb Dwyer
Grant wanted privacy without hearing what privacy costs security. The museum wanted his money without hearing the same thing. I agreed to an exception and entered it as an equipment problem. That was my decision. It kept a donor request out of the ordinary log and kept my department looking competent. Those two conveniences are not looking particularly good tonight.

Question for Claire O’Scuro, Dr. Art E. Fact, Anya Tique, Reed DeLabel, Robin Banks: What did you want changed about the museum's account of Grant's collection?

Claire O’Scuro
I signed off on the bowl label before the ownership file was complete. Grant wanted my department's signature to do the work of a missing document. I replaced the label with a statement that ownership remained disputed. That makes the curator look careless before it makes the lender look dishonest. I knew that when I changed it. I prefer an embarrassing label to an indefensible one.

Dr. Art E. Fact
My name is on the catalog. The footnotes are also on the catalog, which is where the trouble begins. I cited a sale description as though it settled the bowl's ownership. Reed found the gap. Grant wanted the authority of my name without the inconvenience of my correction. I asked for the press copies to be held. A professor can survive a correction. He need not enjoy one.

Anya Tique
Grant wanted the founding story polished until it reflected him. The old memo says we sometimes accepted lenders' descriptions before the papers caught up. I wrote it. It is not the method I would recommend now. I took that memo out of the display file because I did not want our worst shortcut framed as his latest innovation. Apparently vanity survives the founding of an institution.

Reed DeLabel
I found a gap in my supervisor's ownership citation and corrected the catalog without waiting for his approval. That is not the safest way to finish a doctorate. Grant wanted the old wording kept because it sounded settled. I wanted the missing document acknowledged. I also wanted my correction credited, which is the less heroic part I tend to leave out when telling this story.

Robin Banks
Grant wanted disputed ownership turned into a generous gift. I wanted the claimants heard before he received applause. My petition was not an invitation to discuss better lighting around the bowl. It asked for the ownership claim to be taken down. I told the museum I would disrupt the announcement if they refused. I was perfectly serious about that threat, and perfectly aware that security disliked it.
