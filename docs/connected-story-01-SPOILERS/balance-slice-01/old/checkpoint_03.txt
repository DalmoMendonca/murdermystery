Question for Artie Ficial, Monet Bags, Tess Tament: What was at stake in Grant's proposed deal?

Artie Ficial
Grant attached my resignation to his gift. I had accepted a job at another museum and had not told the board. He planned to present my departure as their price for keeping the collection. I wanted the chair to hear the terms before he announced them.

Monet Bags
I moved restricted money without authorization. Payroll would have failed before the donation arrived. Grant discovered the transfer and demanded his wing expenses added to it. He could report me for the first transaction while benefiting from the second. I knew what that report would cost.

Tess Tament
Grant called me ornamental. Then he demanded my signature on the family trust. Apparently ornaments can execute legal documents. I organized the trustees against him. The money was supposed to support people, and his new plan mostly supported a very expensive sign saying GRANT.

Question for Claire O’Scuro, Chip Patina, Sue Venir: What did Grant want you to approve?

Claire O’Scuro
I concealed the independent review from the board. The Star Bowl label now presents a disputed history as settled ownership. That label goes out under my department’s name. Grant supplied the money and expected that to make him the curator. I had spent months building this exhibition; I was angry enough to change its wording without permission.

Chip Patina
I changed a treatment date to meet an insurance deadline. That mistake was mine. Grant wanted a recent repair described as ancient, so that damage would become part of the object’s pedigree. I could correct the date. Once my report went into the exhibition file, people would quote it for years. I refused to give him that report.

Sue Venir
I softened the legal opinion and failed to correct Grant when he called it clearance. Both versions still identify a problem with the title. He wanted my name in the announcement and my qualifications left out. I had let the account matter more than the advice. Hearing him use that compromise as my endorsement was unbearable.

Question for Hugh Bidder: What business did you have with Grant?

Hugh Bidder
I altered my file after discovering the certificate was false. Grant wanted millions back and a public admission that I had created the forgery. He knew that wasn't true. He preferred an expensive villain to a complicated transaction, especially when he could make the villain pay twice.

Question for Dada DiCapo: How had Grant interfered with your work?

Dada DiCapo
Yes, I wrote the warning. Returning what was taken meant my performance concept, which Grant planned to unveil with himself credited as visionary patron. It was a provocation. He turned my work into an advertisement and then complained that the artist had supplied an uncomfortable caption.

Question for Dr. Art E. Fact, Reed DeLabel: Why did Grant object to what you had written?

Dr. Art E. Fact
One inference in my article goes beyond the source. I had sent a correction to the archivist before coming here. Grant wanted the entire article withdrawn. He spoke as though finding one bad sentence made him the better historian. I was embarrassed by the sentence and furious at being asked to abandon the evidence around it.

Reed DeLabel
I copied a reference too quickly and concealed what the original meant for Art’s research. Correcting it would embarrass him as well as me. Grant wanted the old attribution restored because it suited the exhibition. I was expected to take responsibility for the careless note and then keep repeating it. I wanted somebody to read the source before deciding what my work was worth.

Question for Barb Dwyer, Paige Turner: What was Grant trying to keep private?

Barb Dwyer
I ordered the camera disconnected. Grant authorized it; the security procedure did not. He wanted privacy and intended to blame my staff if something went wrong. I followed his order and concealed the exception. The officer giving that instruction was me.

Paige Turner
Grant threatened the report, my source and the paper's ability to publish. I had made a factual error he could use. I corrected that sentence and refused the name he wanted. The person who trusted me was not a term I could trade in a settlement.

Question for Robin Banks: What claim did you want the museum to recognize?

Robin Banks
The stickers were mine. PROVENANCE IS A VERB fits nicely in a brochure that treats ownership as a permanent noun. Grant had me removed from an earlier event and threatened legal action tonight. I came to make the collection's history visible. He wanted me visible only on a security notice.

Question for Anya Tique: Why was your past business with Grant a problem tonight?

Anya Tique
In 1989 I accepted Grant’s donation with papers still missing. The museum needed money, and I believed his promise that they would follow. I had built part of this institution on that promise. Tonight he wanted my old signature treated as permission to do it again. I was ashamed of the first decision and angry at what he thought it entitled him to.

Question for Brie DeVivre: What part of Grant's gala plans did you object to?

Brie DeVivre
Grant refused my invoice, then ordered a new private service. He and his assistant gave opposite instructions. I changed the setup twice and billed the changes. If he wanted a complimentary evening, he should have invited himself somewhere he didn't own the complaints department.
