## Old reader observations

- Act I statements: Distinct professional grievances are coherent, and Artie's alternate job usefully complicates the obvious dismissal motive. Almost everyone confesses a precise ethical failing in polished explanatory paragraphs; that uniform candor feels like game exposition more than spontaneous questioning. Some references to warnings or stickers arrive before their physical discovery, which makes the checkpoint feel excerpted.
- Act II statements: Brie's return for her invoice concretely explains glass handling. Chip's voluntary stock disclosure feels anticipatory, although relevant to conservation work. Dada's unexplained staged donor collapse is strikingly suspicious and confusing: a living donor cannot simply collapse on command without an effect or cooperation. Nearly every guest escalates an earlier compromise through another polished confession, making the interview pattern conspicuous.
- Act III statements: The concrete paperwork and hinged-jacket explanation improve coherence, and late entrance records provide meaningful exclusions. Hugh's unexplained scrap feels designed to nominate him, but the shared-stock caveat prevents forensic certainty. Final interviews repeatedly package misconduct with an exculpatory retained document, making the structure mechanical. Missing exact preview attendance, salon movements, doorway-camera observations, and the transfer mechanism leave a substantial gap if the intended ending requires deductive uniqueness.

## New reader observations

- Act I statements: Distinctive voices and the interconnected ownership dispute feel coherent. Nearly everyone volunteers a damaging admission in carefully calibrated language, which feels like a structured clue round more than spontaneous questioning. Hugh's ruinous exposure is materially stronger than the caterer's unpaid course or the artist's credit dispute. Printed self-reports may be evasive or false.
- Act II statements: The individual acts produce useful overlapping opportunity instead of one obvious culprit. Nearly every guest again offers a polished confession and an exact relevance to paperwork or access, making the questioning feel mechanically complete. Original contents versus inert liquid requires careful tracking across seller, loan, catalog, and press records; a clear chronological record could reduce confusion. Anya's old spare key is plausible but conveniently broadens cupboard access.
- Act III statements: Hugh's abrupt cancellation-and-substitution claim conflicts sharply with the seller and makes the intended accusation conspicuous. It is plausible commercial damage control but cannot logically identify a killer without an unstated truthfulness convention. The other fourteen answers repeatedly distinguish embarrassing misconduct from the murder mechanism, sounding like defense summaries prepared for a clue audit. Restricted payroll misuse and Barb's knowingly excessive camera shutdown leave substantial alternative motives and concealment. The overlapping glass visits and shared source/foil access prevent honest uniqueness. A corroborated action tying one guest to the poison transfer would make the resolution stronger than a single false transaction claim.
