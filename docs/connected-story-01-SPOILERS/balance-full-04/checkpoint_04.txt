Question for Artie Ficial, Monet Bags, Sue Venir, Tess Tament: What would Grant's terms change for the museum or your family?

Artie Ficial
His name over the door, his vote in every department, and my resignation as the finishing touch. It is an impressive thank-you note to demand before writing a check. Monet sees wages we could pay. Sue sees terms we cannot sign. I have spent years asking wealthy people to support this museum. I would like there to be a museum left when one finally agrees.

Monet Bags
I keep hearing "rescue." The staff still expect their wages on Friday. Grant's check could cover them. His terms would also let him question every purchase and every transfer. Artie dislikes losing authority; I dislike being asked to make a budget add up after someone else has announced it. We need money. That is why I have been reading the small print instead of applauding the amount.

Sue Venir
An agreement is not accepted because somebody finds a microphone. Grant intended to announce his gift before the board had settled its terms. Future acquisitions would need his approval. The ownership file behind this exhibition was already troublesome, and I had advised on that acquisition. I wanted a decision on paper, with the objections still attached. Applause is a poor substitute for a board vote.

Tess Tament
"The family gift" when the photographer arrives. "My gift" when the family asks a question. I wanted a say in the thing that would carry our name. Grant wanted me standing beside it looking expensive and agreeable. I asked Sue to read the succession clause before I agreed to the photograph. People assume an heiress can afford to ignore details. I find the details become considerably more interesting when they concern my chair.

Question for Claire O’Scuro, Chip Patina, Dr. Art E. Fact, Anya Tique, Reed DeLabel: What problem in the collection work could Grant use against you?

Claire O’Scuro
A missing ownership document doesn't become less missing beside a generous donation. Grant objected to the revised bowl label. He preferred my earlier approval, when I had let the file proceed without it. I am responsible for that label, including the version I would rather you didn't frame. Tonight's exhibition was meant to show the collection. It wasn't meant to settle a claim by putting a curator's name beneath it.

Chip Patina
He asked me to call a recent repair an ancient feature. That changes the sales pitch, not the object. I would have been a very useful conservator if I agreed: my name, his preferred description. We already had an argument over treatment paperwork. Then the entrance mount began moving, and somehow I became the person ruining the atmosphere by noticing things were not safely attached.

Dr. Art E. Fact
Reed found a gap in my ownership citation. Grant found the gap inconvenient. Those are not the same scholarly judgment. I asked for the earlier press copies to be held. Grant wanted the authority of my name on the catalog without the correction under it. One can survive an error in print. The donors needn't be handed another thousand copies while one is attempting to correct it.

Anya Tique
I recognize the founding story. I was there. Some of the convenient omissions are also familiar. Our old memo allowed lender descriptions while supporting papers caught up. I wrote it; I wouldn't recommend it now. Grant wanted that shortcut polished into a precedent for his gift. I took the memo out of the display file. I did not help found a museum so a new donor could adopt its worst habit as a tradition.

Reed DeLabel
The missing document was a footnote until Grant read it. Then it became an attack on the gift. I corrected the bowl entry without waiting for his approval. He wanted the old settled ownership language kept; I wanted the gap acknowledged. I also wanted the correction credited to me. That is less noble than rescuing scholarship, but considerably more relevant when one is trying to finish a doctorate.

Question for Hugh Bidder, Vincent Van Faux, Elon Mosaic, Saul D. Bid, Ella Gance, Penny Pincher: What dispute did your dealings with Grant leave you hiding?

Hugh Bidder
A refund is a number. "Admit the certificate was false" is a sentence that follows a dealer for the rest of his career. Grant wanted both over the Star Bowl. My firm had passed the certificate to him. Elon began asking which other purchases he should worry about; Saul was delighted to help. I changed our file instead of admitting we'd been fooled. Vincent Van Faux
That early painting was the beginning of my rent payments. Grant kept introducing it as the beginning of my genius. Elon Mosaic
It was a trial of a museum platform, not a subscription to control the museum. Grant began treating the proposal as though it came with the collection and the customer list. Monet asked what it actually cost. Reasonable question. Then Hugh's disputed purchase appeared in the collector photograph. I asked to leave it out. A launch is complicated enough before somebody hangs an ownership dispute beside the demonstration.

Saul D. Bid
My house's name is not a certificate for every object Hugh prices. Grant wanted an auctioneer's authority beside a private dealer's bargain. I removed that comparison from our handout. Ella Gance
I bought a picture, not a place in Grant's endorsement campaign. The disputed purchase was to disappear from the society crop; every collector left in it would apparently bless the rescue. Penny Pincher
Nobody enjoys hearing the price they paid discussed beside the price they told their relatives. I bought out the family's shares using a damaged example as the comparison. Our piece was intact. Then I paid a dealer a great deal more for another work. Grant had both figures and wanted the story in the handout. I removed the sheet. I wanted to speak to my relatives before a donor explained my arithmetic over dinner.
