## Reader feedback

### Reader A

Strongest suspect: Hugh Bidder, 9/10, because his final neutralization/substitution story directly contradicts seller fulfillment and laboratory findings. Remaining alternatives: Chip Patina (seal material, false date, bottle handling), Brie DeVivre (individual drink flavoring and direct glass access), Barb Dwyer (intentional recording blackout), with Anya/Tess/Dada/Minnie having some access but less incriminating late evidence. No premature obvious identification in stages 1-7; stage 8 supplies the first dominant unique contradiction. At stage 7 Chip led without a decisive case. Thirty voices have useful occupational details but closely repeated arcs: donor overreach, concealed compromise, document withdrawal, then correction/settlement and embarrassed accountability. Numerous print/image misreadings become mechanically repeated. The bottle-to-glass chain lacks a unique material match or direct movement witness, leaving Hugh's motive/access case less fully proved than his decisive lie. Protocol deviation: stage 1 disk write failed, checkpoint 2 was read before successful corrected write, but stage 1 scores had already been fixed in memory and were not revised; every later stage was saved before reading the next.

Midpoint: Second statements advance real logistics and secrets, but almost every character altered, withheld, clipped, moved or replaced some document/image. This creates a repetitive correction-and-confession rhythm. The density of suspicious benign errands to exactly the bottle or toast place feels arranged. Brie announcing an ingredient visit is striking but can fairly be innocent drink preparation.

Final: Final round has one dramatic falsification surrounded by near-universal documentary self-correction. Hugh's changed account is conspicuous even without detailed time reconstruction. Character voices still differ, but repeated read-the-column/look-at-the-picture clarifications and ethical admissions make many speakers function as exhibit interpreters. The night has too many neatly resolved financial/publicity scandals. Murder method is plausible, while possession of historical active poison on an open museum bench despite warnings strains institutional practice.

### Reader B

Strongest suspect: Hugh Bidder, 9/10. First obvious-candidate stage is 8; no earlier checkpoint singled one person out convincingly. His canceled-condition/inert-substitution claim contradicts the seller's explicit no-cancellation fulfillment, original-content purchase and actual poison. This is a persuasive identity signal by elimination of explanations, but it does not itself prove he handled the bottle and unattended glass during the window. Remaining alternatives: Brie DeVivre, Anya Tique, Chip Patina and Barb Dwyer (6 each), with direct glass opportunity, actual cupboard access, band expertise and concealed surveillance interruption respectively. Clean flavoring is not an alibi against adding poison separately; multiple staff keys broaden cupboard access. The setting and interlocking money/ownership/service conflicts are coherent, and some occupational voices are memorable. Distinctness suffers because most of 30 people confess a hidden professional compromise, remove or revise a document, then repair the record and make a witty rueful defense. Too many simultaneous donor coercions and almost universal completed corrections make the ensemble feel procedurally arranged. Dates are internally confusing: an October 29 pre-gala proof accompanies an October 30 treatment sheet without a plainly stated gala date; I treated those as supplied story dates rather than importing today's date. Evidence is thorough on excluding proposed mechanisms, substantially thinner on who physically exploited the confirmed mechanism. Hugh is legible at the intended final round, though a reader demanding affirmative opportunity evidence could reasonably retain the alternatives.

Midpoint: Acts integrate useful admissions and revised behavior, but the same structure recurs across the ensemble: I removed/revised/withdrew a document, here is my embarrassing error, here is a limiting explanation. The bottle preview suddenly acquires a very large attendance list. Brie's flavoring stands out because it is unusually direct physical action among repetitive paperwork confessions; this may make her conspicuous even if innocent. Distinct vocabulary survives the common structure.

Final: Hugh's late factual contradiction is striking and easy to detect beside the seller document. His 'Stop treating two purchases as one transaction' defense is plausible bluster, but sits awkwardly alongside an explicit claim disproved by a supplied receipt. Other final speeches mostly retell the exhibits with admissions, regrets and corrected paperwork. That creates a readable puzzle-resolution exercise but very little new interpersonal movement. Grant's death rarely disrupts the uniformly polished voices beyond an occasional pointed reference.

### Reader C

Strongest suspect: Hugh Bidder (9/10), identified only at stage 8 by his canceled-condition/substitution claim contradicting the seller record and toxicology. No premature unique identification in stages 1–7: Artie/Brie initially, Hugh's blackmail motive in stage 4, and Chip's physical seal access in stage 7 each led without closing alternatives. Remaining alternatives: Chip (5), Brie (4), Monet/Dr. Art/Anya/Barb (4), principally because neither a staff-only bottle-access chain nor a unique glass-contamination act is demonstrated for Hugh. The final evidence makes his lie conspicuous and likely intentional, but a non-murder commercial concealment explanation cannot be logically excluded. Largest plausibility/distinctness problems: thirty equally articulate defensive voices; almost universal donor blackmail; mechanically repeated document correction, fee refund and retained-original arcs; a long packet systematically ruling out peripheral theories; and underexplained access controls on a known potentially toxic loan. The strongest actual mystery mechanism is unattended individual glass plus authentic poisoned loan, but the final narrowing depends more on one contradictory speech than forensic opportunity.

Midpoint: Evidence-linked movements broaden the field coherently, but nearly every speaker admits a prior ethical lapse and corrects, returns, retains or withdraws a document. Same guarded-but-eloquent cadence persists. Culinary flavoring is a conspicuously euphemistic item until its contents are identified. The actual service manager remains an unnamed but crucial timeline witness.

Final: The final round largely recites the exhibits and settles everyone's side misconduct. Hugh's statement abruptly supplies the one direct false factual assertion against an explicit document, making the elimination structure very obvious. Distinct jobs survive, but voices and three-step confession/correction/defense arcs repeat. People unnaturally volunteer complete interpretive exonerations instead of being asked focused timeline questions.
