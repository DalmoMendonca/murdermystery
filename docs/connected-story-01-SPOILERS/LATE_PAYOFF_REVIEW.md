The diagnostic's qualitative criticism is supported by the text. Most innocent answers explain why a suspicious act had an ordinary purpose, but still admit the same source knowledge, room access or concealment useful to a poisoner. Physical exclusion and deductive certainty are not required: a useful payoff should defeat or materially weaken the particular criminal explanation that made an action suspicious. A character's willingness to confess another wrong is especially weak evidence of innocence. Their guilty counterpart can have the same respectable motive and commit the murder as well.
