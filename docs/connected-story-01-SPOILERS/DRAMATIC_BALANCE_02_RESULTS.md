## Writing and deduction findings

Concrete emotional details worked: Artie's hosting breaks down, Al calls home, Tess wants an argument Grant can no longer answer, Brie faces crying staff, and Dada refuses to turn trauma into art. Readers preferred these to evidence summaries.

However, vocabulary variation has not eliminated common dramatic shapes: denial, admission, correction, colleague address and a tidy pledge. Too many speeches remain articulate and therapeutically self-aware under pressure. One false categorical account among otherwise corrected accounts still exposes the construction. Reader ratings of voice distinction range5-7, not a strong pass.

Sue's guilty statement that the manager poured at the private place is ambiguous: it can refer to the newly witnessed flavoring, rather than the later shared punch. Reader B correctly selected Sue but found this insufficiently decisive. False cover stories support the intended accusation more strongly than the physical action itself. Preview/source access also remains insufficiently established for some selected culprits before confession. Innocent counterfacts often defeat one accusation without excluding every possible route.

Mona's ending still assumes Sue can accompany her to claimants even when Sue confesses. Poison exhibit access remains theatrically plausible but weakly secured. These are concrete unresolved issues, not reasons to manufacture synchronized alibis or repeated bottle handling.
