Question for Monet Bags, Sue Venir, Brie DeVivre: What did you do about the disputed gala order?

Monet Bags
I stopped payment for the extra cheese course. Grant ordered it; the museum did not. The manager brought me the ticket, and I crossed out our account. Then I went looking for Grant to get him to accept the charge. He wasn't at his private place, so I left the invoice there. I'd already borrowed from a restricted fund to cover payroll. I wasn't putting his dinner on it too.

Sue Venir
My note stopped the public celebration of an accepted gift. It did not stop serving Grant a private drink. Those are different instructions. I found the unsigned agreement at his place and took it out of the announcement papers. I kept the marked running order too. Artie was trying to keep tonight moving; I was trying to keep a draft from becoming tomorrow's statement of what the board had agreed to. No one asked me to remove the agreement. I did it anyway.

Brie DeVivre
The manager came back about the held charge. I showed the original order: extra course disputed, private toast still booked. Then I went to Grant's place with the culinary flavoring for that drink. It wasn't a new request. I left the ingredient container with the service things and came back to the kitchen. I had kept the added course on our purchase list without a funded authorization. That was my problem to fix, and I was not going to pretend the food hadn't cost us anything.

Question for Hugh Bidder, Paige Turner: What did you conceal or take back during the reception?

Hugh Bidder
I kept the seller's correspondence out of the auction handouts. It covered the bowl and the Velvet Widow purchase I'd arranged for Grant. There were two descriptions of the liquid: the seller's and Grant's. I sent the loan description to the people writing the display copy. The other one stayed in my file. Grant had already demanded a refund on one purchase; I didn't want donors asking what else I'd sold him. The handouts weren't going to carry every argument in that file.

Paige Turner
I took my recorder out of Grant's coat in the cloakroom. He had kept it after our interview. I copied the file before anyone could demand it back. I gave the press desk a short export, with the part that could identify my source removed. That was not the original recording. I told the editor what I'd cut and kept the untrimmed file. I wanted the reporting checked. I also wanted the recorder back, and I didn't ask permission to search his pocket.

Question for Artie Ficial, Dada DiCapo, Tess Tament, Barb Dwyer: What did you change about the gala, and why did you go into Grant's private room?

Artie Ficial
I took the acceptance announcement out of the running order. Then I went to the private room to tell Grant we were not going to surprise the board with a done deal. His place was set; he was elsewhere. I left a folded note beside the agreement. I also told intake to keep the bottle out of the public exhibition. The gala could continue. The acquisition could wait.

Dada DiCapo
I tried to keep the collapse cue in the running order after it was rejected. I went into the private room looking for the gala coordinator; Grant's place was already set. I wanted the screen switched on during his toast, not another speech about his generosity. I had the film ready on my phone. I showed the coordinator the jacket falling. She was less delighted than an artist hopes.

Tess Tament
I ordered the gift glass through the museum shop and asked for a quiet private toast instead of the podium. I wanted Grant to hear me before he announced the deal as settled. I went there during the reception. His glass was waiting; he was not. I left the revised seating card and went looking for Sue. Yes, I arranged privacy for the man I was furious with. A family argument is not improved by a microphone.

Barb Dwyer
I ordered the technician to take the private-room camera off the live monitor. I also kept the outside camera crew out while the added-course bill was being sorted out. Museum staff and caterers still had work to do. I checked the room myself. There was a place set for Grant and nobody sitting there. The log calls the camera a fault. It was not a fault. I had decided to carry out the request instead of recording that I should have refused it.

Question for Chip Patina: What did you do with the incoming objects or their records during the reception?

Chip Patina
I applied the red closure band before the preview. That was my work, not an old museum seal. I checked the closed bottle and retained the condition sheet. Later I went to the installation alcove because the mount was moving. The installation team wanted a grand opening; I wanted the object not to land on someone's shoe. I left the treatment folder at the workbench. The false date was still in it.

Question for Claire O’Scuro, Dr. Art E. Fact, Anya Tique, Reed DeLabel, Robin Banks: What did you remove, keep or change, and what access did that give you?

Claire O’Scuro
I removed the ownership label, not the bowl. Reed kept the old wording. I also moved the bottle's incoming papers out of the public display file because its exhibition approval was still on hold. Both files were on the intake desk. I had authority over the labels and the display paperwork. I did not have authority to settle the missing ownership claim by pretending it had disappeared.

Dr. Art E. Fact
I asked Reed to hold the earlier press version and return the purchase attachment to the catalog file. It covered the bottle, not the bowl. My draft repeated the inert loan description; the attachment required original contents. I left both descriptions in the working file and put a query beside them. I was at the preview to see the objects, not merely to admire my own introduction.

Anya Tique
I took the founding memo from the loan-display papers and returned it to the preparation cupboard after the preview. I used the old spare key I still keep. The bottle had been put away in there. I saw it; I did not open it. Nobody had asked me to restore the file. I decided that putting it back in its proper place was preferable to leaving my signature on Grant's proposed exhibit.

Reed DeLabel
I kept both bowl labels. I also prepared the bottle's press entry from the working catalog. I shortened it, including the unresolved original-contents clause. The result repeated the inert description without its warning. That copy went to the press desk. I kept the full working file because I was still trying to get the purchase attachment checked. I should not have sent the short version.

Robin Banks
I distributed the petition after security warned me to stop. At intake I removed the disputed ownership card from the display papers and left it inside the petition. I then interrupted Paige's interview to give Grant his copy. I wanted the press to hear him answer. I had seen the bottle at the preview too, but my ownership demand concerned the bowl. Buying a collection does not end its history.
