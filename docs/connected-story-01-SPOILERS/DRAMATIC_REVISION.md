## Protecting the mystery

The current staged evidence is byte-identical to frozen full04. The named round questions and recorded interview opening also match. Introductions and hunt remain unchanged. Only the spoken rounds and post-vote endings change. In particular, Hugh's substitution assertion stays in Act III, not the hunt or an early speech.

The table in `dramatic-fact-contracts.yaml` records the retained branch discriminators for all thirty routes. It is an author review ledger, not text for players. Testimony provides the observation or claim; guests compare it with evidence. Instructions such as look at the column, compare these exhibits, test my container, and therefore I am cleared are removed from act readings. After voting, Coming Clean may explicitly explain the crime.

The corrections, refunds and settlements already in the exhibits have not been secretly deleted. Their existence is part of the tested mystery. Their emotional importance has changed: a paid difference does not restore trust, a label does not repair a mentorship, a disclosed commission does not guarantee the claimants retain their advocate. Unresolved consequences replace blanket redemption. Selected murderer confessions keep their original crime admissions and clue explanations, then end in the character's own consequence.

## Checks and limits

Coverage/reference checks pass for all thirty roles, twenty-two confirmed roles, thirty single-omission variants, thirty single-role variants and four recorded-opening variants. All184 locked public files match. All240 speeches differ from the baseline. Longest act reading is72 words; longest ending is117. These are text lengths, not a claim that a final PDF has been typeset or visually reviewed.

The clue assertions have been compared as an authoring review. Lexical guards can catch accidental removal of key claims, but cannot prove semantic equivalence, balance, plausibility or quality. New personal stakes and different emphasis may influence suspicion even with identical facts. No claim of unchanged or improved ratings is justified until a fresh staged read is measured.

## Next playtest protocol

Use the same thirty-character Hugh world and eight-stage order as full04 for comparability. Keep exact scores with each reader, rather than optimizing an average. Test whether the final leader and late identification survive; whether credible innocent suspects remain after Act II and at the end; and whether a less engineered story actually results. Do not require numerical identity between stochastic readers.

Ask for qualitative feedback only after all staged scores are saved. Ask readers to identify specific speakers or passages, not just say the prose is better:

- Which voices could they distinguish without seeing a name? Which still sound alike?
- Which characters have the same arc, and what repeated sequence do they hear?
- Which movements feel like natural work or personal choices, and which feel placed for the puzzle?
- Which act speeches tell the player how to interpret an exhibit?
- Which reactions to the death seem real, exaggerated, interchangeable or absent?
- Which unresolved conflicts make them want to hear Coming Clean?

Then reveal the selected Coming Clean branch for all thirty characters as a separate post-vote narrative review. Lock accusation scores first. Ask whether the endings resolve the mystery, honor each person's earlier behavior, and avoid thirty versions of apology-and-refund. This distinguishes an unsatisfying confession from a deliberate unresolved conflict before voting. Do not use new finale facts to retroactively improve solve scores.

If balance deteriorates, locate the actual disclosure or changed emphasis before making another whole-cast rewrite. If story feedback still names the same repeated mechanism, reconsider that mechanism; adding more adjectives or quirks is not a repair. Production remains the existing complete fallback until a tested and visually reviewed replacement is ready.
