### Reader C

Al Baster is my final accusation because his explanation of the glass fragment contradicts the retained materials. The mystery rewards comparing evidence against testimony, and the poison route is understandable: dangerous original contents, a broken closure, and an unattended personal glass. His financial pressure and dependent at home make a credible motive, though his access to the cupboard and glass is less concretely placed than Brie’s, Tess’s or Barb’s. Show him making one ordinary delivery near both locations before the decisive denial. The climactic lie feels a little too obliging: most others honestly repeat documentary corrections while Al alone asserts a disproven material. Give another innocent an understandable mistaken interpretation, and let the paper-versus-leaf distinction require a small player inference. The personal admissions improve the ensemble substantially, especially Tess dwelling on buying the glass, Artie failing at hosting, and Brie protecting kitchen staff. However, nearly everyone has the same articulate rhythm: wrongdoing, explanation, colleague addressed by name, insightful emotional coda. Break this with evasions, interruptions, defensive jokes, brief answers and a character who cannot yet articulate regret. Several arcs resolve through credits, revised labels and restored disclosures rather than active relationships. Keep a few debts or disputes genuinely unsettled, and let one reconciliation fail. The late evidence packet is comprehensive but dense; fewer administrative resolutions and more exchanges prompted by questions would give players greater ownership.
