Question for Artie Ficial, Monet Bags, Sue Venir, Tess Tament: What would Grant's terms change for the museum or your family?

Artie Ficial
Grant wanted his name above the door and his opinion inside every department. I built this museum by asking rich people for money. I know what a thank-you costs. This one cost the board its authority. Monet needed the check; Sue needed an agreement we could actually sign. I needed a museum left to direct.

Monet Bags
The donation is large enough to cover payroll. That's the attraction. It also gives Grant a say in what we buy, what we build and who runs the place. Artie calls it a rescue. I keep asking whether the rescuer gets a vote on every paycheck. We need the money. That doesn't make these terms affordable.

Sue Venir
A draft is a useful place to argue. An announcement from a microphone is a useful place to pretend the argument is over. Grant's agreement reserved control of future purchases to him. I had advised the museum on the acquisition behind this exhibition. If the ownership file fails, my advice will be read with it. I wanted the board to decide what it was accepting before anyone called this a rescue.

Tess Tament
Grant calls it the family gift when he wants applause and his gift when anyone asks a question. I wanted a vote on the thing bearing my family name. He thought I could appear in the photograph and inherit the explanation. I had already asked Sue to read the succession clause. Glamour is much cheaper when you know what you are agreeing to finance.

Question for Claire O’Scuro, Chip Patina, Dr. Art E. Fact, Anya Tique, Reed DeLabel: What problem in the collection work could Grant use against you?

Claire O’Scuro
I signed off on the bowl label before the ownership file was complete. Grant wanted my department's signature to do the work of a missing document. I replaced the label with a statement that ownership remained disputed. That makes the curator look careless before it makes the lender look dishonest. I knew that when I changed it. I prefer an embarrassing label to an indefensible one.

Chip Patina
Grant wanted a recent repair called an ancient feature. There is a difference. One requires a conservator; the other requires a very cooperative liar. I refused the wording, after having made myself rather vulnerable by changing a treatment date on the insurance copy. I was trying to get the object covered. That does not improve what I did to the paperwork.

Dr. Art E. Fact
My name is on the catalog. The footnotes are also on the catalog, which is where the trouble begins. I cited a sale description as though it settled the bowl's ownership. Reed found the gap. Grant wanted the authority of my name without the inconvenience of my correction. I asked for the press copies to be held. A professor can survive a correction. He need not enjoy one.

Anya Tique
Grant wanted the founding story polished until it reflected him. The old memo says we sometimes accepted lenders' descriptions before the papers caught up. I wrote it. It is not the method I would recommend now. I took that memo out of the display file because I did not want our worst shortcut framed as his latest innovation. Apparently vanity survives the founding of an institution.

Reed DeLabel
I found a gap in my supervisor's ownership citation and corrected the catalog without waiting for his approval. That is not the safest way to finish a doctorate. Grant wanted the old wording kept because it sounded settled. I wanted the missing document acknowledged. I also wanted my correction credited, which is the less heroic part I tend to leave out when telling this story.

Question for Hugh Bidder, Vincent Van Faux, Elon Mosaic, Saul D. Bid, Ella Gance, Penny Pincher: What dispute did your dealings with Grant leave you hiding?

Hugh Bidder
Grant wanted his money back on the Star Bowl. The certificate was false before it came to me, but my firm's letterhead carried it to the buyer. I changed our file instead of admitting we'd been fooled. My settlement offer wasn't enough. Elon wanted to know which other purchases he ought to worry about; Saul was delighted to ask that question loudly. Grant wanted the refund and my admission. That combination would finish my business.

Vincent Van Faux
Grant bought an early painting and started calling it the beginning of my genius. It was the beginning of my rent payments. He commissioned a study of his historic bottle. The exhibition gives him both: my new commission and that old picture, with his name larger than mine. I withdrew permission to reproduce the commission in the donor handout. Hugh warned me that collectors dislike a difficult artist. Collectors also dislike a dead easy one.

Elon Mosaic
Grant agreed to fund a trial of my museum platform, then started treating the subscription proposal as a right to control the collection. Monet asked what the trial actually cost. Fair question. I had shown a demonstration as though the museum already had a working installation. Hugh's disputed sale was also in the collector photograph. I asked to leave that object out. A product launch with an ownership dispute is a difficult launch.

Saul D. Bid
Hugh's valuation of the Star Bowl was being passed around as though it were my house's guarantee. It was neither. I removed that comparison from our sale handout. Grant wanted a respectable auction name beside his collection and a private dealer's price when the bill arrived. One can be gracious about a rival. One need not underwrite him. I had already accepted a fee for advising on the sale, which made graciousness rather more difficult.

Ella Gance
I asked for my purchase to be left out of the society photograph. Vincent's picture was worth buying; the ownership story attached to another piece was not worth repeating. Grant wanted every collector in the frame to look like an endorsement of his gift. I had agreed to lend my name to the evening, and I withdrew that permission for the sale handout. A loan of a name can become considerably more expensive than a loan of a painting.

Penny Pincher
I bought out my relatives' shares in an old family object, using a damaged example as the price comparison. Our object was intact. They did not get the share they should have. Then I paid a dealer's much higher price for a second piece and complained about that bargain. Grant had both figures and wanted the family story in the handout. I removed the price sheet. I wanted time to settle the first bill before the room heard about the second.

Question for Frank Lloyd Wrong, Elle Loominate, Al Baster, Anne E. Dote: What would Grant change about your commission or public program?

Frank Lloyd Wrong
Grant wanted the entrance rebuilt around a plaque bearing his name. Naturally the plaque was ready before the donation. I had already approved the alcove without the load calculation Chip requested. Chip brought the unanswered request to the alcove argument, where Grant heard it. One missing calculation, and suddenly the patron was designing my building. I told him the plaque could wait. He told me my next commission could wait with it.

Elle Loominate
Grant called my installation decoration, then asked to own the pattern. The commission covered a loan of the work, not all the designs I might make from it. I had used fabric bought from another project's advance. If that client asked for delivery, I had no money left for a second piece. I told Grant why I needed an early payment. He offered to solve that problem by buying rather more of me than the cloth.

Al Baster
Grant admired the entrance piece until he saw the invoice. Then he wanted the same monument, a smaller price and his name above mine. It is cold-cast resin with metal powder. The publicity called it solid bronze, and I let that stand while the final installment was due. Apparently I can lift a sculpture but not a flattering mistake.

Anne E. Dote
Grant wanted the school tour replaced by a patron preview. He called it outreach because patrons can apparently reach much farther with a checkbook. I had promised the school places before the grant was approved. If his money didn't arrive, I had a bus full of children and a budget full of optimism. I put the school program back in the handout. He asked who had authorized me.

Question for Paige Turner, Mona Lott, Drew Conclusions, Robin Banks, Justin Tyme: What did Grant want made public, and what did you not want made public?

Paige Turner
The paper was going to call the gift a rescue. Justin's property records and the ownership file gave me a different story. Grant wanted the source behind it. I had published one sentence I couldn't support, and his lawyer had every right to demand a correction. That didn't make the rest of the report false. I wanted the correction in print and the underlying records on the editor's desk before the gala announcement swallowed both.

Mona Lott
Grant wanted a photograph of me blessing the acquisition. There was no official return agreement, and my letter made the claim sound rather more governmental than it was. I was negotiating for the claimants, not carrying a ministry's instructions. Tess also asked about the bead commission I recommended. I should disclose that commission before calling the recommendation cultural expertise.

Drew Conclusions
Grant wanted the rescue reviewed before the rescue existed. I published the embargoed announcement with a very favorable introduction. His agency had bought advertising on my podcast. That is a sentence I omitted from the introduction. Then I saw what the deal actually required. I removed the sponsor's credit as if that made the money disappear too.

Robin Banks
Grant wanted disputed ownership turned into a generous gift. I wanted the claimants heard before he received applause. My petition was not an invitation to discuss better lighting around the bowl. It asked for the ownership claim to be taken down. I told the museum I would disrupt the announcement if they refused. I was perfectly serious about that threat, and perfectly aware that security disliked it.

Justin Tyme
Grant's anniversary story starts with his family donating the building. The old deed starts with a purchase from a tenant trust that didn't get its last payment. I posted it as 'the stolen museum.' That was a great headline and a lousy account of the later settlement. Grant wanted a retraction with a confidentiality agreement attached. I was willing to correct the headline. He wanted the folder too.

Question for Dada DiCapo, Barb Dwyer, Brie DeVivre, Cary Crates, Faye Stop, Minnie Malist: What did Grant expect you to deliver, and why did that put you in trouble?

Dada DiCapo
Grant commissioned a performance, then decided the artist was an unfortunate extra. He wanted my name off the program and his name on everything else. My piece was called Donor Collapse. An empty dinner jacket falls out of a gilded chair. Apparently a donor can own satire, provided it never resembles him.

Barb Dwyer
Grant wanted privacy without hearing what privacy costs security. The museum wanted his money without hearing the same thing. I agreed to an exception and entered it as an equipment problem. That was my decision. It kept a donor request out of the ordinary log and kept my department looking competent. Those two conveniences are not looking particularly good tonight.

Brie DeVivre
Grant asked for another cheese course after we'd agreed the menu. The museum hadn't approved the charge, and he hadn't paid it. We had already bought the food. Monet wanted the bill stopped; I wanted someone to tell me where the money was coming from. The private toast was on the original order. I couldn't settle the museum's gift agreement, but I could insist that feeding the donor was still a bill.

Cary Crates
Grant wanted the delivery report clean. So did I, for a different reason. I had cracked a corner of his other frame and put it down as old damage. He offered not to send the photographs to my employer if I signed for everything without another condition check. I move objects for a living. I don't get to move the damage backward in time.

Faye Stop
Grant wanted the originals, not merely the society pictures. One frame showed him with a consultant he had denied meeting about the bowl. I sold the agency a cropped selection and kept that frame. I had let his agency's commercial gallery retouch pass as an editorial photograph. The agency had both versions. He offered to forget it if I forgot my originals.

Minnie Malist
Grant wanted a restrained gala. Then he wanted a reveal, a tribute and a photograph in which the restraint appeared to be applauding him. I had ordered work before Monet approved the additions. He offered to cover the gap if I called it his production. My crew had already done the work. I was not giving him their credit because I had been irresponsible with the order form.
