## Reader feedback

### Reader A

Accusation recorded before reading endings: Justin Tyme. His claim that a later newspaper correction proves neutralization conflicts with the retained clipping and tested active poison. His historical research explains knowledge of the bottle; his admitted wait at the unattended donor table provides an actual chance to contaminate the glass. Motive is suppression and humiliation of his property story, although many others have equally strong grievances. Suspicion trajectory: 1, 2, 2, 3, 3, 6, 7, 9. The solution is supported before a confession by the contradiction plus opportunity, but not uniquely forced by physical evidence. Anya, Barb and Chip retain credible access; the similarity of the scrap does not prove a matching tear. Most other admissions concern professional deception rather than murder. Staged releases are fair and materially revise theories, especially the clean ingredient and physical-bottle evidence; the last round makes Justin conspicuous through a lone disproved assertion. Thirty parallel answers and correction packets make suspense feel scheduled rather than organically investigated. Personal stakes are varied and humane; emotional voices share too much eloquent self-analysis. The reader performs meaningful source comparison but rarely follows an independently chosen question.

### Reader B

I accuse Justin Tyme. His unsupported neutralization story contradicts both the corrected clipping and active-cyanide laboratory findings, while his own visit places him beside the prepared unattended glass. His historical folder gives a plausible knowledge route; reputational exposure and coerced confidentiality provide a motive. The band scrap ties bottle opening to the poisoned glass but has no unique tear match, so it proves the likely method rather than the killer. A lie is not automatically murder: Barb could have used her blackout and room visit, Anya her functioning key and cupboard visit, Chip his band treatment, or Tess/Dada/Minnie/Robin/Paige their glass proximity. Their routes are viable unless timing or transfer evidence rules them out, yet Justin uniquely pushes a false account directly denying the lethal state of the murder source. I am highly confident as a puzzle reader, less confident that this would constitute a uniquely demonstrated prosecution. Suspense holds through stage seven because the clean ingredient, bare glass and accessible bottle narrow method without identifying hands. Stage eight drops a sharp contradictory claim while almost all other deceits receive documented resolutions, making the culprit obvious by editorial pattern. Documentary clues reward reconstructing action rather than counting grievances, but thirty-person mass confession rounds strain conversation and repeat the same confession, correction, personal regret rhythm. Motives cover employment, artistic credit, family power, debt, legitimacy, public benefit and shame; some are resolved before the death and should not be treated as enduring murder incentives. Distinct occupations and relationships help, but voices converge on articulate self-diagnosis. I would prefer fewer polished autobiographical admissions, more interrupted disagreement, and one extra discriminating physical or timing clue against Justin to separate his specific action from mere dishonest interpretation. The institutional failure around an unresolved poison loan is plausible as collective negligence, though many staff casually inspecting it and leaving a poisoned glass unattended feels engineered. The pre-vote story supplies remorse and costs; it leaves whether the actual murderer can be conclusively isolated unresolved.

I accuse Justin Tyme. He had historical source familiarity, preview exposure, an admitted visit to the unattended glass, a personal conflict over Grant discrediting his property story, and the uniquely false claim that the later clipping established neutralization. That claim conflicts with the actual clipping and laboratory evidence and protects the murder mechanism rather than an incidental embarrassment. Strongest alternative Anya Tique explicitly used a working cupboard key after preview and could obtain poison; Chip also knows the seal. Neither has comparably established drink opportunity or a lie denying the active source. Confidence is high within the game’s witness-contradiction convention, but this is not a complete physical proof: there is no observation of Justin obtaining poison or uniquely matched seal fragment.
