# An evening coming apart

**Chapter overview:** Grant expects a controlled unveiling and naming announcement. Guests are already making competing arrangements about the collection, museum governance and the show. Attempts to conceal earlier mistakes create several separate public disputes. A real mounting defect disrupts the exhibition without stopping the reception. The selected murderer exploits the ordinary service gap, and the investigation reinterprets the resulting record.

**Chapter trajectory:** Institutional ceremony becomes institutional embarrassment, then a murder investigation. What first looks like one concerted attack on Grant turns out to include separate unauthorized actions, conflicting loyalties and concealments. The guests must distinguish those acts from the poisoning.

## Scene 1: A private acquisition preview / preparation room, 6:20–6:28

**Scene purpose:** Establish the acquisition's custody, the conservator's objection and common access without thirty individual bottle-handling stories.

**Starting state:** Grant owns the historical bottle and insists on original contents. Chip requests an empty replica and withholds exhibition approval. A new band is applied and photographed intact.

**Ending state:** The preview ends and the original is locked away. Hidden band damage is not discovered until the investigation. In each selected world, exactly one guest has taken poison; others have attended a preview rather than performed synchronized jobs.

### Beat 1: An objection becomes an exception

**What happens:** Grant insists on a preview despite the unapproved display.

**How it happens:** The private ownership and written conservator objection establish conflicting authority. Staff record condition and lock the object afterward rather than quietly approve it for use.

**Trajectory:** Chip's effort to retain a documented objection leaves a hazardous exception in place. This is a safety failure, not evidence that a responsible museum would routinely allow it.

**Continuity:** Four innocent late-arrival versions miss the preview; their guilty alternatives arrive earlier. Do not put a fixed exhibit naming them as permanently late.

### Beat 2: One visitor exploits the exception

**What happens:** The selected culprit takes poison and damages the band beneath its back edge.

**How it happens:** This hidden act occurs in only the selected world. The public preview does not establish a person opening the bottle. Contents remain, preventing an unexplained depletion alarm.

**Trajectory:** The acquisition Grant bought for his announcement becomes a means of stopping it.

**Notes:** Original-poison preservation and the fragment's physical transfer remain plausibility risks. No operational chemical explanation is required or provided.

### Beat 2: Evidence changes what the earlier acts mean

**What happens:** The final laboratory release establishes a selective glass route, a compatible source and common paper construction.

**How it happens:** Players compare the previously heard physical encounters with the final accounts. Stage props, delivery wrapping, kitchen sauce and other materials have specific findings rather than assumed innocence. The common paper supports a class of explanations, not a unique identity.

**Trajectory:** The solution must be a better combined account, not the speaker whose text is shortest or whose scandal has the neatest paperwork.
