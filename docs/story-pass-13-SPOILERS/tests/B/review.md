# Independent first-time review

I read checkpoints 01–08 strictly in order and wrote each result before opening the next. I did not revise any earlier score. My final leading suspect is **Al Baster (9/10)**, with Chip Patina (6) and Brie DeVivre (5) retaining weaker alternatives.

## Causal plausibility

The strongest heard chain is coherent: an intact band before the 6:20–6:28 preview; a bottle locked afterward but later found secretly disturbed; an unattended new donor glass from 6:32–6:44; selective cyanide in that glass with clean punch, food, sauce, and prop; Al at the preview and subsequently lifting the actual glass; matching common foil in the apron he wore at both moments. His unpaid original, replacement replica, and credit dispute supply motive.

This is a strong comparative inference, not a witnessed or exclusive reconstruction. We are never told exactly how Al removed poison during a guest preview, what he carried it in, or how he dosed the glass while making a mock toast. The fragment in an apron pocket does not inherently explain a second fragment inside the glass. One could invent transfer from contaminated fabric or a container, but the released text does not establish that. Likewise, poison consistency does not chemically prove the sole source, and common packing stock is deliberately not a unique tear match.

The cupboard remaining locked is presented as fact; the basis for knowing nobody accessed it is absent. No after-preview photograph is mentioned. The bottle custody account is usable for the puzzle, but a concrete seal or key-accounting basis would strengthen it. The intact band distinguishes pre-preview handling from later disturbance without excluding Chip during the preview.

## Early identification and fairness

No early checkpoint uniquely identified a culprit. Artie led initially on resignation motive; Hugh and Monet rose after severe financial threats. Al became the strongest practical candidate at checkpoint 06 because he alone explicitly combined preview presence and target-glass handling. The decisive comparative trace arrived in checkpoint 08. That progression is fair and allows a provisional solve before the end without making the opening obvious.

The clear distinction between new star glass, rental glass, and PROP glass is useful. Independent lighting/camera power and continuing reception service prevent unfair assumptions. The common-stock limitation is honestly stated. However, there is no full preview attendance list or exact position/timing for everyone, so players cannot audit opportunity across all 22 fairly. Two receipt-supported late arrivals receive stronger exclusions than the rest. Most final speeches explain scandals rather than supply murder-window evidence.

## Scandal explanations do not exclude murder

The answer is **no**: those explanations genuinely account for specific misconduct and some physical traces, but generally do not exclude killing. A person could forge a signature and poison a glass; a conservator could object to dangerous display and exploit it; clear kitchen food does not absolve an individual who touches the donor glass. Hugh and Tess are concretely excluded from personally extracting poison during the preview, subject to the stated locked-cupboard record. That does not establish universal innocence, an accomplice, or an alternative source.

Chip remains a meaningful alternative because band stock belongs in his kit and he had bottle handling access. Brie remains a meaningful alternative because her outer-only wipe is a claim and she helped send the service manager away. Their alternatives require further assumptions to complete the chain; Al requires fewer.

## Style and behavior

The voices have some role vocabulary and pleasant introduction jokes. Across the confession rounds, however, nearly everybody speaks in the same lucid, self-critical register: “I did wrong thing; Grant worsened it; here is the precise distinction; here is my moral admission.” Many lines sound like carefully prepared defense arguments. The final round is particularly repetitive in separating each innocent material or document from the murder material. Because ordinary guests are reading prepared text, the performance cannot be expected to restore distinct voices through improvisation.

Al's final statement is the standout stylistic clue: while everyone else identifies why their suspicious object is irrelevant, he volunteers the matching foil in his apron and his contact with the actual glass without a comparable explanation. Players can identify him by the speech pattern even if they do not follow custody evidence.

Several behaviors serve the evidence layout conspicuously: Al's mock toast with a donor's personal empty glass; Brie wiping precisely its stem and base; Tess breaking and hiding a rental glass; almost everyone providing an orderly admission and corrective paperwork. The mock toast is plausible as resentment, but needs a small social trigger to feel natural. Handling an original poison bottle in a guest preview is reckless; Grant's stated purchase condition and insistence partly justify its presence. Why guests could get close enough to defeat the new band unnoticed remains thin.

There is also a factual inconsistency: checkpoint 02 says a staff technician disconnected the Salon camera under Barb's authorization; checkpoint 06 has Barb say “I disconnected the camera.” If this is shorthand, change it to “I ordered the camera disconnected.” Otherwise it unintentionally suggests another coverup.

## Specific repairs

1. Supply the full preview attendance and a short objective custody record: who observed locking, who held the key, and what prevents later access. Preserve realistic uncertainty where intended.
2. Add one recoverable clue explaining transport and dosing, such as a small container with the relevant residue, rather than relying on an apron fragment to do both jobs. Make it available before accusation, with an innocent competing interpretation if needed.
3. Give Al a natural reason for the mock toast—an assistant has just announced the replica, or a guest provokes him—and let a witness establish his glass handling. Avoid making his own final speech volunteer every incriminating link.
4. Vary the final statements: some deny, some evade, some angrily defend conduct, some answer only the question asked. Put technical material comparisons in a shared lab sheet instead of having every character explain their nonmatching trace.
5. Preserve genuine secondary suspects through actual opportunity, then narrow them through evidence if an exclusive solution is intended. Do not treat a confession's mundane explanation as an alibi.
6. Correct Barb's disconnect wording and clarify the statement that the cupboard remained locked.
7. Give guests a compact shared timeline and glass diagram; 22 voices plus many document distinctions otherwise place a large memory burden on ordinary players.

Overall: the murder route is understandable and Al is strongly favored by the end, but exclusive identification rests partly on asymmetrical disclosure and a conspicuous final-speech pattern. The strongest improvements are a concrete transport clue, auditable preview access, and less uniform confessional writing.
