## Unresolved failures

- **Convenient custody:** an original poison survives in a historical bottle, is displayed over a conservator's objection, sits in an open case when its custodian leaves, and receives a front-only check afterward. A second unattended object supplies the target glass. The combined lapses feel arranged for the puzzle.
- **Explanation is not exclusion:** an altered catalog, clear catering batch, unrelated prop, or legitimate use of packing stock may explain suspicious conduct without excluding a separate murder act. Many innocent branches still lack a sufficient final explanation of the murder-specific suspicion.
- **Shared speech rhythm:** despite occupational details and shorter Method answers, too many statements admit a lesser wrong, justify it, and end with polished self-analysis. The reviewers still hear a common author.
- **Late operational facts:** the custodian's receipt errand and condition comparison become important only in the late report. They need earlier, ordinary-story support if retained.
- **Physical ambiguity:** Paige's coat-pocket/glass movement needs a believable spatial reason; common paper and an unmarked vial support a route but do not identify an owner.
- **Chronology:** the hunt documents place an unveiling hold at 6:35 and a crack finding at 6:37 without explaining an initial concern followed by confirmation. This inconsistency is preserved in the tested input, not silently corrected after scoring.

The final evidence must change the meaning of facts already heard rather than confirm a complete path supplied only by the guilty speaker.
