## Unabridged reader feedback

### Cast 22: Elle Loominate

The dialogue gives several people convincing stress habits: Artie counts chairs, Frank fusses over placement, Monet thinks about wages, and Al wants to call home. Their practical interruptions make the death feel more immediate than the pun names initially suggest. Elle's client message and ownership dispute supply a credible emotional pressure without reducing her to generic greed. The facts allow a defensible pre-vote accusation by combining physical access, unattended glass access, and a demonstrably false explanation. However, the investigation feels visibly assembled in places. Nearly every suspect receives an admission of questionable conduct followed by a documentary correction, and the repeated grouped questions produce orderly testimonies from a room supposedly in crisis. The factual exhibits are precise and useful, but some are unusually tailored to eliminate anticipated alternatives, such as the wooden model and the explicitly absent hood. Hugh and Penny also make final claims contradicted by documents, so lying alone cannot solve the case; that is fair, though the opportunity distinction should remain clear. I found no essential contradiction in the poisoning timeline itself.

Finale: The confession follows the strongest pre-vote chain: Elle's access to the physical preview bottle, her presence beside the unattended glass, and her invented projection contradicted by two retained records. It states the taking and poisoning directly, but those are confirmation of the inferred acts rather than new decisive evidence needed to identify her. The continuing threat after repayment usefully explains why returning the advance did not remove her motive. The fabric becoming an unbearable reminder is a satisfying consequence rooted in her earlier priorities. Hugh's and Penny's denials also admit their specific final lies, validating the distinction between concealment of other misconduct and concealment of the murder mechanism. The confession's opening sounds like an evidence recap, but its closing restores personal feeling. Overall it is earned.

- minor / naturalness: Stages 4, 6, and 8 give almost every participant an orderly personal admission in the same grouped-question format, making the room feel unusually cooperative and the character arcs structurally similar. Suggested fix: Keep the evidence unchanged but let a few answers emerge through interruptions or challenges rather than another complete personal accounting.
- minor / clarity: Stage 8 contains several objectively false claims, while Elle's decisive distinction depends on remembering her stage 6 access to the waiting glass and the preview's physical bottle. Suggested fix: Provide a compact neutral location-and-custody reference alongside voting so players can compare opportunities without interpreting guilt for them.

### Cast 30: Elle Loominate

The mystery is solvable before voting through physical access, individual dosing, and a directly contradicted claim about projection. Independent image retention makes the critical photograph more persuasive than a suspect's export. I appreciated the differences between motives and unrelated wrongdoing: payroll, credit, restitution, and family standing create more than one emotional kind of pressure. Dialogue often sounds like pressured people, especially the practical requests about coats, chairs, food, and calling home. With thirty speakers, however, repeated admissions followed by a personal wound or terse request become noticeably structured. The releases also re-explain many resolved distinctions, which makes the ending feel assembled for evidentiary coverage. I did not find an actual factual contradiction beyond intentional suspect lies. Elle's inference is convincing, though not a strict physical proof: several other visitors could have reached both bottle and glass. The best clue is her invented projection story rather than a uniquely identifying trace. Players should have time to compare the documents instead of treating the final speeches as sufficient summaries.

Finale: The confession follows the existing decisive contradiction: physical preview bottle versus Elle's invented projection. Her taking poison at the preview and later dosing the glass confirms an inferred sequence rather than supplying a missing discriminator. Grant's threat to expose her diverted advance was already established, so the motive explanation is earned. The last line connecting ownership of the pattern with permanent memory of the victim gives an emotional consequence beyond simply listing clues. Hugh and Penny account for their separate lies without claiming innocence through virtue, which helps the final comparison. The opening still reads partly like a document answer key, but the personal ending improves its dramatic impact.

- minor / naturalness: The collection answers repeatedly recap the same contents-query and delivery distinctions across the final two rounds, making speech feel organized around document coverage. Suggested fix: Keep one precise recap and let the other speakers respond more briefly to personal stakes or questions.
- minor / evidence: The accusation rests on her false projection barrier plus access; the glass scrap expressly lacks a unique torn-edge match, leaving alternate physical perpetrators possible. Suggested fix: Give one bounded contemporaneous observation connecting her to bottle handling, while retaining the projection lie as the primary deduction.
