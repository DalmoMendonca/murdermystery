## Cary Crates / t001

Accusation: Cary Crates

Cary's final account falsely separates the tested crest glass from Grant's actual drink. The service manager explicitly unwrapped the shop glass at 6:32, the prepared-place image shows its bare crest, and the consumed-toast image shows Grant drinking from it. Cary instead insists it remained boxed, a plain spare was served, and testing therefore concerned the wrong container. That is a specific attempt to erase the fatal route, beyond his admitted frame-report fraud. He delivered the gift, returned through service during preparations, and faced Grant's threat to expose damage that could cost his employment. Staff had the original-contents warning and ordinary keys opened the bottle cupboard. Cyanide and band paper in the glass connect the actual bottle and drink. Brie is the strongest alternative because she prepared the individual glass alone and washed her measure. However, she acknowledges that access, her retained flavoring is clean, and her account fits the photographs. Cary uniquely contradicts several independent records to deny the murder's proven vessel.

The physical evidence makes a coherent puzzle: original bottle, active cyanide, clean communal punch, exposed personal glass, and a clearly photographed consumed toast. Cary's final denial is falsifiable from material already released. I could vote confidently without needing an offstage speaker to answer a missing question. The biggest limitation is that the decisive distinction becomes an extremely conspicuous false statement in the last round, after many suspects have delivered tidy corrections that align with the documents. Earlier uncertainty felt broad rather than progressively focused. The dialogue contains appealing details: Monet's payday memory, Al needing care at home, and Sue asking Mona to sit while remaining angry. However, nearly everyone follows the same rhythm of admitting a professional wrong, exposing a private vulnerability, and addressing a colleague. That repeated structure feels assembled despite the distinct occupations. Extensive document pairs also explicitly resolve numerous hypothetical mechanisms, making the final round partly an answer key. Events remain plausible within gala farce, although permitting a poison bottle to remain accessible after a keep-closed warning requires collective negligence. The modern loan papers acknowledge that negligence, so it is an ethical premise rather than a hidden contradiction.

Post-vote: The confession follows the established glass contradiction and confirms the already supported connection between bottle and toast. Stealing poison during the preview specifies an unobserved action, but does not introduce a new evidentiary trick or previously unavailable means. Cary's job fear and false damage report were present before the vote. The distinction between emptying his packing bin for the frame and poisoning the drink usefully preserves his lesser concealment without making it the proof of murder. His line about never being asked to move anything again gives the confession emotional specificity. Brie and Chip receive credible consequences rather than simply announcing innocence. The finale feels earned, though its opening explanation of the crest and receipt is somewhat like a formal solution recap.
