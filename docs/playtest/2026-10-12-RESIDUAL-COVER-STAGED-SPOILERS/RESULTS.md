## Unabridged reader feedback

### Cast 22: Hugh Bidder

The mystery is solvable before voting because Hugh's substitution claim can be tested against an explicit seller record, with motive and unattended-glass access already established. The supporting physical evidence usefully stays modest: the scrap is similar stock, not a magically exact match. The characters feel more human when they worry about payroll, a person needing care at home, or an employee's ride than when they explain object histories. Several voices stand out: Chip's clipped inventory, Artie's chair-counting, and Anne's insistence on school visits. Still, the repeated group questions and orderly turn-taking make the experience feel deliberately assembled. Almost everyone has a concealed professional failing, an acknowledgment, and a bounded explanation, which becomes a noticeable pattern across twenty-two people. The documents often do necessary factual work, but the sheer number of corrections, retained copies, and delivery distinctions makes the middle demanding. The dangerous bottle's continued preview is plausible as institutional negligence, although several staff knew enough that I wanted a stronger practical reason nobody halted it. The final contradiction is strong evidence of deception, but the exact transfer remains inferred rather than directly witnessed.

Finale: The confession follows the available chain: Hugh knew the uncanceled purchase condition, had a serious refund-exposure motive, visited the unattended glass, and supplied the false substitution story. It confirms the inferred extraction and transfer rather than introducing a new decisive clue. His closing concern with becoming impossible to remember gives emotional shape to his earlier fear of losing respectability. The explanation of which documents contradicted him is somewhat instructional and could be shorter once players have voted. Brie's staff-centered response and Chip's failed protective instinct give the other suspects credible aftermaths. I find the reveal earned, though the absence of a uniquely identifying physical transfer keeps the solution dependent on recognizing Hugh's particular source-denial lie.

- moderate / pacing: Stages 6 and 7 distribute many similar retained-copy, revised-label, and separate-delivery explanations across twenty-two people, obscuring the bottle and glass chain. Suggested fix: Add a compact neutral chronology of preview, glass setup, room visits, and collection without identifying which testimony is false.
- minor / naturalness: Multiple senior staff acknowledge the unresolved original-contents warning, yet the bottle remains on an open preview support. Their negligence is admitted but the decision feels institutionally convenient. Suggested fix: Provide one concrete operational reason the closed preview continued despite the restriction, such as intake awaiting a scheduled verification during the staff-only viewing.

### Cast 22: Penny Pincher

The people sound distinct through more than occupational jokes: Artie counts chairs, Monet remembers payday anxiety, Al worries about care at home, and Penny checks a silent phone. Those gestures make the final answers feel pressured rather than merely informative. The disputed donor terms, unpaid commissions, and professional corners cut around ownership form a plausible social environment. The physical murder chain is available before voting: real accessible bottle, active contents, exposed prepared glass, broken stopper band, and a visitor denying that band. It is solvable as a best-supported inference, although it does not provide exclusive custody or a unique forensic match. The organized question rounds and dense document packets sometimes feel deliberately assembled, especially when almost every person brings an unrelated confession and a corrected record. Most speeches leave interpretation to players. Hugh's final statement about the press preserving the contents condition creates an additional contradiction that distracts from Penny's decisive physical lie and needs clearer document naming. Overall the emotional details carry the extensive evidentiary apparatus effectively.

Finale: Penny's confession follows the available physical chain and her already admitted opportunities. Opening during preview and poisoning the waiting glass confirm an inferred sequence rather than introduce a missing mechanism or secret access. The price-collar cover, repayment that could not erase betrayal, and fear of family reaction were all available before voting. Her final inability to manage their response gives the confession emotional consequence. Hugh and Barb own their separate wrongdoing, which makes the ending more credible than blanket innocence. Hugh's speech does not resolve his earlier press-version claim, so that ambiguity persists, but it does not supply a new decisive fact needed to identify Penny. The outcome feels earned despite the evidence supporting inference rather than exclusive forensic proof.

- moderate / clarity: Hugh says the press version printed the original-contents condition, while the working proof and press delivery explicitly omit that sentence. If he means a different seller handout, the released wording does not make the distinction clear. Suggested fix: Name Hugh's document explicitly, or have him acknowledge that the shortened press catalog omitted the condition while his full intake attachment retained it.
- minor / pacing: Stage seven supplies a long series of corrected records for nearly every subplot immediately before another full-cast answer round, increasing reference burden. Suggested fix: Group supporting subplot corrections into a compact appendix while keeping bottle, glass, service, and timing records prominent.
