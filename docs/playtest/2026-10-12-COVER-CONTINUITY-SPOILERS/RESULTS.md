### Cast 22: Elle Loominate

The mystery is solvable before voting through comparison of admissions and independent records, especially Elle's changed bottle account. I appreciated that the glass was visibly bare, the preview bottle accessible, and the camera genuinely unpowered: those concrete facts prevented speculative technology from swallowing the case. Dialogue gave people different habits under pressure, such as Artie counting chairs, Frank wanting furniture moved, Monet thinking about wages, and Al trying to call home. Several relationships developed beyond simple hostility toward a donor. The uniform questioning structure and widespread confessions of separate professional misconduct nevertheless made the gathering feel carefully assembled. The final round sometimes repeats documentary distinctions almost verbatim, making player interpretation rather easy. Elle's financial motive remains plausible, but the returned advance makes the immediate threat less sharp than her early account suggests. The most persuasive evidence is her physical-access lie, not motive. Hugh and Penny's late factual denials provide useful competing suspects, though the large volume of records demands attentive comparison rather than conversational deduction alone.

Finale: The confession follows the available evidence closely: Elle's projection claim was contradicted by the stitch contact sheet and real glass bottle, and her access to the waiting glass was already admitted. Taking poison at the preview and using it later supplies the expected reconstruction rather than an unavailable decisive clue. Her explanation that repayment did not prevent exposure answers my remaining motive concern without changing who the evidence identified. The final image of being unable to touch her fabric again gives the admission an emotional consequence beyond a checklist of clues. Hugh and Penny acknowledge their documented lies and resolve their own misconduct coherently, making the rivals feel like people with losses rather than discarded puzzles.

- minor / pacing: Their final answers repeat the complete-versus-shortened warning delivery distinction already stated in the marked catalog proof and earlier accounts. Suggested fix: Shorten one final answer and let the retained proof carry the explicit delivery comparison.
- minor / motive: The returned advance and retained pattern rights weaken the urgency of the financial threat by the last round, while Grant's knowledge remains the principal unresolved pressure. Suggested fix: Clarify briefly what exposure to the client could still cost Elle after repayment, without adding new murder evidence.

### Cast 30: Elle Loominate

The central physical chain is understandable before voting, and the production records let a reader test Elle's projection claim without relying on a confession. The lack of a unique tear match is stated honestly. Distinct priorities help voices: Monets payroll concern, Arties compulsive hosting, Al's concern for someone at home, and Brie's attention to staff feel human under pressure. The repeated questions also create a conspicuous assembly pattern: nearly everybody has an exposed professional compromise, a donor pressure point, and a corrective paperwork action. With thirty people, retaining those minor distinctions becomes work. The finale-stage dialogue mostly lets characters speak for themselves rather than explicitly solve the murder, which helps. Hugh and Penny retain documentary contradictions alongside Elle, giving real competition rather than one universally truthful cast. Elle's claim is more directly connected to means and opportunity, but the timeline of how the bottle reached the private place remains inferred. That is a reasonable deduction, though one more physical movement clue could make it feel less dependent on identifying the most relevant false statement.

Finale: The confession follows the pre-vote contradiction cleanly: the slides contain stitches, the bottle was physical, and Elle invents a projection to deny access. Taking poison at the preview and using the waiting glass specifies the inferred mechanics, but adds no independent decisive evidence needed to identify her. Her final image of being unable to touch the fabric is emotionally stronger than the factual recap and connects ownership to guilt. Hugh and Penny acknowledge their remaining lies without becoming secret accomplices, preserving the single-murderer premise. There is a small spatial ambiguity between Elle's earlier bottle beside fabric and the finale's extraction at the preview, but the source and target chain remains coherent. Overall the ending feels earned.

- moderate / pacing: The second interview round gives nearly every character a similar edit-withheld-corrected-paperwork structure, accumulating many peripheral administrative facts before the final discriminating claim. Suggested fix: Trim redundant confirmations for characters whose factual conflicts are already settled, preserving the source, target, and three remaining contradictory claims.
- minor / evidence: Elle initially notices the bottle beside fabric at the donor glass and later calls it a projection; production records refute that, but no independent timing detail tracks the bottle from cupboard to private place. Suggested fix: Add a brief independently observed transport detail or dated empty-support observation that supports movement without naming the culprit.
