Act II

Artie Ficial
I copied Grant's signature onto the board's acceptance letter and put my resignation in his speech folder. The rival museum had offered me a job. I wanted our board to see the gift terms before he reached the microphone. I left the folder in the Donor Salon while the service table was unattended. Then the chair called; I took that argument to my office.

Claire O’Scuro
I slipped the independent review under the label Grant was signing. I wanted his name on the ownership finding he planned to suppress. Our argument began in the gallery. Afterward I left the signed sheet beside his private place setting; the server had gone to the kitchen. The label ink stained my hand. Grant thought he was approving the exhibition copy. He had approved rather more than that.

Hugh Bidder
My first entrance was the time I gave you. I brought a settlement envelope and asked Grant to release the disputed painting before the auction house heard about its certificate. He wanted a larger payment. I offered a smaller one and asked him to keep his voice down. The envelope stayed with him. There is a very expensive difference between a disputed attribution and a forged certificate.

Monet Bags
I took the unauthorized payroll ledger to Grant. He wanted his marble-wing expenses added to the transfer I had used to pay staff. I refused, and he tore a page from the schedule underneath it. His pen leaked across the papers. I went back through the salon looking for that page. His place was set; the crew was elsewhere. I wanted the board to see the original, not the addition he proposed.

Chip Patina
I altered the treatment date on the insurance copy. Grant wanted me to go further and call the damaged mount an ancient repair. I took the original away instead. I put the disputed insurance copy at his private place setting, rather than filing it publicly. Then I went to the closed alcove to check the actual mounting fault. There was a real defect beneath a very convenient story.

Dada DiCapo
I wrote the warning. I also handed the stage manager a program with Grant's name removed from my performance. He had put his foundation's name on the concept I developed. I intended to perform my version in front of his donors, credit included. When he asked whether I was threatening his unveiling, I said he should stay for the ending. The stage manager took my program and asked which version she was meant to run.

Vincent Van Faux
I offered Grant cash to withdraw the student painting he had bought. I took a replacement sketch to his table and put the money beneath it. He counted the money and left the sketch upside down. I had hidden the pigment changes in my recent work; I was now trying to buy control of my older work too. He said the gala audience would enjoy comparing them. I asked whether embarrassing the artist was included in his acquisition policy.

Sue Venir
I softened my first legal opinion because Grant threatened to take the account elsewhere. I kept the original. I collected the annex from preparation, put the two versions in my case, and told him that his ownership claim still had a problem. He asked which opinion I intended to stand behind in public. I said the one I had signed. He had paid for advice and expected permission.

Dr. Art E. Fact
I took my corrected citation to Grant. I had removed the paragraph naming the person who admitted me to the private archive. He wanted the entire article withdrawn. I offered a correction and told him the ownership announcement would be challenged before the toast. He asked for my source. I left him the citation sheet and kept the source's name. His proposed exhibition label still contradicted the file I had seen.

Tess Tament
I entered at the time I already gave you. I brought the trustees' objection in my bag. Grant had treated my vote as an inheritance he could collect early. I told him the foundation would hear about the transfer before he announced it. He reached for my bag; I moved it behind my chair. The objection stayed with him. He read the first line twice and asked who else had signed.

Barb Dwyer
Grant ordered the camera disconnection in writing. I followed the order and concealed his threat to blame my staff. When the outage began, I checked the gallery door. My route back to the public entrance passed through the salon, where the private table was set and the server was absent. I thought his signed order would protect us. I had also made a private conversation considerably harder to reconstruct.

Paige Turner
I brought the corrected proof but withheld my source's name. Grant offered to withdraw the injunction if I supplied it. I left the marked carbon at his place while he was talking in the gallery; the recorder kept running in my bag. He had crossed out a sentence of his own. I wanted that demand kept beside the correction I actually owed him.

Frank Lloyd Wrong
I signed the earlier installation note and wanted it replaced. Grant demanded the alcove reopened for the photographs. I refused to restore the crossed-out load figure. I carried the stop-work drawing through the salon on my way to the crew; his private table lay between the doorways. He could cancel my contract. He could not make the support carry more weight by signing another note.

Mona Lott
I leaked the negotiations and delivered an amendment on delegation letterhead without permission. Grant thought the letterhead meant everyone had approved it. I let him read before correcting that impression. The amendment would stop his permanent-ownership announcement. He wanted it withdrawn before he reached the microphone. I said my delegation had spent long enough waiting for him to make a gracious decision. This time we had brought a written one.

Elon Mosaic
I concealed my auction purchase and promised another bidder that Grant's board appointment would fail. I took Grant my installation proposal with a new price written on the fold. I had also canceled his access code for the presentation. He called it extortion. I asked which part he wanted restored first: the price, the access, or his name on the display. He began with the name.

Anya Tique
I arrived when I said I did. Grant wanted my signature on a new accession statement before the old file was discussed. I had accepted the object in 1989 with its export papers still promised. I put that original record in front of him. He said the museum could issue a cleaner version tonight. I told him a second signature would not make the promised document appear.

Saul D. Bid
I brought Grant the subsidiary fee statement and asked him to keep the client name private. I had concealed the commission. He wanted the fee waived as the price of his silence. I put my ceiling figure on the folded sheet and left it beside him. He said the figure was insulting. I told him it was the ceiling. We had reached the point where both of us were bidding with someone else's reputation.

Elle Loominate
I asked preparation to return my loan amendment and took it to Grant. I had promised another institution the same textile and failed to disclose it. Grant wanted permanent ownership announced tonight. I told him the amendment still said LOAN and asked him to read that word into the microphone. He folded the page over it. I unfolded it. We managed a lengthy conversation without moving beyond four letters.

Reed DeLabel
I collected the checked reference slip and compared it with the accession entry. Grant had found a sentence in my draft that he could use against Art. I had copied that reference without checking it myself. The original undermined Grant's exhibition label too. I took the comparison to him and said I would publish the correction. He wanted the original returned to the drawer before anyone else saw it.

Al Baster
I had omitted the fabricator from the sculpture proposal. Grant was using that omission to demand a replacement at my expense. I went from the installation area through the salon to leave the shop drawing at his place. It showed who had made the internal support. I wanted the actual work considered before he announced whose work would replace it. The unpaid balance was mine; the concealed labor was not.

Ella Gance
I entered when I said I did. I brought a replacement offer for the work I had promised to another institution. Grant had the correspondence and wanted me to endorse his permanent-ownership announcement. I put my offer in front of him. He said I could settle the matter by smiling beside the object. I said the other institution would need something it could hang on a wall.

Drew Conclusions
I brought Grant the revised newsletter page. I had written the anonymous jokes and accepted payment for a favorable correction. He wanted the correction enlarged into a tribute. I put the smaller paragraph beside him and told him the other edition was already circulating. He asked how many copies. I gave him the readership figure. He reached for the red pencil before asking what it would cost to stop the next episode.

Brie DeVivre
I withheld Grant's unpaid extra course and used a cheaper supplier than the menu promised. While the manager was in the kitchen, I went to his private table for the disputed invoice. Sauce had spilled across it and onto the glass base. I moved the glass and rescued the paper. He wanted more service while disputing what he had already ordered. I wanted his signature on the charges.

Cary Crates
I had concealed a cracked frame with the packing blanket and a reversible mount. Before the freight lift failed, I left the repair estimate at Grant's private place setting. I wanted his signature before the unveiling. Later I stayed with the trolley while the mechanic dealt with the lift. The damage I should have reported was traveling under a blanket because Grant preferred an immaculate delivery record.

Anne E. Dote
I collected the revised education figures and circulated the petition with the cuts left visible. I had used a staff mailing list without approval. Grant wanted the petition collected before the donors saw it. I asked him to tell the docents himself which programs were closing. He wanted his name on a workshop. I wanted enough staff left to run one. The signatures kept arriving while he argued.

Faye Stop
Grant ordered the camera image deleted. I agreed, but the separate print showed his meeting beside the shipping papers. I let him see it and then regretted that choice. I returned to the salon to retrieve the print while the service station was unattended. He thought he had asked about every copy. I had answered about one file, which was a distinction I did not explain.

Robin Banks
I gave Grant the provenance brochure after planting the protest stickers and contacting a reporter. He had barred me from an earlier event and wanted me removed again before the unveiling. I told him the reporter already had the shipping record. He tore a sticker off the brochure and left the paper beside his glass. I kept circulating the other copies. The museum's welcome table was more helpful than its owner.

Penny Pincher
I brought the family letters and left out the one accusing my grandfather as well as Grant. I threatened to publish the rest if he announced permanent ownership. He offered to buy them. I wrote his price on the cover sheet so I would remember what he thought the family's silence cost. Then I asked him to read the disputed sale date. He offered a larger price instead.

Justin Tyme
I brought the parcel story with a private land-file note I had promised never to publish. Grant's version depended on nobody checking that note. I intended to read the corrected boundary into my recorder at the gala. He offered me another story. I told him this would be his last public claim to that parcel. He heard a threat. I had brought the deed to show him exactly where the claim ended.

Minnie Malist
I removed the donor lettering and reduced the lighting sequence without permission. Grant wanted the original cue sheet restored. The spare was at his private place, so I collected it before he could give the old sequence back to the operator. The safety override then took priority. I had changed the program, and I had every reason to resent him discovering exactly what I had removed.
