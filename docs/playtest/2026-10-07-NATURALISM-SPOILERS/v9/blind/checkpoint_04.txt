Act I

Artie Ficial
Grant wanted my resignation attached to his donation, like a complimentary tote bag. I had accepted a job at a rival museum. There, I've said it. I kept smiling because thirty years of museum management teaches you to applaud while someone repossesses your chair.

Claire O’Scuro
I commissioned an independent review and concealed it from the board. Grant wanted the Star Bowl exhibited with a gap in its history large enough to park his limousine. He could remove me from the show. He couldn't make the missing export permit materialize by pointing at his checkbook.

Hugh Bidder
The certificate was forged before it reached me. I then altered my file to hide how badly I had been fooled. Grant intended to call that a forgery of my own unless I refunded millions. My professional judgment was poor. His price for keeping quiet was excellent, for him. I first entered at 6:29, delayed by a dispute over a reserve price.

Monet Bags
I wanted to see what the conservation budget had bought. The transfer was unauthorized. I moved restricted money to keep payroll from bouncing and intended to reverse it after the donation. Grant wanted his naming-wing expenses hidden there too. The difference is several salaries versus a marble likeness of a man who already owned mirrors.

Chip Patina
I changed the date on a treatment record. There was an insurance deadline, an intact object, and a donor who thought paperwork was something conservators should eat. Grant wanted a much worse alteration: a report calling recent repairs ancient. I refused to turn a condition report into fiction.

Dada DiCapo
The reference shelves had more room than my performance space, which I pointed out to the steward. Yes, I wrote the warning. Returning what was taken meant my performance concept, which Grant planned to unveil with himself credited as visionary patron. It was a provocation. He turned my work into an advertisement and then complained that the artist had supplied an uncomfortable caption.

Vincent Van Faux
The anonymous buybacks were mine. I paid to remove my student paintings from circulation. Grant owned the worst one and wanted it beside my recent work as a joke. A patron buying art is charming. A patron buying the right to humiliate its painter is a different transaction.

Sue Venir
I issued a softer opinion after Grant rejected the original. Both versions survive. He wanted my name on his announcement and none of my qualifications in his speech. I am accustomed to clients ignoring advice. It is particularly irritating when they also demand that I sign the consequences.

Dr. Art E. Fact
I overstated one inference in my published article. Grant knew it and threatened to use that error against research into his collection. The error deserves a correction. It does not transform every questionable sale in his papers into a respectable one. Scholarship is not a donor loyalty program.

Tess Tament
Grant called me ornamental. Then he demanded my signature on the family trust. Apparently ornaments can execute legal documents. I organized the trustees against him. The money was supposed to support people, and his new plan mostly supported a very expensive sign saying GRANT. The entrance attendant admitted me for the first time at 6:31.

Barb Dwyer
I ordered the technician to unplug the Salon camera on Grant's instruction. I should have refused. He said he needed private meetings and that security would own any failure afterward. That's a lovely chain of command: he gives the order, I inherit the consequences, and the camera gets the evening off.

Paige Turner
The reference labels were useful background for my reporting. Grant threatened an injunction and my source. A rich man threatening a newspaper is weather. Threatening the person who trusted me is different. I concealed that source and kept the documents. You can ask me about my reporting. You cannot have their name as a party favor.

Frank Lloyd Wrong
Grant thought a photograph would have been sufficient. I signed off on a temporary lighting rig below my own standard. Grant demanded another load and threatened to withhold payment if I refused. A drawing can accommodate many unreasonable requests. Gravity remains annoyingly independent of the donor agreement. I should have stopped the installation sooner.

Mona Lott
I leaked the restitution talks. Quiet diplomacy had become a polite room where Grant could keep saying no. He intended to announce permanent ownership tonight. I wanted the people represented by the collection to have a voice stronger than the donor's microphone. I broke confidentiality to obtain it.

Elon Mosaic
The anonymous buyer was me. Grant lost, then decided the auction required a moral inquiry into my character. He intended to announce it tonight and kill my board appointment. A man who purchased the naming rights to a museum objected to someone else purchasing an object.

Anya Tique
In 1989 I accepted Grant's donation with incomplete papers. We needed money, and I believed the documents would follow. They did not. He planned to call that my founding principle tonight. It was a compromise I regretted, not permission for him to repeat it indefinitely. The staffed entrance admitted me for the first time at 6:30.

Saul D. Bid
I routed the sale through a subsidiary. The client demanded discretion, and I supplied a discreet invoice with an indiscreet fee. Grant threatened to expose the arrangement unless I waived that fee. He admired confidentiality until someone charged him for it.

Elle Loominate
The loan paperwork is incomplete because I feared my family would refuse. Grant then offered to buy the textile as if that solved consent. It isn't a decorative opportunity. People in my family remember who made it, and none of those names is Grant.

Reed DeLabel
Grant threatened my fellowship after I found a document contradicting the Star Bowl label. The same document undermines a sentence in Art's research. I concealed that second problem. Being the youngest researcher does not make delivering two unpleasant truths feel like a promising career strategy.

Al Baster
I changed an internal support after the fabricator missed the deadline. Grant intended to replace my original with a cheap replica and dispute the final payment. A sculpture's support is supposed to remain invisible. Apparently he wanted the sculptor's labor to follow the same principle.

Ella Gance
I sold the work I had promised to donate. A failed investment made the promise unaffordable, and I hoped to replace it before anyone noticed. Grant intended to announce the discrepancy at dinner. He appreciated generosity most when it supplied a weapon against another donor. The entrance attendant admitted me for the first time at 6:32.

Drew Conclusions
Grant's poison exhibit seemed an unusually honest donor portrait. The anonymous newsletter is mine. Some jokes came from private conversations, and one hurt somebody who didn't deserve it. Grant planned to expose the author tonight. I was prepared to admit the newsletter. I was considerably less prepared to let him introduce it as a tribute to himself.

Brie DeVivre
I checked which shelves held museum materials before collecting anything for catering. Grant refused my invoice, then ordered a new private service. He and his assistant gave opposite instructions. I changed the setup twice and billed the changes. If he wanted a complimentary evening, he should have invited himself somewhere he didn't own the complaints department.

Cary Crates
I concealed a repairable frame crack with a reversible mount. Grant saw it and threatened my job. He wanted the frame immaculate for photographs and the handler gone afterward. I should have reported the crack at once. Quietly fixing things is not always the same as doing them right.

Anne E. Dote
The anonymous petition was mine. Grant wanted the education budget turned into a private lounge. I slipped copies into trustee folders. If trustees can spend an evening discussing champagne access, they can spend two minutes discussing children's access. I didn't request permission for the comparison.

Faye Stop
I kept a backup after agreeing to delete the photograph. It shows Grant and Hugh beside a shipping file. Grant demanded the image because, apparently, candid photography is excellent until the subject is candid. I had already surrendered one version of the truth. I kept another.

Robin Banks
The stickers were mine. PROVENANCE IS A VERB fits nicely in a brochure that treats ownership as a permanent noun. Grant had me removed from an earlier event and threatened legal action tonight. I came to make the collection's history visible. He wanted me visible only on a security notice.

Penny Pincher
I brought the family letters. They show that we contested the sale Grant called perfectly voluntary. I intended to confront him. He valued the object generously when displaying it and remembered its purchase price with astonishing modesty. I can recognize a bargain without mistaking it for justice.

Justin Tyme
I owe Tulsa a correction. Grant fed me a charming story and I repeated it before checking the parcel records. He wanted the error preserved as local history. I've seen too many confident men turn a convenient story into a plaque. I was about to remove his plaque, figuratively speaking.

Minnie Malist
I changed the cue after approval. Grant wanted a gaudy spectacle and threatened payment when Frank objected. I gave the room a simpler sequence. The flickers were my correction, badly timed. Minimalism loses some dignity when it has to apologize to an electrician.
