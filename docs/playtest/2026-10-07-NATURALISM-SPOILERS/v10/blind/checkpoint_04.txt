Act I

Artie Ficial
Grant attached my resignation to his gift. I had accepted a job at another museum and had not told the board. He planned to present my departure as their price for keeping the collection. I wanted the chair to hear the terms before he announced them.

Claire O’Scuro
I concealed the independent review from the board. It identifies a missing export permit in the Star Bowl's history. Grant wanted it exhibited as settled ownership and threatened to remove me from the show. I could lose the exhibition I had built by insisting on the objection.

Hugh Bidder
The certificate was forged before it reached me. I then altered my file to hide how badly I had been fooled. Grant intended to call that a forgery of my own unless I refunded millions. My professional judgment was poor. His price for keeping quiet was excellent, for him. I first entered at 6:29, delayed by a dispute over a reserve price.

Monet Bags
I moved restricted money without authorization. Payroll would have failed before the donation arrived. Grant discovered the transfer and demanded his wing expenses added to it. He could report me for the first transaction while benefiting from the second. I had no intention of letting him do that.

Chip Patina
I changed a treatment date to meet the insurance deadline. Grant knew. He demanded a report calling a recent repair ancient, then threatened the contract when I refused. I had compromised one record and was being asked to falsify another. The original treatment photograph still existed.

Dada DiCapo
Yes, I wrote the warning. Returning what was taken meant my performance concept, which Grant planned to unveil with himself credited as visionary patron. It was a provocation. He turned my work into an advertisement and then complained that the artist had supplied an uncomfortable caption.

Vincent Van Faux
The anonymous buybacks were mine. I paid to remove my student paintings from circulation. Grant owned the worst one and wanted it beside my recent work as a joke. A patron buying art is charming. A patron buying the right to humiliate its painter is a different transaction.

Sue Venir
I softened my legal opinion after Grant rejected it. He was using the revised wording as clearance for an announcement it did not authorize. I should have objected publicly. I had let him believe I would sign it, and he threatened to disclose exactly how accommodating I had been.

Dr. Art E. Fact
One inference in my article is wrong. Grant found it and threatened to discredit all the research into his collection with that sentence. I would correct it. I would not withdraw the ownership challenge, and I did not want my mistake used to silence the archive.

Tess Tament
Grant called me ornamental. Then he demanded my signature on the family trust. Apparently ornaments can execute legal documents. I organized the trustees against him. The money was supposed to support people, and his new plan mostly supported a very expensive sign saying GRANT. The entrance attendant admitted me for the first time at 6:31.

Barb Dwyer
I ordered the camera disconnected. Grant authorized it; the security procedure did not. He wanted privacy and intended to blame my staff if something went wrong. I followed his order and concealed the exception. The officer giving that instruction was me.

Paige Turner
Grant threatened the report, my source and the paper's ability to publish. I had made a factual error he could use. I corrected that sentence and refused the name he wanted. The person who trusted me was not a term I could trade in a settlement.

Frank Lloyd Wrong
I approved a temporary installation below my own standard. Grant demanded more load and threatened to withhold payment when I objected. I should have stopped the work earlier. He intended to use my first signature as approval of something I would not sign now.

Mona Lott
I leaked confidential restitution talks. Grant intended to announce permanent ownership while the people represented by the collection were still disputing the sale. The partners had not authorized my disclosure. I did it because the announcement would otherwise become the only account guests heard.

Elon Mosaic
I was the anonymous buyer. Hugh handled the bid so Grant wouldn't inflate the price merely to punish me. Grant then threatened to block my board appointment and describe my collecting as speculative theater. I do enjoy theater. I prefer the seats to come with an actual purchase receipt.

Anya Tique
In 1989 I accepted the donation with incomplete papers. We needed the money, and I believed the documents would follow. They did not. Grant planned to reveal my compromise tonight and call it permission to repeat it. I wanted the original record examined before he described what I had signed. The staffed entrance admitted me for the first time at 6:30.

Saul D. Bid
I routed the sale through a subsidiary. The client demanded discretion, and I supplied a discreet invoice with an indiscreet fee. Grant threatened to expose the arrangement unless I waived that fee. He admired confidentiality until someone charged him for it.

Elle Loominate
I promised the textile without obtaining my family's consent. Grant offered to purchase it and call the problem settled. The people who made it had not given me that right. I was trying to withdraw a promise that I should not have made, and he intended to announce possession anyway.

Reed DeLabel
I concealed a problem in the reference I had copied. It contradicted the exhibition label and part of Art's research. Grant threatened my fellowship if I supplied it to the review. I could lose that opportunity by exposing my own shortcut as well as his claim.

Al Baster
I changed the sculpture's internal support and omitted the fabricator's contribution. Grant threatened to replace it with a replica and dispute the balance. There were workers still waiting to be paid. I had helped him hide their work by putting only my name on the finished piece.

Ella Gance
I sold the work I had promised to donate. A failed investment made the promise unaffordable. I hoped to bring a replacement before anyone asked. Grant knew where the original had gone and intended to reveal it in his announcement. I had invited people to see me thanked for something I could not deliver. The entrance attendant admitted me for the first time at 6:32.

Drew Conclusions
The anonymous newsletter is mine. Some jokes came from private conversations, and one hurt somebody who didn't deserve it. Grant planned to expose the author tonight. I was prepared to admit the newsletter. I was considerably less prepared to let him introduce it as a tribute to himself.

Brie DeVivre
Grant refused my invoice, then ordered a new private service. He and his assistant gave opposite instructions. I changed the setup twice and billed the changes. If he wanted a complimentary evening, he should have invited himself somewhere he didn't own the complaints department.

Cary Crates
I concealed a frame crack instead of reporting it. Grant found the damage and threatened my job. He then demanded an undamaged delivery record for the photographs. I had made the original mistake. He wanted me to sign the account that would hide it from everyone else.

Anne E. Dote
I circulated the petition without authorization. Grant's gift replaced the education space with a private donor lounge. The staff whose jobs would disappear had not been consulted. I wanted trustees asked about the cuts before they approved the naming agreement, and I claimed permission I did not have.

Faye Stop
I retained a print after agreeing to delete the camera image. It shows Grant and Hugh beside the shipping file. Grant wanted every copy destroyed. I had promised one deletion and let him believe it meant more. He knew I had seen something he did not want in the event photographs.

Robin Banks
The stickers were mine. PROVENANCE IS A VERB fits nicely in a brochure that treats ownership as a permanent noun. Grant had me removed from an earlier event and threatened legal action tonight. I came to make the collection's history visible. He wanted me visible only on a security notice.

Penny Pincher
The family letters challenge Grant's account of the sale. I brought them intending to confront him and left out the letter implicating my grandfather. Grant knew the missing part and offered to buy our silence. I was asking the museum to tell a history I had not told honestly myself.

Justin Tyme
I owe Tulsa a correction. Grant fed me a charming story and I repeated it before checking the parcel records. He wanted the error preserved as local history. I've seen too many confident men turn a convenient story into a plaque. I was about to remove his plaque, figuratively speaking.

Minnie Malist
I changed the approved lighting cue. Grant restored the donor lettering and threatened payment when I objected. I then prepared a blackout he had not authorized. It was intended to interrupt the naming announcement. The operator did not know what I had hidden in that sequence.
