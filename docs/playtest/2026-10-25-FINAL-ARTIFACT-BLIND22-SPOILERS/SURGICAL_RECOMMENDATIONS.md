# Last-pass recommendations

These are author recommendations grounded in the blind readers' feedback, not edits already made. Keep the six-page Act III limit and the three previously approved pages. The test does not establish that any proposed edit improves solve rates.

## Highest-value presentation edit: narrow Grant's recording

Readers in the Penny, Fact, Mona, Vincent and Elon scenarios found the executor message conveniently comprehensive. The Elon reader called it a "prepared clue digest." The Vincent reader similarly described a "curated answer sheet."

The smallest change is to frame it as a private gala briefing, retaining its factual paragraphs. Suggested heading: **Grant Larceny / Private Gala Briefing**. Suggested opening: **Before anyone calls this gift settled, check the unsigned papers.** Remove the executors label and death-conditional opening. Keep the board vote, family seat, announcement/toast distinction, loan/sale status, reproduction dispute and rejected performance facts. Add no new recording timestamp or witness.

This would address the conspicuous foreknowledge and give the recording a routine purpose. It would not fix uncertainty about which visitor poisoned the drink.

## Small artifact edit: give physical findings a physical source

The Dada reader singled out the sculpture's "Sealing paper: NONE" entry in an accounts table. The Brie reader independently called the estate table an answer grid. Keep the material facts but place them in a small **Surface Examination / Retained Sculpture Sample** slip on the same sheet: **Bronze powder and gold leaf. No red foil-backed paper.** The resin construction, corrected label and reduced settlement remain with the commission record.

For other entries, use concrete objects and sources as labels: the family appraisal's condition and comparable; the merchant payment for the gala fabric; the installation completion record for the transparent cover. Preserve every existing factual assertion. Do not invent dates, signatures, witnesses or handling events to make the props look authentic.

## Lowest-risk hosting changes, with no reprint

Read Grant's brief recording aloud once when Act III opens, rather than requiring every guest to silently parse it. Keep the Act II service timeline available at the vote. The Sue reader specifically asked to make that chronology easy to revisit. These reuse information already released; the AI tests did not measure their effect on human play.

## Dialogue: preserve the lies while varying delivery

Many readers noticed the parallel pattern of a documentary entry followed by a flat denial. Their suggestion that some innocents simply admit the truth is risky: those innocent cover lies create the competing cases. Keep the factual claims and concealments, especially the seven established unrelated scandals. If there is time for a speech pass, trim a repeated emotional coda or vary an interruption; do not turn an innocent's lie into an admission without another balance check. If packets are printed, leave them intact.

## Changes that exceed a surgical pass

Several readers requested an independently witnessed handling or timing clue. That is a structural request. A fixed printed clue that identifies one particular person can break the other random-murderer scenarios. It would need checking against all 22 routes; it should not be slipped into the final print run as a cosmetic improvement.

Treat the incorrect accusations and low-confidence correct accusations as evidence of unresolved attribution, not proof that a wording polish alone will repair the puzzle. These tests cannot distinguish a presentation effect from ordinary reader variation, because there was one reader per scenario and no matched control group.
