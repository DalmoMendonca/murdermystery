## Unabridged reader feedback

### Cast 22: Hugh Bidder

The final contradiction makes this solvable before voting, and the physical route survives sensible questions about the bottle, wrapping, ingredients, and camera. The best voices use present worries: Monet calculating Friday payroll, Tess fixating on having bought the glass, Al wanting to call home, and Artie unable to bear another seating request. Those moments convey pressure without simply naming a motive. The cast nevertheless shares a conspicuously even rhythm of admitting a mistake, addressing another character, and delivering an emotionally revealing closing line. The repeated question rounds also make it feel like a deliberately assembled hearing. Stage seven is a large administrative release; several documents seem designed to close specific hypothetical escape routes. That supports fairness but slows dramatic momentum. Hugh's false substitution claim is satisfyingly checkable, though it becomes much more diagnostic than the earlier opportunity material. The refund withdrawal slightly blurs his immediate motive, while public exposure of the altered bowl file remains credible. I found no necessary logical contradiction or decisive missing clue.

Finale: Hugh's confession follows the available chain: the uncanceled original-contents purchase, his knowledge of the correspondence, the physical bottle at the preview, and his admitted visit to the unattended glass. It supplies the exact taking and dosing actions, but those confirm the inferred method rather than introducing a new decisive clue. His concern with remaining respectable gives the professional motive emotional shape. The reference to the refund demand sits awkwardly beside its documented withdrawal; fear of exposure would have been the cleaner emphasis. Chip and Barb's responses give consequences to their separate failures. The ending feels earned, though its shared polished introspection slightly reduces the sensation of people speaking spontaneously.

- moderate / voice: The later answer rounds repeatedly combine a procedural admission, a named appeal, and an introspective closing statement, making different speakers share a visible dramatic template. Suggested fix: Vary the response shapes for several speakers: allow an interrupted practical answer, a short defensive refusal, or a concrete demand without a reflective final line.
- moderate / pacing: Stage seven delivers many settlement and correction records together, including several unrelated to the poison route, slowing the progression toward accusation. Suggested fix: Move a few resolved commission or publicity corrections into earlier releases while retaining the purchase, glass, and access evidence together.
- minor / motive: The retained sales papers withdraw the dealer refund demand after Hugh has framed Grant's public admission demand as career destroying; the remaining distinction is inferable but somewhat blurred. Suggested fix: Clarify briefly whether the demand for a public admission survives the withdrawn monetary refund request.

### Cast 30: Penny Pincher

The central chain is accessible before voting: genuine bottle, live poison, prepared individual glass, compatible scrap, and a suspect denying the band arrangement despite physical records. The non-unique scrap is appropriately qualified. Penny is a persuasive solution, though not a deductive certainty, since timing remains broad and Hugh also delivers a demonstrably false statement. I appreciated that characters retain damaging faults while being distinguished from murder suspects: payroll diversion, family exploitation, artistic jealousy, and institutional complacency do different emotional work. Brie and Barb have especially recognizable practical voices. The recurring structure becomes conspicuous across thirty people: each explains a work dispute, admits a flaw, addresses another guest, and supplies a rueful personal reflection. The final round contains many polished self-diagnoses that feel composed after the crisis rather than spoken during it. Documents also repeatedly resolve very specific alternate theories, giving the investigation a deliberately assembled quality. Nevertheless, the physical evidence is clear and the emotional stakes exceed a simple parade of motives.

Finale: The confession follows the pre-vote evidence closely. Penny reverses the precise false claim about the stopper band, connects her admitted preview inspection with taking poison, and connects her admitted private-place visit with the waiting glass. The family top-up failing to erase an earlier dishonest calculation was already a credible motive, so the ending needs no sudden inheritance or undisclosed grievance. Opening the bottle during the preview is newly specified action, but it confirms the inferred route rather than supplying a missing decisive clue. Hugh's admission clarifies why a competing lie mattered without making him conveniently blameless. Penny's inability to manage her relatives' response provides a satisfying emotional consequence. The polished explanatory delivery still feels theatrical, but the solution is earned.

- moderate / naturalness: Final-round statements repeatedly combine an evidentiary clarification with a polished reflection about the speaker's own public persona. The shared rhythm makes the ensemble sound authored together. Suggested fix: Let a few answers end abruptly at the factual correction, or use interruption and practical requests without an accompanying self-diagnosis.
- minor / evidence: Penny's physical-band lie strongly favors her, but Hugh also contradicts the press proof and several people retain broad access without precise exclusion. Suggested fix: Add a modest independent observation bounding Penny's visit relative to the glass setup and bottle storage, without introducing a unique forensic match.
