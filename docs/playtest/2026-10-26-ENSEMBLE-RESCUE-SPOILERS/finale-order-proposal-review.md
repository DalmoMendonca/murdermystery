# Finale-order proposal: isolated editorial review

## Judgment

This is a safer first experiment than eight conditional or muted social rewrites. It solves the specific post-confession invitation problem through one shared rule, preserving character voice and avoiding extra branch selection inside already selected finale boxes. It does not yet constitute a tested staging improvement or justify promotion.

Use the original top-three tally, filtered by innocence, with no replacement suspects. That means two innocent readings plus the murderer if the culprit ranked in the top three; three innocent readings plus the murderer if the culprit ranked outside them. The maximum remains four speeches. Escaped Justice must continue to use the original tally, not the people who actually read before the confession.

## Ordinary-guest cue

The task is simple once expressed as two phases: called innocent suspects read; the player using IF MURDERER waits and then reads last. Each guest already knows their own branch, so the host need not identify the murderer in advance. Nobody needs to improvise a response or decide whether a particular social invitation needs changing.

The potentially confusing phrase is “top three innocent suspects.” It can sound as though the host should search down the ranking until three innocent people have read. The printed instruction must instead refer to the three most-voted names and explicitly prohibit replacing a waiting murderer with fourth place. A single common instruction before the first call is safer than asking each named suspect whether they are innocent. The latter invites an unnecessary verbal confession or denial.

The host should still call the three names in tally order, applying the existing alphabetical tie rule, and allow an innocent reading to finish. The murderer does not begin their confession when their name is called; they wait for the explicit final cue. This distinction needs to be visible on the private Coming Clean page as well as the host page. An ordinary player who hears their own name will otherwise reasonably start reading their selected box, as previously instructed.

## Reveal and tension

Votes are already locked, so a skipped suspect cannot contaminate accusations or the Escaped Justice outcome. It can nevertheless reveal the culprit early. If a named person visibly declines their turn, everyone can infer the murderer before the innocent readings finish. The proposal moves the confession last; it cannot guarantee that the identity is unknown until then.

That is an acceptable trade if the intended final movement is hope followed by the killer's account. When the culprit is not among the top three, the three innocent readings can sustain uncertainty until the final cue. When the culprit is called early, the remaining innocent invitations become dramatic irony in a room that may already suspect or know who is waiting. They remain plausible for speakers who have not heard a confession, but actors should not be required to simulate ignorance of an obvious skip. The audience may listen less closely to those innocents while waiting for confirmation.

The plan prevents the literal clash of Elle inviting Chip to help pack after his confession, Sue proposing to leave with murderer Mona after hers, and comparable invitations. It does not resolve unsupported assumed replies during investigation, Reed's internal contradiction, or any future-facing line that is implausible even before a confession. Those are separate editorial findings.

## Print and trial implications

Revise the host sequence and both private finale-branch instructions together; include the original-tally award rule. Also reconcile any general directions that currently say the named suspect immediately reads their selected Coming Clean box. Isolated host-only changes leave a predictable collision with printed player instructions. The speech prose can mostly remain intact; a compact common cue is preferable to eight local social alternatives.

For the experimental candidate, rehearse three cases: culprit first in the top three, culprit third, and culprit outside the top three. Check that nobody starts the guilty box early, nobody recruits a replacement fourth-ranked innocent, and the host awards Escaped Justice from the locked tally. Preserve the existing release until those concrete cues work without explanation. No source or canonical material was changed for this review.


## Addendum: all-three announcement cue

The refinement sufficiently removes the concrete predetermined-skip problem. Announcing the three names together, then inviting only their innocent branches in any order, creates no scheduled individual turn for the murderer to refuse. If the murderer is among them, that player knows to wait for the other two; otherwise the murderer waits for all three. Their standing then supplies the intended explicit transition into confession. The room might infer identity from behavior, but this cue no longer manufactures an early identification through a conspicuous skipped name call.

The exact cue is workable for ordinary guests and needs no additional social prose variants. Its small practical cost is self-organized turn-taking: two innocent guests may start together or both wait. The host can indicate a willing reader without restoring a fixed sequence or identifying the culprit. A short pause while people check who remains is acceptable; do not interpret it as a new accusation opportunity.

The must-fix is print consistency: the private murderer instruction must say to wait for this final phase even when named among the three, and the innocent instruction must limit participation to named guests. Remove conflicting instructions requiring immediate response to an individual name call. Keep the originally selected three fixed, no replacement fourth-ranked innocent, and retain the original tally for Escaped Justice. With those existing requirements reconciled, this refinement is a cleaner candidate than the sequential-call version and preserves the stated emotional purpose. It still requires a brief rehearsal; no new rating, source change or promotion follows from this judgment.
