# Fresh ensemble judge 03

Reviewed the complete candidate07-stage-order.md: all 22 roles, all 176 alternatives, and the exact guest-chooses-next and locked-ballot procedure. This is a literary/table-read judgment, not an author review, solve trial, or forensic fairness finding.

**Naturalness: 7.6/10. Arc variety: 7.3/10. Ready for a fair-play test: true.**

The ensemble is playable and substantially human. Its strongest writing gives people an immediate bodily or practical concern alongside their institutional dispute: Frank adjusting a chair, Al needing to call home, Cary offering to move something, Monet remembering payday, Dada discovering that her cruel joke is no longer funny. Those moments make a shared professional crisis feel inhabited. Short speeches, clear first-person stakes and recurring relationships provide useful anchors in a large cast.

The principal limitation is a common dramatic engine: Grant holds a professional embarrassment over someone; that person resists his terms, supplies a defensible account, and is eventually forced to name the compromised act beneath it. This is an effective suspect structure, but it is also the dominant emotional structure. Distinct jobs and costs keep it from becoming 22 copies. The distribution could still use more people whose response to pressure is exuberance, evasive comedy, stubborn practical action or bewilderment rather than polished, shame-aware eloquence.

## Actual performance and listening

The analytical groups need not be performed together. Most speeches establish their own incident clearly enough to survive an arbitrary guest chain, and the questions provide real cueing. Named addresses generally read as unilateral remarks, not invitations requiring invented dialogue. Directions to read one complete branch, answer once per act and keep later material private are usable. The host's name tracking is essential but understandable.

Some incidental addresses are harder to place: “Production” sounds like a department rather than a particular person. A speaker can deliver it without a reply, but actor instructions should explicitly make that acceptable. Likewise “bring the receiving copy,” “take the seating list,” “sit down” and “keep your recorder out” can sound like instructions for immediate stage business. A single global clarification that embedded requests need no action or response would prevent literal-minded players from interrupting the chain. This is a practical clarification, not a requirement for new dialogue.

Listening load is substantial. The bowl ownership dispute and the poison bottle's acquisition/contents paperwork are distinct stories sharing words such as label, caption, query, approval and file. Add public versus private announcement copies, invoice versus estimate, the glass versus carton, and a novice listener must retain several document distinctions while also hearing 22 personal disputes. That is a real table-read risk, rather than evidence that shared clue vocabulary is poor prose. The corpus does offer good anchors—payday, cloth, camera, school bus, recording, chair—but the most administrative passages sometimes spend these anchors too quickly.

The finale order is sound dramatically. Named innocents can voice unfinished plans before learning their friend's guilt; the murderer closes the event. I have not treated all 22 innocent finales or all 22 confessions as consecutive speeches. At most four are heard. Consequently the repeated denial/confession openings are useful ritual, and the aggregate volume of confessions is not itself a defect. The similarities that remain audible are the three-act rhetorical habits across the full cast: short factual corrections, direct address, a concise personal admission, then a weighted closing sentence.

## Revision priorities and conventions

**Material must-fixes before further circulation:** none that requires holding back a diagnostic fair-play test. The next test should include a genuinely arbitrary speaking order and record where listeners lose the object or document referent. A favorable literary score cannot establish that the evidence is solvable or fairly distributed.

**Recommended literary refinements:** clarify that embedded commands and named addresses require no improvised responses or physical business; identify how “Production” is to be addressed; lighten the densest paperwork speeches using concrete object names at key transitions. These are small changes, not a replacement performance structure.

A later polish should also vary the cadence of moral reckoning. Several people arrive at unusually complete formulations of what their wrongdoing means for trust, their profession or the people they meant to protect. In any one finale this can land well. Across the preceding acts, the consistent aptitude for self-diagnosis is a more audible authorial signature. Preserve some unfinished, defensive or practical endings, especially where those traits already belong to the role. Avoid stripping the best character-specific lines merely to reduce rhetorical symmetry.

Acceptable conventions include pun names, heightened donor villainy, many secrets, volunteered incriminating details, distinct culprit lies and explicit final explanations. The poison vocabulary must recur for an investigation to function. The confessions are alternatives, not a single 22-person chorus. With those conventions understood, this is ready to learn from players rather than requiring another speculative literary overhaul.
