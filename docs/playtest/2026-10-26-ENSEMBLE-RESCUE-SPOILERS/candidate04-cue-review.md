# Candidate 04 cue and branch compatibility audit

Scope: inspected `candidate04-stage-order.md`, `candidate04-source/performance.yaml` and `procedure.txt`; reviewed the complete-role context where needed. This is a technical directing assessment, not literary acceptance or a print-build check. No source changes.

## Feasibility and turn accounting

The proposed system is feasible for ordinary guests if the exceptional group instruction replaces, rather than accompanies, the ordinary answer-and-choose instruction. It adds one recognizable exception per act. Choosing any group member starts the group from its first printed speaker, not from the person named. Outside those blocks the existing free choice of next unheard guest remains intact; the manuscript's analytical group presentation is not a prescribed whole-act running order.

The schedules agree between the stage manuscript and performance metadata:

- Motive: Artie 1, Monet 1, Sue 1, Artie 2, Monet 2.
- Opportunity: Claire 1, Chip 1, Dr. Fact 1, Reed 1, Dr. Fact 2, Reed 2.
- Method: Dada 1, Barb 1, Brie 1, Barb 2, Brie 2, Cary 1.

These are 17 entries replacing 11 ordinary entries: exactly two extra entries per act, six overall. Artie, Monet, Dr. Fact, Reed, Barb and Brie return once; Sue, Claire, Chip, Dada and Cary remain single-entry participants inside a block. Everyone outside the marked group retains one answer per act. The total becomes 24 speaker entries per act, 72 across the investigation, still covering 22 people each act. Parts are silent navigation labels, not spoken clues.

## Must fix before a printable trial

1. **Assign the cue reader and print the trigger where it is needed.** “Ask its existing shared question, then read the short printed part schedule” leaves the reader of the schedule unspecified. The host or selecting guest must have that responsibility explicitly assigned. Every question entry targeting a marked member must visibly trigger the same entire block. Selecting Sue must visibly lead to Artie 1; selecting Reed to Claire 1; selecting Cary to Dada 1. A procedural paragraph elsewhere is insufficient when the named player instinctively starts their own box.
2. **Replace ordinary handover instructions within the marked boxes.** Only Monet 2, Reed 2 and Cary 1 choose the next unheard person. Earlier participants stop at their numbered part and wait. Each returning speaker needs a plainly separated second part plus the intervening speaker cue; never leave the old “read your answer and choose next” instruction attached to part 1. Host instructions, question pages, private boxes and round navigation must agree. Neither branch should require reading the other to find its stopping point.
3. **Give the host a per-act name checklist with a group indicator.** Track people, not speaker entries. During a block treat its whole membership as reserved/in progress; mark all three or four people heard at completion. Artie 1 does not complete Artie's answer. No one selects another guest until the block finishes. If someone selects a completed member, the host redirects to an unheard guest rather than replaying the exchange.
4. **Cover the last-block case.** “The final speaker chooses an unheard guest outside the completed group” is impossible if the marked group is the last unheard set. The host instructions need an explicit end-of-act condition when all 22 are covered. This applies equally to ordinary final readers. Also require a fresh checklist each act: membership and returning roles change between acts.
5. **Synchronize post-vote instructions.** Announce the original top three together; only those named innocents read now. The murderer waits for the final cue even if named. No replacement fourth-ranked innocent; Escaped Justice uses the locked original tally. Existing immediate-name-response instructions cannot survive alongside this rule.

These are implementation requirements for the candidate's stated mechanics, not evidence that the unpublished print files already satisfy them.

## Mid-act triggers and all-culprit compatibility

A marked group may occur first, midway or last without depending on earlier analytical groups. Its first invocation consumes every member's answer. It cannot legitimately be half-completed before invocation; that situation would indicate someone followed an obsolete single-turn instruction. Named addresses to people outside the block are not invitations to interrupt or to insert their later answer.

Across all 22 possible murderers, I found no schedule that changes with branch. The 11 block members each have matching required part counts in both alternatives; the other 11—Vincent, Elon, Penny, Frank, Elle, Al, Anne, Paige, Mona, Robin and Justin—do not alter a block when guilty. Nobody must know another player's private branch to cue the next part. Ordinary factual disagreement between a guilty account and an innocent account remains permissible; it is not a cue failure.

At the finale, murderer ranked first or third produces two named innocent readings followed by the confession; outside the top three produces three innocent readings followed by confession. Because the three names are announced together, there is no individual scheduled turn visibly skipped. A brief simultaneous-start hesitation among innocents is manageable. No ranking or branch changes the actual speech order inside investigation blocks. Frank's innocent finale drops the ambiguous Production address; Reed's now explicitly retains deliberate humiliation as his reason for removing the warning. Neither change adds a cue dependency.

## What the six returns buy

Motive has the clearest interaction: Artie's replacement line now immediately precedes Monet's “Your replacement, Artie.” Both Artie branches supply that premise, and both Monet branches can answer it. Opportunity lets Dr. Fact's instruction to Reed intervene between Reed's edit admission and explanation; it is a plausible pressure-and-response rhythm, though several surrounding addresses still refer to prior relationships rather than immediate remarks.

Method buys a genuine brief response in the innocent Barb/Brie pairing: Brie asks for staff release, Barb grants it, Brie returns to the bill. Guilty Brie still says to leave staff alone, so the permission remains intelligible. Guilty Barb instead insists on footage; Brie's bill line or service explanation does not answer that claim. That is playable deflection, but the block should not be sold as six seamlessly reciprocal lines in every world. Barb's “before calling my visit unobserved” still presupposes an accusation Brie has not explicitly made in her printed part.

The returns offer plausible local interaction without extra facts. Their cost is navigation and six more handovers, not an additional discussion round. A brief cue rehearsal should test non-first-member selection, the group coming last, and guilty Barb's exchange. Literary variety and emotional success remain for the isolated judges to assess.
