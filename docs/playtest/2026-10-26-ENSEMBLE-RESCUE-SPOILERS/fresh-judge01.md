# Independent complete-ensemble table-read judgment

Source: `candidate03-stage-order.md`, read in full: all 176 passages, covering both alternatives for all 22 roles in all three rounds and the finale. This is a literary/performance assessment, not a solve trial or an evidence-sufficiency certification.

**Naturalness: 7.4/10. Arc variety: 6.2/10. Ready for a separate fair-play playtest: yes.** The manuscript is playable and often moving, with distinct occupations, relationships, and immediate wants. Its principal weakness is a shared dramatic grammar: injured professional explains compromising paperwork, defends a questionable choice, and eventually discovers an articulate moral lesson. Strong local characterization makes that pattern less conspicuous, but does not eliminate it.

I assess a performance with 21 innocent tracks and one murderer track. Only three voted finales, plus a possible murderer fallback, are heard. The complete set of confessions is a branch library, not a sequence of 22 speeches. Their repeated mechanism and explicit admissions are therefore largely acceptable. My variety concern principally concerns the 66 round readings everyone actually hears.

## What works in performance

There are playable actions rather than biography alone. Artie seats the room; Frank cannot stop arranging furniture; Chip orders the load down; Brie protects the staff; Al wants to telephone home; Penny watches for a family call. These give ordinary guests something concrete to do with a speech.

The interpersonal links carry real pressure. Claire and Reed are more than interchangeable colleagues. Vincent's resentment of his early success differs from Dada's fear of artistic erasure. Sue and Mona can wound one another while still wanting to go home together. Cary's remembered coat on the peg makes precarious work immediate without explaining his whole life. Dr. Fact's failed dinner invitation is awkward in a recognizable, individual way.

The question groups provide useful changes of subject and a listening scaffold. Several short readings in the method round release the density accumulated earlier. Repeating “original contents,” “private room,” “glass,” and the precise material names generally helps listeners identify the same disputed thing. These are not, by themselves, writing defects.

## Actual ensemble repetition and clarity costs

**The empty-place errand is overused as a scene.** Even among innocent readings, Artie says, “His place was set; he wasn't there”; Monet, “He wasn't there, though his glass was waiting”; Elon tries the display at the “prepared place”; Elle rolls fabric “beside Grant's prepared place”; Cary says, “His glass was set out. He wasn't there.” Dada and Barb also encounter the prepared room, and Brie prepares the drink alone. There are good individual reasons, but the cumulative action is repeatedly an occupational errand conducted beside the same conspicuously unattended place. This is a dramatic-shape criticism, not an objection to naming the glass. Giving each role a different tiny gesture cannot entirely diversify a shared scene.

The murderer opportunity library intensifies that tendency on the page: Claire moves a damp glass off paper, Sue lifts it from an agreement, Vincent leaves a release beside it, Mona puts a letter under its edge. Only one of these substitutes into performance, so this is a secondary concern. Each needs to be credible individually, not different from 21 alternatives nobody hears.

**Moral self-correction has become a common register.** Innocent Claire begins with “I taught that student to question an authority” and admits failing the principle; Dr. Fact ends his opportunity speech, “I was the authority in that conversation”; Reed distinguishes “a public disgrace” from the harm he failed to imagine; Justin realizes “the interesting bit isn't always the bit people need.” Those are different mistakes, but the repeated movement from confession to neatly stated insight sounds like a shared author speaking through several professions. This is already present before voting, independent of the limited finales.

The finale library repeatedly turns to a future explanation or repaired trust: Elle must “call the person who paid for something else”; Cary must tell the next employer; Penny must “make that call”; Artie must tell staff. Three such endings together could feel arranged. This is a moderate risk, not 21 consecutive redemption speeches. Monet's refusal to apologize and Robin's unanswered petition retain useful resistance; more emotional incompleteness elsewhere would help.

**Ordinary listeners face document congestion.** Bowl ownership, bottle contents, announcement permissions, private service, and installation safety are distinguishable when read carefully. But the grouped accounts ask guests to retain masters, copies, queries, captions, attachments, photographs, invoices, and several forms of approval. “The bottle had its own file” is valuable orientation; the same plain orientation is less consistent elsewhere. The museum can sound like a room of very eloquent file custodians. This warrants a listening test, rather than automatic simplification of necessary evidence.

## Compatibility and disposition

I found no obvious literary branch collision that prevents testing one murderer alongside the other innocent tracks. Contradictions about what the glass, camera, bottle, or tour actually did are presented as disputed testimony and later admissions, not automatically continuity errors. The selected finales generally stand alone and do not require the other 19 innocent resolutions to be heard. Their personal addresses can work before or after a confession. A separate fair-play test must establish whether guests can adjudicate the documentary disputes; this manuscript-only judgment cannot establish that.

**Must fix before treating the writing as finished:** reduce the number of round speeches that conclude with a perfectly formulated ethical insight; diversify the actual innocent opportunity scenes enough that the room does not hear a procession of errands to an empty place. Preserve evidence while changing dramatic emphasis, emotional response, or speech action.

**Acceptable conventions:** explicit denials and confessions, repeated shared object names, compact factual answers under grouped questions, and a common poisoning mechanism across exclusive murderer alternatives. The three-plus-fallback finale rule materially limits repetition.

**Proceed to a separate fair-play playtest.** The remaining literary weaknesses are substantial revision targets, but do not justify postponing observation of how ordinary guests listen, remember, and compare these accounts.
