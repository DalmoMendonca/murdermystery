# Independent editorial comparison: A and B

**Preference: B, narrowly. Substantial improvement: false.** B makes a few exchanges easier to hear as people responding and gives the finale a better last beat. It does not substantially change whole-ensemble arc variety. The added scheduling is modest but real, and several split speeches remain monologues delivered in installments.

| Edition | Spoken naturalness / 10 | Whole-ensemble arc variety / 10 |
|---|---:|---:|
| A | 7.2 | 6.2 |
| B | 7.5 | 6.3 |

I read the complete 176 alternatives in each edition and both procedures. This is an editorial judgment, not a solve trial or an evidence-completeness certification. Each performed world has one murderer and 21 innocents. I have not treated the 22 murderer confessions as a single evening, or assumed that every guest gets a finale.

## What actually improves

In B's motive exchange, Artie's interrupted account returns with the personal humiliation of introducing a replacement. Monet's subsequent “Your replacement, Artie” then makes the payroll-versus-status conflict immediate. This is a real response in either Artie branch and either Monet branch. Sue supplies the institutional stakes between them, though her full answer still stands largely apart from their quarrel. The benefit is sharper placement of existing character material, not a new dramatic development.

The opportunity exchange makes the original-contents/inert discrepancy more audible by putting Dr. Art and Reed's accounts next to each other. Dr. Art's return to his request about the press copies or collection attachment gives Reed an identifiable person to push against. The document distinctions survive: Reed's press edit is not automatically the same act as withholding intake's full file. Neither branch requires Reed to confess murder or agree with an accusation. Claire and Chip, however, still give substantial uninterrupted reports before that two-person relay. Calling all four an exchange overstates the amount of reciprocal action.

The method exchange contains the strongest small response: innocent Brie asks to be questioned and her waiters released; innocent Barb explicitly lets them go. It works with murderer Brie too, whose demand is simply to leave her staff alone. The authority to release staff changes the present room for a moment. Murderer Barb's reply is weaker: “Check the storage before calling my visit unobserved” follows Brie's statement about her own preparation or the shared pitcher, not an accusation about Barb's visit. It remains a playable defensive assertion, but the script has not supplied the particular challenge it appears to answer. Dada and Cary mostly bookend this exchange with independent testimony.

Small B edits also help ordinary readers: Elle and Cary lead some answers with an immediate request; Frank no longer directly addresses “Production” in the same awkward way; Justin's note-writing sequence becomes clean spoken beats. Reed's innocent finale now explicitly retains his wish to humiliate Grant, avoiding A's softer “finish a page” account after his earlier denial that space caused the edit. These are useful local corrections.

## Cost and whole-cast limits

A has 66 uninterrupted pre-vote answers. B still has all 66 complete answers but divides six of them, producing 72 speaking turns. Its three marked groups involve 11 different guests; only six guests actually return for a second part. The host must interrupt the ordinary choice chain, track a fixed internal schedule, prevent premature choices, and mark the whole group heard at the end. This is manageable with clear cards and a short demonstration, but ordinary dinner guests must now recognize two turn-taking rules. The text supplies the rules; an unobserved read cannot establish their reliability in a busy room.

The new order is branch-safe in the broad editorial sense: it does not demand that guests read hidden alternatives, supply new historical facts, or accept the murderer branch as established truth. It also does not guarantee a fully responsive scene. Artie's family memory, Reed's complaint to Claire, and Dr. Art's appeal to Dada can still feel like held speeches resuming after someone else's speech. Fixed adjacency improves listening opportunities without necessarily changing a relationship.

Both editions already distinguish jobs, attachments, and speech rhythms well. Chip's practical imperatives, Monet's wages, Al's home responsibilities, Penny's desire to manage the explanation, and Dr. Art's embarrassed courtship are recognizably different. The repeated trajectory nevertheless dominates: Grant threatens professional or personal exposure; the guest reports a disputed action and paperwork; the method answer defends a version; a called finale accepts responsibility and names a future consequence. Many opportunity accounts necessarily return to a waiting glass and an absent Grant. Those clues need to remain legible, but the surrounding emotional beat is often the same defensive frustration.

B changes little of that progression for the 11 guests outside marked groups, and much inside them remains unchanged. Their strongest changes of position still depend on being among the few called to the finale. I therefore do not award the whole cast the growth visible in every alternative finale printed in the manuscript.

## Finale and remaining must-fixes

B's murderer-last rule is its clearest structural gain: the confession can end the sequence instead of being followed by innocent accounting. It keeps three speeches when the culprit is in the original top three and four otherwise; it does not add a fourth-ranked innocent. Announcing all names and having a named murderer wait can identify that person through the procedure before the final speech. Since ballots are already locked, that is a suspense tradeoff rather than pre-vote clue leakage. B retains the original tally for Escaped Justice. A's ranked sequence is simpler, but its explicit requirement to finish innocent speeches after confession risks a falling ending.

Before treating B as a substantial ensemble revision, the following need attention:

1. Give more pre-vote relationships an actual changed stance or present action. Do not rely on private finale alternatives to supply the ordinary guest's arc, and do not require everyone to admit their side lie early.
2. Revise the murderer-Barb return so it responds to what the other guest actually said, or let it remain an uninterrupted defense. Retain power/network/local-recording distinctions.
3. Make every retained split earn its return cue. Test the 72-turn schedule with ordinary readers; remove splits that mainly cause searching and pauses. Preserve original contents/inert, glass/pitcher, foil-backed paper/leaf, and public/private-location vocabulary.
4. Before release, synchronize the murderer-last rule across host and private instructions, as B itself requires. No finding here establishes that external synchronization has happened.

**Separate full fair-play playtest: justified, yes, before promotion.** The local writing gains and changed scheduling merit testing comprehension, timing, branch handling, accusation quality, and the locked-tally finale. Start with a cue read-through so mechanical confusion is not mistaken for deduction difficulty. This comparison does not establish fair-play balance, and the small editorial preference is not a reason to skip that independent test.
