## Unabridged reader feedback

### Cast 22: Hugh Bidder

The final accusation is well supported before voting: the seller's fulfillment record directly tests Hugh's story, while the glass, band, and private-service evidence supply a coherent method. I appreciate that neither a historical poison label nor an unpaid cheese dispute is treated as sufficient proof. The cast usually sounds like people with different jobs and immediate worries. Artie's chair counting, Al's need to call home, and Brie's concern for rides give the death consequences beyond the puzzle. Hugh's refusal to release files also fits his professional defensiveness. The assembly is nevertheless visible: nearly everyone has a compromised document, an admission, a personal vulnerability, and then a closing speech clarifying the corresponding clue. Twenty-two such arcs make the same pattern conspicuous. The documentary release also contains several exclusions concerning a podcast, photographer, and handler who are not selectable suspects; those details distract from the cast under review. The final lie is decisive enough for an accusation, although it does not independently place Hugh at the bottle when it was opened. I found the solution persuasive rather than mechanically proven beyond every alternative.

Finale: The confession follows the strongest pre-vote chain: Hugh knew the original-contents condition, visited the glass, faced the refund scandal, and falsely claimed a harmless substitution contradicted by seller records and residue. His taking poison during the preview is newly narrated action, but confirmation of the inferred method rather than a new decisive clue needed to solve the case. His wish to remain respectable gives the admission emotional weight, especially the surrender of clients and files. Chip and Barb's responses also fit their established failures without turning those failures into murder evidence. The closing speeches are a little polished for shock, but their practical consequences make the ending earned.

- moderate / pacing: The large stage-seven document bundle spends attention on nonselectable photographer, handler, and podcast details, alongside the essential seller, glass, and band evidence. Suggested fix: Remove or abbreviate nonselectable personnel's side records unless a selectable suspect actually relies on that account.
- minor / naturalness: Several closing answers restate document delivery and ownership-caption distinctions in parallel, making the interrogation feel organized around an evidence checklist. Suggested fix: Let one answer carry the delivery distinction and give the others shorter, less comprehensive reactions while preserving the documentary facts.

### Cast 30: Faye Stop

The case is solvable before voting. The camera-card chronology makes Faye's last denial testable without trusting her own exports, and the glass, service schedule, toxicology, and treatment band give the accusation a physical foundation. Hugh and Penny remain useful alternatives because they also lie, but their lies protect separate frauds. That distinction requires interpretation rather than a confession delivered early. Several voices are memorable: Chip's clipped practical responses, Mon­et's payroll fixation, Cary's wish for a concrete job, and Tess's mixed feelings toward Grant. However, nearly everyone develops the same rhythm of admitting a professional fault, adding a private vulnerability, then asking someone in the room to stop or do something. Across thirty people that pattern becomes conspicuous. The long corrective document release also feels designed to close an exhaustive checklist of possible misunderstandings. It is clear and fair, but slows the emotional pressure. Faye's motive and shaking hands work well; her final explanation is conspicuously vulnerable to a single preserved image, making the last leap easier than the broad earlier field suggests.

Finale: The confession follows the evidence already available: Faye's physical source access at the preview, her visit to the waiting glass, the independent card import, and her false composite explanation. Taking poison specifically during the preview is newly admitted action, but no new decisive evidentiary fact is needed to identify her. The source and transfer sequence feels earned. Her need to erase her own moment connects neatly to her photographic profession and existing threat of exposure, giving the solution emotional shape. The final crop metaphor is polished enough to sound rehearsed, and Hugh and Penny's tidy admissions reinforce the assembled feeling, but their separate lies resolve coherently without weakening the murder deduction.

- moderate / voice: The later rounds repeatedly combine an admission with an emotional aside and a directive to another person; chairs, labels, hands, music, and lights become similar closing gestures despite different occupations. Suggested fix: Keep a few of these gestures, but give several speakers different response structures: an interrupted answer, a terse factual denial, or a question that another speaker answers.
- minor / pacing: Stage 7 resolves many tangential possibilities in consecutive records, including uninstalled technology, the wooden model, and the excluded tour route; the exhaustive correction load briefly overtakes the poisoning investigation. Suggested fix: Move a few clear physical exclusions into earlier releases so the final evidence packet can foreground the glass, bottle, and independently preserved photograph.
