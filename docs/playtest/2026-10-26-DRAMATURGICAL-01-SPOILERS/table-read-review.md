# Editorial table-read: dramaturgical candidate

Read: 2026-10-26. Scope: all 22 characters, both innocent and murderer arcs, across the three hearing boxes and coming-clean box; compared against baseline-source/confirmed.yaml. This is a fully informed editorial reading, not a blind player, playtest result, inference test, or assessment of who would be accused. No manuscript edits made.

## Main finding

The candidate is more speakable than the baseline, especially at its endings, but it has not yet escaped the repeated-arc problem. Most changes redistribute the same sentences: a named relationship or rhetorical question moves to the front, the evidence explanation moves into the middle, and a personal request closes the box. That improves local performance while making a new cumulative template visible. The underlying movement still overwhelmingly runs: donor humiliates or compromises me; I explain my errand or professional task; I defend a precise account; I concede the account and articulate the harm to someone who trusted me.

The best revisions replace a finished moral verdict with behavior under pressure. Artie's fear that he will tell the staff he was protecting the museum, Elle's refusal to touch the fabric, Elon's request to leave the phone down, and Chip's request to stay away from the workbench all give a reader something playable. Keep those. The weakest surviving lines explain the thematic meaning of the character's conduct in the same lucid register across very different people. A small final pass should cut some of that commentary, rather than adding another round of rhetorical questions or partner addresses.

Neither version gives the reader a literal scheduled confrontation checklist in these boxes. The candidate nevertheless often sounds like one when the named partner becomes the launch button for each scheduled statement. Simply moving 'Claire,' 'Dada,' or 'Monet,' to the beginning does not produce an exchange. The questions and instructions need to work even when nobody answers and no prop is available.

## What improves, and what remains

Spoken naturalness improves through contractions, interruptions of the speaker's own thought, and practical wants. 'I know I keep saying that' is stronger than Monet's baseline summary of the wages as 'the small fact I kept hiding behind.' Dada's forgotten cruel reply is more immediate than the baseline's elegantly retrospective 'when the evening stopped being funny.' The innocent endings generally become acts: hand over the file, pack the weave, make the call, send the entry, keep the settlement in the tour.

There is also a cost. Front-loading a conversational hook sometimes delays the useful subject until the audience has already spent attention decoding the hook. 'What was that silence going to cost?' is less clear aloud than Penny's baseline sequence, which establishes the demand before naming its price. Justin's 'best rebuttal already' and Reed's 'would you have found wording' have the same tendency. These do not need new facts; they need the concrete object or dispute first.

The precise distinctions remain important and should remain precise: bottle versus painting/model/projection; glass versus pitcher/water/spare; power versus network; actual installation versus approved design; original contents versus inert/empty/neutralized. Do not vary those terms just to reduce repetition. The repetition worth reducing is the narrator's account of what every distinction means morally, and the nearly universal placement of an emotional relationship after the forensic explanation.

## Cumulative pattern and limits of a minimal pass

Read in sequence, the character specifics are real but the progression is still highly uniform. Most innocent arcs end in some mixture of an admitted secondary wrong, a promise to fix the public record, and a practical request. Most murderer arcs name the true evidence, describe the poison/glass sequence, explain the tactical falsehood, then locate an injured audience outside the room: staff, family, source, claimants, builders, children, future employers. The outside audience is effective in isolation. Repeating it makes the whole ensemble sound as though the same careful moral intelligence has edited their final words.

The candidate's cleanest gains are therefore the endings that refuse that completion: Artie catching himself, Elle unable to touch the fabric, Elon wanting the phone down, Monet involuntarily returning to wages, Dada wanting the looking to stop. Preserve unevenness. Some people can be lucid, some repetitive, some practical, some still defensive. Innocent Reed's continued credit demand and Robin's continued political demand are especially valuable because they do not resolve into universal humility.

The private errands are necessarily similar because the underlying opportunity facts are similar. There is a ceiling to how much line editing can conceal 22 accounts of approaching an absent man beside a prepared glass. Do not invent witnesses, alternate routes, different objects, or a new event to disguise that. The feasible improvement is varying what the speaker is trying to make this room do now: let me work, stop looking, hear the claim, give me the file, leave my source alone, accept the bill, let me phone. The candidate already contains these objectives; several are weakened by appending a moral explanation after they have landed.

## Questions, interruption, corroboration, and mandatory-improv risk

The briefing says to read the selected box as written and invent no events, witnesses, or evidence. Many new opening questions sound like requests for an immediate answer. Artie asks Claire for a verdict; Vincent asks Dada how to make new work; Elon asks which part is being asked about; Al asks when he can call home. These are not evidence violations by themselves. They are a performance ambiguity: a dutiful reader may pause indefinitely or a helpful partner may supply an unscripted answer. Prefer a desire or statement where the response is not printed. Any general facilitation clarification should say that these are rhetorical character speech and no answer/action is required; it should not authorize improvised testimony.

Likewise, 'Yes, Reed,' 'that's why I was quiet,' 'don't soften it,' and 'you've heard my answer' can work as resistance to imagined room pressure. Used across many boxes, they turn into fabricated mini-conversations. Retain some, but do not make every character's first sentence sound like a reply to an event the room never saw. Any timing or pause instructions belong outside the factual testimony.

Corroboration remains readable when a speaker names the actual object: work order, intake sheet, photograph, marked route, contact sheet, proof, original recording. 'Ask the docent' or 'ask my editor' should be understood as reference to the existing supplied corroboration, not a required improvised interview with an absent person. The final confession should preserve what establishes the contradiction; the minimal cuts proposed here target the moral gloss or duplicated framing, not those facts.

## Recommended smallest revision set

1. Resolve Reed's inherited 'finish the page' ambiguity without replacing the deliberate humiliation motive.
2. Make Artie's Claire question and Vincent's Dada question self-contained, and remove Elon's undefined follow-up invitation. Make the repeated call-home request a stated need if the event has no explicit procedure for answering it.
3. Shorten roughly the clearest overlapping moral endings: Claire, Barb, Al/Robin, Cary, Anne, and Justin. Do not impose the same new ending device on all of them. Preserve all admissions and evidence distinctions in the remaining wording.
4. Clarify that partner addresses, prop requests, staff-release statements, and 'Production' do not require an unscripted response or an actual change to event procedure. This is a performance clarification, not new evidence.
5. Keep the successful unresolved or physical endings. Another broad reordering pass risks smoothing those back into the shared template.

This review supports a small targeted polish. It does not establish blind readability, mystery balance, evidentiary correctness across every other source, or readiness to promote. It also does not justify calling the repeated-arc concern fully solved: the candidate has made the repetition easier to perform, and only some passages have acquired genuinely different dramatic movement.

## Priority finale destinations: five focused alternatives

These are scene-level editorial directions, not manuscript changes or permission to omit confession facts. Keep each character's evidence correction, actual extraction/poisoning, tactical lie, and motive. Change what the speaker is doing with the final portion of that admission. These five are more valuable than another global punctuation or opening-hook pass.

- **Barb: an investigation she cannot keep running.** Her existing footage claim has given everybody a task. At confession, the task ends. Place the department-trust consequence before the final return to the work order / absent recording; land on the fact that she signed away the recording, with no more storage to check. That reverses her current coping mechanism. It does not require a new duty, person, or response. Avoid appending another request for a job merely as a tic.
- **Al: care still demands attention despite the confession.** Retain the material comparison, non-unique-match qualification, poisoning, and publicity leverage. Then let his already established need to call home interrupt the conclusion: 'I need to call home. Someone's still waiting.' If retaining 'They didn't ask me to do this,' place it before that practical insistence, rather than use it as his final lesson. He has not become absolved or fully comprehending; he still needs the call.
- **Anne: anger survives the collapse of her claimed witness.** Keep the approved route, docent, actual solitary visit, poisoning, and exposed promise. End with the children/group not being beside the glass and her appropriation of them, rather than a lucid forecast of her removal from the tour. She can still insist the school promise mattered. This gives her a different destination from a uniformly repentant educator, while preserving that the promise was unauthorized and her account false.
- **Robin: the political demand survives an advocate's disqualification.** Retain that claimants neither requested nor authorized the act, the recording's private setting, preview poison and later glass use, the public-gallery lie, and harm to the claimants. End by restating the already established unresolved request: intact return or a hearing. The action is a compromised speaker still trying to keep the room on that demand. This is more complicated than ending 'I had no right,' without implying the claimants approved the murder or adding an outcome to their claim.
- **Cary: professional usefulness is no longer available as escape.** Keep the glass/carton proof, extraction and poisoning, spare-glass lie, and leverage through the false report/unsafe signature. Move the general loss comparison out of the ending. Finish with his existing recognition that nobody will ask him to move anything. Earlier boxes have repeatedly sought another lifting job; its disappearance makes the final silence do the work. Do not replace it with a new repair plan or employment event.

Across these alternatives, the contrast is in objectives: keep the investigation operating; meet a care obligation; defend the value of a program while losing the borrowed witness; preserve an unanswered claim; find useful work and fail. None requires an added prop, revelation, scheduled confrontation, partner response, or new evidence. The existing Artie/Elle/Monet/Dada destinations should remain distinct alongside them.
