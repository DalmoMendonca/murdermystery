## Causal plausibility

The central mechanism works: the original is stolen at 6:12; a distinctive donor glass is unwrapped at 6:32, left accessible until 6:44, filled from clean shared punch at 6:46, then used at 6:49. Cyanide inside that glass, a compatible original, and a contaminated common favor bottle support a transfer into the empty glass. The canceled unveiling does not cancel drinks, and lighting controls do not disable the cabinet alarm. Those distinctions are planted before they become necessary.

Several links remain inference. Nobody sees the thief or transfer. Compatible residues do not individually identify a unique source, recipient, or user. A favor could have been borrowed, stolen, refilled, or discarded by another guest. All guests had essentially interchangeable containers. Staff also have opportunity: Casey handles the glass immediately before consumption and supplies much of the chronology; Jordan retains the key and reports the theft. Their statements are not automatically truthful because they are printed. Damage and an alarm cannot exclude a keyholder staging a forced opening.

The hazardous exhibit's retention is difficult to buy operationally. The story acknowledges locked storage and specialist review, which helps, but a museum needs a believable reason to retain an active historical poison during a gala, and a clearer account of the breach. Barb's quiet search after an actual poison theft is alarming. This is a substantial professional failure, not a small paperwork lapse, and it remains a plausible murder-enabling act.

Grant's assistant appears to bring nearly every person's compromising message, document, or demand into play. Without a time span or explanation of preparation, she feels like an exposition device who can confront an entire crowded cast at once.

Hugh's and Tess's post-theft arrival receipts meaningfully constrain theft, if authenticated; they do not exclude later possession or an accomplice. The clean meal, sauce, punch, PROP glass, brush-water bottle, and FIXER bottle genuinely eliminate those tested routes. The glass identity distinguishes Tess's rental-glass breakage from the target.

Brie's claim to wipe only the stem and base is a claim, not independent verification. Anne's location when the toast was served misses the earlier unattended-glass window.

Al remains especially troublesome for a unique solution. He admits both an uncapped favor and holding the empty target glass. His final speech even explicitly acknowledges poison could go into an empty glass. A benign fragrance spill is fully compatible with subsequently adulterating that bottle or using a second bottle.

## Contradictions, gaps, and precise repairs

1. **Service surface:** 'desk,' 'reception desk,' and 'sideboard' may denote the same area or different ones. Define their relation once, with a simple plan or Casey's description. This affects Elon's bottle opportunity and Artie's claim that his papers were elsewhere.

2. **Glass-access window:** Give independently recorded times for Al's mock toast, Brie's wipe, Elon's paperwork, and the service manager's absence. Anne needs evidence for 6:32–6:44, not merely 6:49. Avoid inventing retrospective all-purpose alibis.

3. **Retained favors:** Have investigators separately collect and test the actual returned favors in Brie's packet, Reed's pouch, Penny's case, and Justin's sleeve. Current unopened stock testing cannot clear opened individual bottles. Al needs a tested recovered bottle plus some stronger constraint on subsequent glass access; an invoice stain alone is inadequate.

4. **Elon-specific clue:** Plant a small identifying feature before the final round and recover it on the contaminated bottle, or supply an independent witness to pouring plus verified timing. A recipient label would undermine the established common-label fact; use a privately added nick, repair, or other observable detail with an innocent earlier reason. Alternatively, establish a verifiable missing/returned-bottle contradiction instead of relying on his lack of an explanation.

5. **Theft chain:** Show a physical finding or independent corridor observation tying acquisition of the original to the eventual transfer opportunity. Do not make cabinet damage proof that the keyholder cannot be involved. Account for staff access.

6. **Staff corroboration:** Anchor Casey's chronology in photographs with times, tray handoff witnesses, and an actual observation of the glass after filling. Establish what Jordan was independently observed doing at 6:12. They need not become full players, but their exclusion needs evidence.

7. **Hazard response:** Explain why the original remained onsite and why Barb treated its disappearance as a collection loss rather than a toxic hazard. Give the bottle a sealed secondary container or cabinet that plausibly contains a hazardous object, then show how it was breached. Clarify elapsed investigation time before detailed laboratory results.

8. **Reduce mirrored confession prose:** Retain occupational diction but vary response behavior: interruptions, evasive fragments, angry denials followed by documentary correction, concrete sensory recollection, or an unpolished admission. Not every participant should supply a complete prosecution-and-defense summary.

9. **Assistant workload:** Identify which confrontations occurred before the gala and which tonight. Attribute selected documents to the board, editor, lender, family, or kitchen directly so the assistant does not carry nearly every revelation.

There is no fatal timeline contradiction in the supplied releases. The chief problem is evidential incompleteness masquerading as exoneration. The final vote is possible; a uniquely justified conclusion needs more independent discrimination among the bottle-and-glass handlers.
