## Causal plausibility

The source-to-miniature-to-empty-glass route is coherent. Casey unwraps the designated glass at 6:32, retrieves it at 6:44, fills it at 6:46 and serves it for 6:49. A specific-target poisoning fits clear shared punch and meal. Transfer into a small ordinary gift bottle offers concealment. A stolen bottle left behind the sideboard also fits disposal after transfer.

However, the theft and transfer are never attached to a person by observation, possession mark, authenticated route or trace. Access is explicitly broad. Casey remains a legitimate post-6:44 alternative because the material provides direct service access but no surveillance or continuous witness chain. An unspoken restriction to the thirty attendee roles would be an authorial convention, not story evidence.

The historic bottle is received as a hazardous object pending specialist review, yet enough active cyanide survives to cause immediate death. This is possible as a fictional premise, but the chemical form, amount and storage integrity are unstated. A short receiving report documenting crystals or analytically confirmed active contents would make it earned. Compatibility of residues alone is weaker than a unique source match. Fragrance odor, solvent and poison transfer practicality also deserve a concrete description rather than assuming all small liquids behave interchangeably.

Security conduct is the largest causal strain. At 6:12 a locked cabinet containing a known poison is forced and its contents missing. Barb suppresses the incident and permits normal reception service until a donor dies. Protecting privacy and institutional reputation can motivate this, but the text should show what she believed the hazard was, why she withheld a warning and who else knew. Currently it risks making an experienced art-crimes investigator implausibly careless chiefly to enable the plot.

Grant and his assistant manage an extraordinary number of simultaneous blackmail, invoice, restitution, art, trust and scandal confrontations. The assistant is a functional exposure machine and never a developed or investigated person. Give these demands an earlier written schedule, combine some into an audit dossier, or explicitly distribute them over weeks. Otherwise thirty conflicts seem to happen in one reception for the sake of thirty roles.

## Speech: naturalness and distinction

The writing has concrete occupational grievances and some good character-specific lines. Artie's embarrassed alternative job, Anya's committee photographs, Penny's grandfather portrait and Frank's approval shame are memorable. Professional diction varies enough to support roleplay.

Yet almost everyone speaks as an intelligent, self-aware defendant: admit a minor or serious misconduct, distinguish it from the implied murder, identify a retained document, then end on a neatly crafted moral observation. The second and third rounds repeat this rhythm at scale. Their shared articulate remorse is less natural than mixed reactions would be. Several people should answer briefly, interrupt, make uncertain sensory claims, misremember a sequence, resist naming a source, or fail to understand the forensic question. Preserve factual clarity in evidence cards rather than making every speaker their own forensic/legal analyst.

Al's “Yes, poison could go into an empty glass” is unusually candid and logically useful, but openly warns players that his reassurance does not clear him. Brie's “stem and base, not the bowl” is lawyerly and sounds prepared. Ellas's last speech is mechanically suspicious by comparison, because it gives no equally reassuring container outcome.

## Genuine exoneration versus scandal explanation

The laboratory genuinely excludes the tested meal, sauce, shared punch, PROP, brush-water and FIXER as detected cyanide vehicles. It does not exclude their associated people from using the targeted glass or gift miniature.

A canceled blackout or death illusion excludes the performance mechanism. It does not exclude its planners from poisoning independently. Forged board papers, wrong acquisition lot, unsigned beneficiary instruction and continuing estate liability narrow certain motives or interpretations. They do not prove lack of another motive or opportunity.

Hugh, Tess and Anya arriving after the reported theft could exclude personal cabinet theft if admission evidence truly proves first entry. Ella's ticket-collection clarification shows precisely why ticket timestamps alone do not necessarily do that. The later characters could also poison from a source obtained by another person; that possibility should be assessed rather than quietly forbidden.

Capped favors are not necessarily unused favors. Seals, possession continuity and specific contents testing matter. Justin admits opening and recapping his favor; Al admits an uncapped miniature, a spill and actual glass handling. Fragrance on papers does not establish that all bottle contents were always harmless. Brie could have returned a different favor or acted with another container. These are not extravagant alternatives under the broad public access established here.

## Contradictions and exact repairs

1. Ticket evidence inconsistency: Anya's issued ticket and Hugh/Tess admission receipts are offered as theft-time reassurance; Ella's later receipt is only ticket collection. State what each record records, where entry is controlled, and whether staff or earlier admission bypasses exist. Give independent first-arrival verification where intended.
2. Glass versus papers location: Artie says the glass was not where he left the papers, and that papers moved to the office. Earlier the sideboard/reception desk holds both service and correspondence. Add a simple plan or timed photograph showing desk and sideboard positions. Otherwise this assertion proves little about his access.
3. Source identification: compatible cyanide is described as matching the stolen bottle, but compatibility need not be unique. If uniqueness matters, name an independently tested unusual impurity or state modestly that it supports rather than proves the source.
4. Security inference: forced damage plus retained key establishes unauthorized opening more strongly than user identity. Stop using replica/no-preview clarifications as personal clearance; add actual theft route evidence.
5. All-scandal resolution: most final speeches answer their own red herring, not the poisoning window. If the goal is unique deduction, give independent location/handling evidence for Al and Brie, including the relevant minute. Al's fragrance spill needs a chemically tested recovered favor or continuous observation, not just a stain.
6. Ella-specific fairness: independently plant early arrival and envelope possession before the finale. Then supply an evidentiary reason her removed miniature is the recovered one. Her threatened public embarrassment remains a motive, but corrected acknowledgment may already diminish it; articulate what exposure still remained without introducing a secret motive after the solution.
7. Staff alternatives: show Casey handing over the glass under observation from retrieval through toast, or include Casey in the actual suspect field. Likewise explain assistant access; routine narrative service is not innocence.

The strongest version would keep the open-access container puzzle, establish a credible hazardous-theft response, and use one modest but decisive physical/timing clue. It would retain the enjoyable scandals while acknowledging that their explanation is not an alibi.
