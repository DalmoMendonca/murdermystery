## Overall judgment

The mechanism is substantially more persuasive than the identification. The documents support cyanide inside the individually identified donor glass, an accessible empty glass before filling, a stolen historical source, and a contaminated common favor bottle. They do not establish who stole, transferred, or administered the poison.

Justin Tyme becomes my final lead (8/10), with Al Baster a serious alternative (7/10). Justin admits removing his favor beside Grant's glass and leaves its later location unstated. This is relevant opportunity. But Al explicitly had an uncapped favor on the desk and held the empty glass. Neither has a demonstrated connection to the cabinet theft. Brie's and Casey's service access remain unresolved. A final answer naming Justin as proved would overstate these releases.

## Causal plausibility

The timeline is coherent: source photographed at 5:45; forced cabinet alarm at 6:12; camera disconnected in a separate room at 6:29; donor glass wrapped at 6:30 and unwrapped at 6:32; collection at 6:44, filling at 6:46, toast at 6:49. Independent lighting and security systems prevent an easy but false blackout explanation. The service continuation despite a mounting hold is sensible.

The glass timeline still leaves several routes. Empty-glass dosing in the twelve-minute reception interval is plausible, but later tray or filling contamination is not closed by independent observation. Casey's statement supplies almost the entire chain of custody. A wrapped-glass photograph does not alone prove the wrapper was tamper-evident.

The lab says residues are consistent, not exclusive. That supports a source inference without proving it. The historic bottle's continued potency and use as a transfer source deserve a short specialist finding; the narrative should not rely entirely on its theatrical name. No procedural dosing detail is necessary.

Barb's quiet search makes the absent warning causally understandable but leaves a museum security failure that helps the killer. The receptionist/service staff continuing with unattended donor glass while poison is missing is plausible only if they were not told what had disappeared.

## Early giveaways and fair planting

No character is an obvious early culprit. Motives spread broadly and the universal favors are properly planted before laboratory identification. The donor glass, historic original versus open-bottom replica, canceled prop effect, and independent camera power are all available early enough to reason about.

However, the packets repeatedly anticipate and disallow specific mistakes: no ownership substitution, no authorized guest handling, no console-security equivalence, bottles not necessarily service supplies, packing paper not identifying anyone. These read partly like retrospective repairs to a deduction puzzle, not ordinary museum paperwork. The clues can retain these distinctions with fewer explicit prohibitions.

The strongest final identity cue is distributional: almost everyone receives an explanatory ending, whereas Justin's ending adds proximity and withholds a favor-disposition explanation. An attentive player may select him because of unequal author treatment. That is a solvable pattern, but weaker fair play than an earlier, independently checkable contradiction.

## Speech

The introductions are clear and comic, but mostly use the same polished occupational joke. Later speeches have useful recurring relationships—Claire/Reed, Art/Reed, Hugh/Elon, Chip/Frank, Sue/Penny—but virtually all guests follow the same rhetorical form: confess a lesser offense, state Grant's coercion, distinguish that offense from murder, then finish with a dry aphorism.

Twenty-two people simultaneously offering carefully qualified admissions makes the murder inquiry feel scripted. Grant's assistant conveniently produces most incriminating records, becoming a universal exposition device without a name or independent account.

Brie, Barb and Frank have some practical occupational diction. Many others sound like lawyers framing concessions. Tess could be more evasive and emotional; Hugh could negotiate rather than plainly confess; Reed could overexplain in fragments; Al could interrupt or become defensive; Justin could narrate and correct himself. Preserve the facts but vary willingness, syntax, rhythm and response to other speakers. Let a few facts emerge through confrontation or another person's account rather than universal confession.

## Genuine exoneration versus scandal explanation

Genuine restrictions:
- The meal and common punch test clear, ruling out those tested communal poison routes.
- PROP, brush-water and FIXER tests clear, ruling out the recovered containers.
- The star mark distinguishes the donor glass from Tess's broken rental glass.
- Hugh's and Tess's late admission receipts reduce their ability to perform the 6:12 theft personally.
- Operator cancellation defeats the actual performance-collapse route.

These are not full person exonerations. A guest associated with a clear container could still use a favor. Late arrival leaves later dosing possible. A surviving alarm does not clear Barb. Anne being elsewhere at the toast does not cover earlier glass access.

Likewise, Artie's other job, unsigned legal instruments, unresolved board votes, estate liability, correspondence blocking restitution, and scandal explanations weaken particular benefits or reinterpret behavior. Murder can still arise from humiliation, revenge, exposure or failed pressure. Death need not erase every legal problem for a motive to exist.

Al's fragrance-stained invoice does not prove his bottle was never contaminated later. Brie's claim to wipe only the exterior is not independently supported merely by exterior fibers. Justin's failure to provide a limiting explanation is not itself forensic evidence.

## Contradictions and precise repairs

I found no unavoidable timeline contradiction, but several unresolved deductions should be repaired if the ending is meant to establish one killer:

1. Establish the dosing interval independently. Add a service photograph or two independent observations showing the tray/glass remained continuously observed from collection through handoff. Otherwise Casey remains a live suspect.
2. Close the theft route with timed corridor access or physical evidence linked to a specific action. The key remaining with Jordan plus forced lock proves unauthorized entry, not entrant identity.
3. Resolve the contaminated favor's ownership with a clue planted before the lab result. For example, an independently observed distinguishing damaged cap or label, later matched to the waste bottle, can discriminate without assigning all favors unique names after the fact.
4. Give Al's bottle and Brie's packet an actual recovered-object lab result if they are intended to eliminate those particular containers. State the limits: this still does not exclude a second favor.
5. Make Justin's statement contradict an earlier checkable observation, if he is intended as final suspect. Proximity plus an ominous last line currently cannot defeat Al's stronger admitted uncapped opportunity.
6. Specify whether the investigation assumes a single unaided offender, or avoid using late arrival as complete exoneration. Do not introduce an innocent-truth rule after players have read the speeches.
7. Give the assistant a name and one limited independent account, or distribute the conveniently produced incriminating records among believable sources.
8. Reduce repeated explanations of what evidence does not mean. Let the factual distinction appear once in ordinary records and let players infer it.

The story can support a lively group investigation now. It needs one earlier discriminating identity clue and a tighter independent glass custody record before its final lead becomes a defensible solution.
