# Round 16 — SPOILERS

Thirty revised Motive speeches replace the earlier ethical-summary cadence. Anne remains an education director; Penny keeps the family archive; Elle is a textile artist; Drew is a critic; Minnie owns show control. The private roles are reconciled with the sent public identities. Public copy and portraits remain unchanged.

The common miniature bottle is planted as Grant's absurd self-branded fragrance favor before the investigation. Multiple innocent people also disclose favors at reception. Thus a miniature or a generic label cannot identify the culprit. The original remains in secured storage until a forced theft; no hazardous preview returns. The source alarm and service record remain separate.

This round repeats the originally random round15 culprits with fresh readers. That controls the culprit/cast selections, not reader calibration. A second B reader was required after the first exposed release02 before verifying the failed release01 save. Both reviews are retained. The replacement uses a CLI that prevents a subsequent release until the prior score file has been separately validated and hashed.

The test deliberately removes a former identifying writing pattern. If attribution becomes weak or wrong, the story needs an independent discriminator, not restoration of the guilty-only bottle class. Role coherence, words on a page, and believable transfer opportunity are necessary but insufficient.

The Game Narrative, Genre Craft and Story Project methods remain in use. Their causal scene and fair-planting principles do not provide a substitute for an earned culprit inference. Provisional questions are contained in the packet source, with each role targeted once per act. Actual layout, evidence artwork and every-culprit review are deferred until a story candidate passes. No public deployment uses this candidate.
