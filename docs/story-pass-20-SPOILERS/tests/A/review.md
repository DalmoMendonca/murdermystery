# Blind trial A, version 20

My final accusation is Ella Gance, with moderate confidence (8/10 suspicion; roughly 65% confidence in choosing her over the field). I can defend investigating or accusing her, but cannot regard the individual identification as effectively conclusive.

The earliest fair accusation was stage 7. Stage 6 first made Ella my leader because she voluntarily identified herself as the person who moved the recovered bottle to its exact shelf and handled Grant's glass. At stage 7 the recovered bottle became the poison-containing original, making her replica account materially wrong. This is the first specific contradiction connecting one guest to both poison source and target, rather than motive alone. Stage 8 does not independently prove knowing identification: her claim that she did not inspect its bottom remains plausible.

The physical causal case is coherent. An intact original containing cyanide was locked in a cabinet and secondary case at 5:45. Both were forced at 6:12. Its neck was newly broken; a cyanide-bearing fragment fits the break. A contaminated miniature fragrance bottle suggests transfer or temporary transport from the original. Grant's empty glass waited at reception 6:32–6:44, then received clean shared punch at 6:46 and remained on the service tray until his toast. Poison could have been deposited in the empty glass during that unattended interval and dissolved when filled. Cyanide inside the glass, original, fragment and miniature supports this sequence; clean food, punch and alternate chemical/prop bottles weaken competing vehicles. The photographs establish moments, not continuous coverage.

Ella admits moving the original to the recovery shelf and touching the glass. The donor threatened to expose a promised painting she had already sold to cover an investment loss. That combines urgent motive, target access and physical contact with the source. It does not prove she forced storage or used the miniature. Neither favor paper nor contaminated miniature is attributed to her. Her handling could have occurred after another guest's poisoning.

Someone else could have placed the original in the replica's vicinity before her arrival. Exact recovery-location admission supports her movement account as readily as intentional concealment. A staff-returns shelf is also a surprisingly visible place to leave a murder source deliberately.

The presentation sometimes feels engineered. Nearly everyone visits the same sideboard, suppresses some document, states a grievance and supplies a surviving third-party copy. That repeated confession template makes access artificially universal and individual voices less helpful to inference. Ella's request for the card and repeated preference to discuss the bottle over the painting feel like possible writing tells; I did not treat them as proof. The initial board agreement overemphasizes Artie's motive, which later has an existing-job qualification. Reducing a murderer to the one guest whose plausible mistaken-object claim receives forensic contradiction risks a puzzle of statement wording more than a complete causal identification.

Accessibility and confusion: thirty pun names create considerable working-memory load. Several document rounds repeat rather than sharpen core facts. The original, replica, fragrance miniature, prop glass, rental stem, donor glass, brush-water and FIXER require an explicit object glossary for comfortable play. Stage 7 says a fragment was found in folded favor paper without previously introducing that recovery in my releases; ownership of that paper never becomes available. Open-bottom replica versus closed original is crucial, but no supplied actual image let me inspect whether the difference would be visible on a waist-high shelf. The folded favor paper/miniature linkage supports method while withholding individual attribution. Distinguishing a defensible accusation from a uniquely proven culprit remains essential here.
