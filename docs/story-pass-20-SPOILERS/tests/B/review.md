I accuse Justin Tyme if required to select one attending guest. Confidence is moderate, approximately 65 percent; final suspicion is 8/10, not effectively conclusive. He admits depositing the loose blue bottle on the exact shelf where the cyanide-bearing historical original was recovered and lifting Grant's eventual fatal glass. ## Physical causal case

The original arrives intact and containing historical poison, is locked in a cabinet and secondary case, and disappears after both are forced at 6:12. The recovered original has a fresh neck gap; a fragment in folded favor paper physically joins that gap. Cyanide occurs in original, fragment, miniature fragrance bottle and inside Grant's distinctive museum-star glass. Stock favors, shared punch, meal, sauce and alternative theatrical/studio vessels test clear. The plausible route is opening the original, decanting poison into a portable miniature, and contaminating the donor glass before clear punch is poured at 6:46. The sideboard window is 6:32–6:44, with photographs showing an apparently empty glass at its endpoints. Small poison quantities or residue could remain in an apparently empty glass; the photographs do not give continuous custody.

Justin's confessed movements connect him to original and glass. They do not establish that he broke the neck, owned the contaminated miniature, used its contents, or forced storage. The miniature has no recipient name and every guest had a favor. The folded favor paper has no released individual ownership link. The chain supports a poisoning method more strongly than it identifies its operator. Also, no chemical matching beyond the presence of cyanide demonstrates that the historical original necessarily supplied the glass.

## Plausible innocent interpretations

Justin could genuinely mistake the similarly colored original for the replica, stage his video with it, and move an already-contaminated glass while retrieving his lease. Somebody could have placed the original among reception items before him or exchanged it after his recording. His failure to inspect the bottom is ordinary for a person choosing a visual prop, especially with text rather than a supplied image. Showing the actual replica in Chip's kit does not establish when it entered that kit.

The original theft and poisoning need not have been done by the same person unless an additional premise says so; the one-murderer/no-accomplice premise does not itself rule out a separate theft. Grant's misleading empty-bottle assurance creates broad innocent confusion. The confession-like concealments by other guests often have documented professional explanations.

## Conduct and writing tells

The uniformly confessional structure is conspicuous: nearly every guest admits a professional wrong, a donor grievance and interference with reception papers. This increases noise and makes confession itself a poor guilt indicator. Everyone apparently prioritizes their original dispute over the death, which can feel theatrical rather than natural.

Justin's unusually exact description of relocating the blue bottle to the recovery shelf and lifting the glass reads as a deliberate clue. His final statement then repeats both acts and adds failure to inspect underneath, while several rival statements supply checkable harmless explanations. This narrative allocation points toward Justin more strongly than some physical facts do. I avoided treating it as proof.

The poison remaining usable in an 1891 historical bottle and a fresh break fragment concealed in favor paper require acceptance of the stated laboratory findings; without hazard or chemistry detail, I cannot independently assess those mechanics. I did not invent missing evidence about hands, miniature ownership or recording timestamps.

## Confusion and accessibility

Twenty-two similar pun names, many repeated paper disputes and several blue vessels create substantial tracking burden. The original/copy distinction is comprehensible once the closed versus open base is supplied. PROP labeling and the new museum-star glass help distinguish competing objects. Time-stamped service and alarm records are useful.

The attending roster is clear, but nonplaying staff names multiply rapidly. Stages 4, 6 and 8 are long and structurally repetitive. The most important distinction is access to the inside of the glass versus harmless material on its outside; the laboratory explicitly supports that distinction.

Only text equivalents were supplied. I could not inspect the bottle underside, fragment join, favor paper, photographs, packaging or their visual legibility. The absence of actual images limits any conclusion that depends on what Justin's recording visibly shows. The released text says his remaining recording concerns the boundary wall; it does not give a continuous filmed sequence proving poison transfer.
