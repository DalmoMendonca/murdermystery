Cyanide appears in the original, fragment, discarded fragrance miniature and Grant's glass. The miniature itself is intact and unopened favor stock is clean, supporting a separately contaminated small transfer vessel rather than poisoned factory favors. Shared punch and foods are clear. The glass was photographed empty at 6:32 and 6:44, then filled at 6:46; poisoning a dry interior during reception can remain compatible with those pictures. The pictures are moments, not continuous observation.

Elon admits putting the recovered original on the returns shelf and lifting the target glass. The releases do not directly attach the neck fragment or discarded miniature to him, establish his presence at the 6:12 theft, or show the actual dosing. Those are inferential steps.

## Plausible innocent readings

Elon could have mistaken the closed-base original for the similar blue replica without checking underneath. Another guest could have contaminated the glass before or after his touch. Since the shelf held general returns, substitution after his placement remains conceivable; Chip confirms he took his actual replica back. Elon's photograph of a pledge query corroborates his business activity only weakly, because it does not show the bottle. His conduct is suspicious but neither knowing possession nor dosing is independently observed.

Most misconduct concerns papers, money, credit or concealment, and has documentary support. The exterior plaster and fibers are contact evidence, not cyanide-route evidence. A broken rental stem is separate from the donor glass and cannot supply the matching original neck fragment.

This creates equal opportunity but makes separating genuine investigation from scheduled exoneration harder. Leaving suspected historical poison hazards incompletely checked for roughly half an hour is possible negligence, yet the repeated omissions are unusually convenient.

## Confusion and accessibility

The original/copy/miniature/PROP distinction is crucial and requires careful tracking. The open bottom versus closed base is understandable in text, and explicit clear laboratory results help. Cyanide transfer into a visually empty glass remains inferred rather than stated; players unfamiliar with residual contamination may wrongly eliminate the reception window.

Reception is used by nearly everyone, with different papers and objects. A simple shared timeline would help readers distinguish cabinet theft, copy handling, sideboard exposure and tray filling, without selecting a culprit for them. The final release mostly repeats alibis and explanations rather than adding independently witnessed contact evidence. The case supports Elon as the strongest answer, but the honest-mistake and later-tampering alternatives remain substantive.
