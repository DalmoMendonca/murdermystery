## What improved

The recovered source object and victim's glass are connected by conduct disclosed before Act III. The later original/copy comparison changes the meaning of an earlier statement rather than supplying the guest's first important contact. Readers understood the distinction between photographs of empty glasses and continuous surveillance. They could explain a clean shared punch being poured into an already contaminated individual glass.

## Why this still fails

None of the four tests meets the Act II breadth target. Two leave only one final alternative; A leaves six. The physical prototype improves the selected case but leaves too many other guests with mere proximity, unrelated document fraud, or weak theoretical access.

All four readers still noticed an engineered pattern: numerous reception paperwork errands followed by third-party documents that qualify the misconduct. Changing the professions' vocabulary did not remove that structural repetition. The lone recovered-bottle placement is conspicuously useful to the investigation.

The key miniature and fragment remain anonymous. Moving the original and the glass is a stronger connection than an unrelated lie, but another person could have poisoned the glass before the selected guest moved the objects. D explicitly distinguishes a best suspect from a proven attribution. Do not convert the one-murderer premise into a new guarantee that every theft or innocent mistake must have been committed by the murderer.

Separate the staff's safety failure, the catering payment dispute, the collector's interference, the performance dispute, and the historical-object research into scenes with their own purposes.
