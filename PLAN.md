## 1. What the study changes

The 2025 Masters of Mystery kit gives ordinary guests a dependable procedure: introductions and gossip, a secret culprit selection, evidence followed by grouped questions about motive, opportunity, and method, voting, then confessions. Each round gives people something concrete to ask and a prepared answer. The host manages transitions and participation rather than improvising an investigation. Shared questions produce different answers because the characters have different histories and habits.

We should adopt that procedure and the connection between evidence and questions. We should also improve on the reference kit's weak points: several innocent explanations do not firmly exclude the crime before voting, some guilty responses are conspicuously evasive, and much of the final crime sequence repeats across characters. Its procedure is a useful model; its text is not proof of perfect balance.

Our current problem is deeper than presentation. The case revolves around a contamination interval, clock correction, and an archive of thirty individual corroborations. Twenty-nine innocent branches match their records; the guilty branch lacks the relevant match. That invites guests to spot the missing certification rather than investigate people. Perfect scores in the earlier agent tests are evidence that this structure needs replacing. They do not establish party readiness.

Other weaknesses to address together:

- Motive, opportunity, and method questions reuse the same character buckets instead of forming groups around each round's new evidence.
- Several official releases repeat custody facts. The decisive work falls to a large technical archive.
- Discovery dependencies treat too much of the hunt as essential. A missed envelope should not strand the investigation.
- Many speeches share an administrative register. The personality and secrets do too little work in the investigation.
- Existing checks establish that authored clearance metadata matches authored answers. They cannot independently establish that a guest can derive the answer from the evidence.
- The current playtest preparation uses a limited roster and extracted text. It must include every page of multi-page exhibits and the visible content of image clues.

## 3. Build the crime before writing the speeches

### 3.1 Fixed crime backbone

Retain the private silver coupe and poisoning premise provisionally. The murder must have a short, physically credible causal sequence that works for every possible selected culprit:

1. Grant's personal toast vessel is prepared.
2. Someone deliberately contaminates it before an established custody boundary.
3. The drink is poured and Grant uses it.
4. Medical and material evidence establish the delivery mechanism.

The present clean-at-6:40, covered-at-6:44, poured-at-6:46, toast-at-6:49 sequence is a starting point for checking that mechanism. Prefer observable events and custody changes over requiring every player to correct two clocks. Exact times can remain in the host's continuity ledger when useful.

Before locking the crime, verify the vessel handling, inspection, seal, toxin access, and subsequent pour. A lab result cannot establish more than it realistically measures. A photograph cannot prove continuous absence. A witness cannot know what they did not see. If the coupe mechanism cannot support thirty coherent routes, revise the mechanism here, before writing thirty packets.

All potential culprits need plausible access in the fictional setup. Access cannot depend on an occupation that only a few roles possess. A conservator can have professional access while a donor, caterer, or visitor has a different documented route. Those routes must exist before the random animal selection; the draw selects which fictional account is true.

### 3.2 Crime constraints and evidence ledger

Create an author-only ledger with:

- Each necessary condition of committing this murder: relevant access, physical action, custody boundary, and means.
- The fixed public facts supporting each condition, with exhibit and release stage.
- Each character's branch-dependent claims, what corroborates or challenges them, and what a guest can reasonably infer.
- The separate non-murder secrets and the evidence that makes those secrets suspicious.
- The final resolution of each apparent contradiction.

For each of thirty culprit selections, check the whole world together: one guilty account, twenty-nine innocent accounts, and the same fixed evidence. No accomplice, secret outside killer, miraculous exception, or newly invented finale evidence may rescue a broken route.

Keep three questions separate in the ledger: Is a statement true in this branch? Can a player verify it? Does it exclude a necessary part of the murder? A suspicious act can be true without proving murder; a sympathetic motive can be true without proving innocence.

### 3.3 Replace the uniform clearance system

Remove the thirty-record archive as the universal clearing engine. Remove the requirement that all innocents supply the same continuous interval in different costumes.

Build varied exclusions from the crime constraints. Candidate forms include a witnessed incompatible action, the custody of a relevant object, the identity of a material, or a document's actual revision sequence. Each must genuinely contradict a necessary crime condition. An unrelated timestamp, mere denial, or lack of motive is insufficient.

Distribute the ingredients across acts. Where practical, an exclusion should require a character statement plus a fixed exhibit or another independently released fact. The innocent should not announce, "This proves I could not have done it." Players perform that inference after Method.

The guilty version can truthfully resolve another scandal or admit a harmless activity. It cannot resolve the actual murder constraint. Avoid making guilty responses consistently longer, vaguer, defensive, or packed with missing-record excuses. Both branches should sound like the same person under pressure.

These exclusions remain branch-dependent. A fixed exhibit must not permanently clear a named role, since that role can be selected as murderer in another run.

## 5. Give each act a distinct job

### Act I: Motive

Release a short, authentic piece of museum business: the donor agreement, board action, or associated correspondence. It should explain what Grant threatened to take, expose, cancel, or claim. The death announcement establishes the broad crime without revealing the complete mechanism.

Group questions around grievances revealed by that material. Multiple named characters can answer the same question with different stakes: ownership, career, family reputation, funding, credit, or institutional power. Let several innocents sound worse than the murderer here. Suspicion should be spread across the room.

### Act II: Opportunity

Release the relevant service/custody account, a useful image, or a short witness statement. It changes what players understand about access and the sequence of events.

Regroup the targets around those facts. Questions can concern an errand, an object handled, a route through the room, or an interruption. Avoid thirty versions of "Where were you between these two times?" An answer can explain one suspicious detail while exposing another. Some exclusion ingredients appear here without being decisive yet.

### Act III: Method

Release concise findings about delivery, material, and the physical action required. These findings should make earlier details useful in retrospect. They must not name the culprit or instruct players to compare an author-defined checklist.

Questions revisit actions and materials the guests have already heard about. Every innocent route now has its complete exclusion available, including any corroboration. The murderer still fits the physical constraints. Preserve several credible scandals so the final conversation has drama even when a careful guest can solve the murder.

### Question-page rules

- One question page per act; answers on the following page.
- Each shared question names a manageable group of characters. Aim for roughly two to five targets where the story supports it, rather than forcing identical group sizes.
- Group membership changes with the evidence. Every present character has an applicable question and exactly one required answer per act.
- Questions ask for facts the prepared answer actually addresses. Guests need no invented private history or specialist knowledge.
- Keep complete instructions in each packet. No external catalog or ID lookup.

## 11. A playtest that can actually reject the design

Generate fixtures from the final player-visible materials, including every exhibit page and the information in images. Each participant sees their own packet, the announced animal, and public information only as it is released. Other characters' unused branches, endings, author ledgers, and clearance labels remain unavailable. Do not omit the second page of an exhibit because an export reads only its first text field.

Vary the culprit, attendance, and participant behavior. Include literal readers, shy guests, enthusiastic improvisers, distracted listeners, and a playing host. Test missed hunt locations, a repeated question, an absent target, a mistaken page turn, and delayed arrival. Record how the host restores the procedure without revealing the culprit.

Capture private guesses and confidence after each act, then final votes and the evidence each player cites. Record accidental disclosures, unclear questions, unexplained facts, reading duration, and whether the finale contradicts anything. Keep those guesses private during play so they do not manufacture consensus.

Use a provisional final-solve target of roughly 15â€“45% of innocent players across several sessions, reflecting the user's request for a real mystery with some successful detectives. Treat the percentage as a design diagnostic. All-correct results across repeated sessions fail the difficulty gate; nobody solving with no defensible route fails the fairness gate. Small samples and agent consensus cannot establish human difficulty.

Separate four verdicts: logical coherence, player comprehension, difficulty, and physical usability. A perfect logical checker does not pass the other three. Agent sessions can find problems, but human memory, social dynamics, and dinner pacing still require a human rehearsal. If none is available, state that limitation honestly at release.
